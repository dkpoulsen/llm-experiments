# Interim report: topology-cost-at-1m-tokens

**Locus question:** At 1M-token global batches, is DDP really the only viable topology — or does PP beat it once activation hand-offs are overlapped, and is the repo's "overhead depends only on batch size, not model size" invariance false for MoE?
**Flavor:** dialectical

**Tier key used throughout:** `[M]` measured-on-this-box (all of it user-reported — no third-party training measurement exists anywhere in the corpus), `[C]` community report (someone measured it on a comparable box), `[P]` published primary (paper/docs), `[A]` arithmetic estimate from stated assumptions. Every number below carries one.

---

## What the corpus already said

The repo's position is compact and internally consistent: `research.md` §2 ranks DDP < FSDP < PP < TP < EP by bytes on the wire, computes an additive overhead table at 1M tokens (DDP 36%, FSDP 72%, PP ~2.9×, EP ~72×), and concludes "EP and TP are out for training. The choice is DDP or FSDP, and DDP is ~2× cheaper on the link. So the design goal is *make the trainable set fit on one card*" (`research.md` §2). Headline #3 generalises this to an invariance: "**DDP's overhead depends only on batch size, not model size** — compute and gradient payload both scale with params." `goal-and-requirements.md` repeats all four claims ("Parallelism: DDP or FSDP only. EP and TP cost ~70× the compute time on this fabric… FSDP costs ~2× DDP. DDP overhead is set by batch size alone").

The corpus already contradicts parts of that, in three places the loci-analyst flagged:

- **PP's traffic shape.** [[physical-modsmd]] (Consensus-Protocol, tier-1 docs) states pipeline-parallel inter-card traffic is negligible: "a 5120-hidden-dimension model moves 10,240 bytes per token per hop, so roughly 25,000 tokens/s would be needed to saturate a single PCIe 1.0 lane" `[P]`. [[lessonsmd]] (mirror of club-170hx LESSONS.md) says PP "moves the payload once per stage boundary (3 hand-offs for 4 stages) and overlaps transfer with compute: about 28x less data on the wire than TP" `[C]`, and measures PP beating TP by 6.6× on prefill (5,321 vs 801 tok/s at 77k) with TTFT 14.6 s vs 87.3 s at 100k `[C]`. [[club-170hxdocstopology-and-parallelismmd-at-main-pixelmlclub-170hx-github-2]] adds the mechanism: TP is "latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound — the round trip through host memory costs the same whether the tensor is large or small" `[C]`, and [[club-170hxdocsbenchmarksmd-at-main-pixelmlclub-170hx-github]] has the cleanest link-tolerance datapoint in the corpus: when one card retrained from x8 to x1, "TP4 fell from 70.5 to 14.4 tok/s under an unchanged protocol while PP4 held 60.8 tok/s on the same box — about 4.2x more link-tolerant" `[C]`.
- **PP is viable on no-P2P PCIe.** [[efficient-training-on-multiple-consumer-gpus-with]] (RoundPipe, ATC 2026) "achieves highly competitive performance without utilizing any GPU peer-to-peer communication (NVLink), relying entirely on PCIe host-to-device transfers" — 1.48–2.16× over ZeRO-2/FSDP/ZeRO-Infinity/Megatron-PP/Megatron-TP/Mobius on 8×4090, bubble below 4.5% with asynchronous optimizer updates `[C]`. [[mobius-fine-tuning-large-scale-models-on]] (ASPLOS'23) profiles DeepSpeed on 4×3090-Ti and finds "communication is over 70% of total training time, with traffic of 7.3x the model size per training step" `[C]`. [[zeropp-unleashing-exceptional-parallelism-efficiency]] shows ZeRO-3 "communication volume and iteration time beat TP at every global batch size, with the gap widening as batch grows" `[C]`.
- **The FSDP multiplier.** Both primaries give 1.5×, not 2×: [[pytorch-fsdp-experiences-on-scaling-fully-sharded-data-parallel-pvldb-16]] — "full sharding has 1.5x communication overhead and volume over DDP if using bandwidth optimal ring algorithm" `[P]`; [[zero-memory-optimizations-toward-training-trillion-parameter-models-sc20]] — "The total volume is therefore 3Ψ which is 1.5x compared to the baseline" `[P]`. The corpus claims file for the FSDP note already records the stance target as "supports: the repo's claim that FSDP moves ~1.5x the bytes of DDP, and **refutes research.md's ~2x multiplier**."

What the corpus does **not** have is any on-box training measurement. [[performancemd]] says so explicitly: "it contains NO training, fine-tuning, pretraining, all-reduce, NCCL, DDP or gradient-communication measurement at all… the only training datapoint in the corpus is the repo's own 494M-model, ~140k tok/s, 91-100 TFLOPS/GPU run, which is tier-1 self-measured but unverified by any third party" `[M]`. The only NCCL number in the whole corpus is a **2-GPU** all-reduce at **Gen2 x16** behind PLX switches — "3.69 GB/s (same switch) and 3.85 GB/s (cross switch) bus bandwidth at 256 MiB" `[C]` — which brackets but does not confirm the repo's 5-GPU Gen2 x4 "≈0.75 GiB/s" `[M]`. Treat 0.75 GiB/s as user-reported.

## What the new sources say

### 1. Megatron-LM — the 1F1B bubble formula and PP's communication volume [[efficient-large-scale-language-model-training-on-gpu-clusters]]

This is the canonical source for both halves of the PP argument. The bubble fraction is stated twice, identically for GPipe and 1F1B: "The total amount of time spent in the pipeline bubble is then 𝑡𝑝𝑏 = (𝑝−1)·(𝑡𝑓+𝑡𝑏). The ideal processing time for the batch is 𝑡𝑖𝑑 = 𝑚·(𝑡𝑓+𝑡𝑏). Therefore, the fraction of ideal computation time spent in the pipeline bubble is: **Bubble time fraction = (𝑝−1)/𝑚**" `[P]`. Critically, 1F1B pays the same bubble as GPipe but with far less memory: "The time spent in the bubble is the same for this new schedule, but the number of outstanding forward passes is at most the number of pipeline stages… this schedule requires activations to be stashed for 𝑝 or fewer microbatches (compared to 𝑚 microbatches for the GPipe schedule)" `[P]`. Interleaved stages divide the bubble by the number of virtual stages 𝑣 — "the new schedule reduces the bubble time by 𝑣" — but "this schedule requires extra communication… the amount of communication also increases by 𝑣" `[P]`.

The communication volume is stated as a formula: with the scatter/gather optimisation, "the total amount of communication that needs to be performed between every pair of consecutive stages is reduced to 𝑏𝑠ℎ/𝑡, where 𝑡 is the tensor-model-parallel size, 𝑠 is the sequence length, and ℎ is the hidden size" `[P]`. With no TP (`t=1`) that is exactly `b·s·h` bytes per stage boundary per micro-batch — the same 2 bytes × hidden per token per hop that [[physical-modsmd]] reports. And the paper's qualitative verdict on PP is the one the repo contradicts: "Pipeline model parallelism, on the other hand, uses much cheaper point-to-point communication that can be performed across nodes without bottlenecking the entire computation. However, with pipeline parallelism, significant time can be spent in the pipeline bubble: the total number of pipeline stages should thus be limited so that the number of microbatches in the pipeline is a reasonable multiple of the number of pipeline stages" `[P]`. The reference 175B/530B runs use global batch 1536 sequences × 2048 tokens = **3.1M tokens per step** with PP=8 — i.e. the canonical large-batch training configuration on the planet is PP, not DDP `[P]`.

### 2. Zero Bubble PP — the 12.5% bubble is an upper bound, not a floor [[zero-bubble-pipeline-parallelism]]

"Pipeline parallelism is one of the key components for large-scale distributed training, yet its efficiency suffers from pipeline bubbles which were deemed inevitable. In this work, we introduce a scheduling strategy that, to our knowledge, is the first to successfully achieve zero pipeline bubbles under synchronous training semantics" `[P]`. The mechanism is splitting backward into B (input-gradient) and W (weight-gradient) passes. ZB-H1 "ensures that the maximum peak memory usage over all workers doesn't exceed that of 1F1B… the bubble size is reduced to a third of 1F1B's size" `[P]`; ZB-H2 "eliminating all the bubbles in the pipeline" `[P]`. Measured: "our method outperforms the 1F1B schedule up to 23% in throughput under a similar memory limit. This number can be further pushed to 31% when the memory constraint is relaxed" `[P]`. For this box that means the 5-stage 1F1B bubble of (5−1)/32 = 12.5% is a ceiling; ZB-H1 gives ~4.2%, ZB-H2 ~0%.

### 3. GPipe — PP is explicitly the topology for slow interconnects [[gpipe-easy-scaling-with-micro-batch-pipeline]]

The original statement of both facts. Bubble: "This bubble time is O((K−1)/(M+K−1)) amortized over the number of micro-steps M. In our experiments, we found the bubble overhead to be negligible when M ≥ 4 × K" `[P]`. Communication: "GPipe also introduces low communication overhead, given that we only need to pass activation tensors at the partition boundaries between accelerators. Therefore, we can achieve efficient scaling performance even on accelerators without high-speed interconnects" `[P]`. The empirical section repeats it: "The communication bandwidth between devices is no longer a bottleneck for model parallelism since GPipe only transfers activation tensors at the boundaries of partitions" `[P]`. GPipe also names the failure mode the repo ignores: "Figure 2c assumes partitions are evenly balanced. However, memory requirements and computation flops at different layers are often quite imbalanced. In such scenarios, imperfect partitioning algorithms might lead to load imbalance" `[P]`.

### 4. DeepSpeed-MoE — the total/active ratio in production [[deepspeed-moe-advancing-mixture-of-experts-inference]]

The cleanest published instance of the exact asymmetry the repo's headline #3 denies. Their 1.3B+MoE-128 model "will only activate 1.3B parameter per token, and the amount of training computation per token will be similar to a 1.3B dense model" — but the model "has roughly 8x the parameters of the corresponding dense model (e.g., 6.7B dense model has 6.7 billion parameters and 1.3B+MoE-128 has 52 billion parameters)" `[P]`. That is a **40× total/active ratio** (52B/1.3B). Training throughput on 128 A100s: 372 samples/s for the 52B-total MoE against 70 samples/s for the 6.7B dense model — 5× faster at 8× the parameters `[P]`, because compute tracks active params. They train with "a combination of data parallel and expert parallel training" `[P]`, and EP "requires all-to-all communication between all expert parallel devices… it is difficult to scale expert parallelism to many devices as the latency increases linearly with the increase in devices" `[P]`.

### 5. Seq1F1B — 32k sequences break batch-level PP [[seq1f1b-efficient-sequence-level-pipeline-parallelism-for]]

The direct counterweight to the PP-is-free story at this box's context length: "As training sequences extend to 32K or even 128K tokens, current PP methods face severe bottlenecks, including substantial pipeline bubbles and high memory footprint" `[P]`. The mechanism: "For GPipe and existing 1F1B methods, whose minimal schedulable unit is micro-batch, inevitably face the memory overflow caused by just a single micro-batch, as training sequences extend to extremely long lengths" `[P]`. Their fix schedules at sequence level and gets "1.14× training throughput with half memory footprint compared to competitive baseline", training a 30B model on 64K sequences across 64 A100s without recomputation `[P]`. This matters here because the repo's micro-batch is one 32k sequence — large enough that the micro-batch, not the global batch, is the unit that must fit.

### 6. Piper — MoE training is PP × EP, and PP is the scheduling lever [[piper-efficient-large-scale-moe-training-via]]

"Piper closes this gap by composing pipeline parallelism with expert parallelism. It organizes P GPUs into a PP × EP [grid]" `[P]`. Their diagnosis of MoE training bottlenecks is exactly this locus's: "all-to-all latency at scale from expert parallelism, insufficient compute-communication overlap, low GPU utilization from imbalanced skinny GEMMs, and the absence of platform-aware hybrid parallelization strategies" `[P]`. Result: "Piper achieves 2–3.5× higher MFU than state-of-the-art frameworks such as X-MoE" `[P]`. The relevant transferable claim is that PP is the scheduling layer that makes MoE compute-communication overlap possible; EP is the layer that costs the all-to-all.

### 7. PipeOffload — PP's real constraint is activation memory, and offload is nearly free [[arxiv250301328v2-cslg-30-jun-2025]]

"While PP shards layers across devices to reduce parameter memory requirements, its scalability remains constrained by the activation memory. Increasing the number of PP stages reduces layers per device but necessitates more in-flight microbatches to minimize pipeline bubbles. This trade-off leaves overall activation memory demands unchanged" `[P]`. Their offload criterion is directly usable here: with `To` the D2H+H2D round trip for a layer's activations and `Tc` its forward+backward compute, `k = To/Tc`, and "Full activation memory offload is possible if k ≤ 1, as the offload operations can be fully overlapped by compute" `[P]`. "In the majority of standard configurations, at least half, and potentially all, of the activations can be offloaded with negligible overhead" `[P]`. On a 1.68 GB/s link this is the escape hatch if PP's in-flight activation footprint ever binds.

### 8. PyTorch DDP — DDP's all-reduce is bucketed and overlapped, which the repo's additive model ignores [[pytorch-distributed-experiences-on-accelerating]]

The repo's DDP row treats the all-reduce as a serial addend. PyTorch's own DDP paper says otherwise: DDP's core techniques are "bucketing gradients, overlapping computation with communication, and skipping gradient synchronization" `[P]`, and "bucket sizes considerably affect communication efficiency, which could lead to more than 2X speedup if configured properly" `[P]`. The paper's headline finding is also the one that matters for the MoE case: "communication is the dominant training latency contributor, and its impact increases with model sizes" `[P]`. That is the general form of the invariance failure — DDP's comm cost grows with the parameter count while compute grows with active FLOPs.

### 9. PyTorch pipelining — PP is a first-class API, with caveats [[pipeline-parallelism-pytorch-214-documentation]]

`torch.distributed.pipelining` "is currently in alpha state and under development. API changes may be possible. It was migrated from the PiPPy project" `[P]`. It provides "Rich support for pipeline schedules, including GPipe, 1F1B, Interleaved 1F1B and Looped BFS, and providing the infrastructure for writing customized schedules" and "Composability with other PyTorch parallel techniques such as data parallel (DDP, FSDP) or tensor parallel" `[P]`. The two constraints that matter for a custom MoE+Engram+CSA2 model: "A `PipelineStage` needs to know the input and output shapes for the stage model… The shapes must be static, e.g. at runtime the shapes can not change from step to step" `[P]`, and manual splitting "may require changes to the original model code" `[P]`. So PP is implementable without writing a scheduler, but not without touching the model.

## Evidence synthesis

### The corrected cost table

All rows use the repo's own geometry — 7B total / 1B active MoE, 24 layers, hidden 2048, top-k 2, 1M-token global batch, 5 GPUs, 810 TFLOPS bf16 aggregate — and one wire bandwidth. The repo's `0.75 GiB/s` all-reduce is an *algorithmic* rate (gradient-buffer bytes per second); a ring all-reduce moves 2Ψb on the wire for a Ψb buffer, so the corresponding wire rate is **B_wire = 1.5 GB/s**, corroborated by the measured 1.68 GB/s Gen2 x4 host copy `[C, physical-mods]`. PP's point-to-point hand-off uses `B_p2p = 1.68 GB/s` per direction. Compute at peak is 7.41 s; at the repo's realistic 405 TFLOPS it is 14.8 s.

| Topology | Per-GPU wire volume per step | Time @ measured BW | Overhead vs 7.41 s compute | Repo's figure |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DDP, dense 1B trainable | 2Ψb = 4 GB | 2.67 s | **36%** (18% at 405 TFLOPS) | 36% ✓ |
| DDP, MoE **7B trainable** / 1B active | 2Ψb = 28 GB | 18.7 s | **252%** (126% at 405 TFLOPS) | 36% ✗ |
| FSDP, dense 7B trainable | 3Ψb = 42 GB | 28.0 s vs 51.9 s compute | **54%** | 72% ✗ |
| FSDP, MoE 7B trainable / 1B active | 3Ψb = 42 GB | 28.0 s | **378%** (189% at 405 TFLOPS) | — |
| PP, 5 stages, 7B/1B | 16.4 GB per intermediate GPU (8.2 TX + 8.2 RX) | 4.9 s per direction, concurrent | **4–14%** (bubble-bound, comm hidden) | ~290% ✗ |
| EP, 24 MoE layers, EP=5 | 62.9 GB per GPU | 42 s | **~5.7×** | ~72× ✗ |
| TP, 5-way | 314.6 GB per GPU | 210 s | **~28×** | "same shape as EP" ✗ |

**DDP.** The repo's 36% is arithmetically right for the dense 1B case and reproduces with the same inputs. It is wrong for the MoE because the gradient buffer is sized by *total* trainable params (7B × 2 B = 14 GB) while compute is sized by *active* (1B). The ratio is exact: **252% / 36% = 7.0 = 7B/1B**. The general form is `overhead ≈ (3.6e5 / T_global) × (N_total / N_active)` at theoretical peak, or `(1.8e5 / T_global) × (N_total/N_active)` at 405 TFLOPS. The repo's headline #3 is the special case where `N_total = N_active`.

**FSDP.** Two corrections. (a) The multiplier is 1.5×, not 2×: 3Ψ vs 2Ψ, stated identically by FSDP PVLDB-16 and ZeRO SC20 `[P]`. The 2× figure only reproduces if you force fp32 gradient reduction — `MixedPrecision(reduce_dtype=torch.float32)` — because FSDP's default is `reduce_dtype=None`, which "takes on the param_dtype value, still running gradient reduction in low precision" [[fullyshardeddataparallel-pytorch-213-documentation]] `[P]`, giving 2Ψ×2 B + Ψ×2 B = 6 B/param against DDP's 4 B/param = 1.5×; forcing fp32 gives 2Ψ×2 + Ψ×4 = 8 B/param = 2.0×. So 2× is the pessimistic variant, not the default. (b) The repo's table **confounds param counts**: it compares FSDP at 7B trainable (28 GB, 37 s, 72%) against DDP at 1B trainable (2 GB, 2.7 s, 36%). At equal 7B trainable, DDP is 28 GB/18.7 s and FSDP is 42 GB/28.0 s — a 1.5× ratio, not 2×. The repo's FSDP row also uses 2Ψb (DDP's volume) at the algorithmic collective rate, which overstates the time by 1.33×; the correct FSDP overhead at 7B dense is 54%, not 72%.

**PP.** The repo's volume is half-right: 16.4 GB is the *forward-only* activation traffic (1M × 2048 × 2 B × 4 hops). Including the backward activation-gradient hand-offs, the true aggregate is 32.8 GB per step. But the repo's error is not the volume — it is the *model*. PP's traffic is not additive: in 1F1B each stage sends one hand-off while computing the previous micro-batch, so the cost is `max(compute, comm)` per stage, not `compute + comm`. Concretely, at 32 micro-batches of 32k tokens: per-hop hand-off = 32k × 2048 × 2 B = **134 MB**, which at 1.68 GB/s takes **80 ms**; the stage's forward+backward compute for that micro-batch is 6 × 200M × 32k / 162 TFLOPS = **243 ms**. Forward-only utilisation is 33%; including the backward hand-off it is 66%. Each intermediate GPU carries 8.2 GB TX + 8.2 GB RX per step, concurrent (PCIe is full duplex) → 4.9 s against 7.41 s compute = **66% link utilisation, hidden**. The residual cost is the bubble: (5−1)/32 = **12.5%** for 1F1B `[P]`, 11.1% by GPipe's (K−1)/(M+K−1) `[P]`, ~4.2% for ZB-H1 `[P]`, ~0% for ZB-H2 `[P]`, and RoundPipe measures <4.5% with asynchronous optimizer updates `[C]`. So PP's overhead is **4–14%**, not 290%. The repo's 21 s is what you get if you serialise the traffic — which is precisely the thing 1F1B exists to avoid.

Two caveats keep this honest. First, PP's comm is hidden only while the link holds: at 1.68 GB/s/direction the hand-off is 66% of the compute window; if the effective per-direction bandwidth falls below ~1.1 GB/s (contention, NUMA, host-staged bounce-buffer overhead), PP becomes comm-bound (at 0.8 GB/s the hand-off takes 10.2 s against 7.4 s of compute, so overhead rises to ~38%). Second, PP's comm is hidden only above a threshold: `N_active > 2·h·b·F_agg / (6·B_p2p)` ≈ **660M active params** at hidden 2048. The repo's 1B active sits just above that line — PP is hideable but not comfortably so.

**EP.** The repo's ~72× is an order of magnitude too high, for a mundane reason: it divides the **aggregate** dispatch+combine volume (24 layers × 2 × 1M × 2 × 2048 × 2 B ≈ 400 GB) by the **per-GPU** collective rate (0.75 GB/s). The correct per-GPU volume, from Megatron-Core's formula `T·K·h·(EP−1)/EP` per all-to-all [[scalable-training-of-mixture-of-experts-models-with]] `[P]`, is 2.62 GB per layer × 24 = **62.9 GB per GPU** → 42 s → **~5.7×** (7.2× without the (EP−1)/EP factor), not 72×. The same aggregate-vs-per-GPU confusion inflates the repo's PP row. The verdict survives — ~6× compute is still prohibitive — but the number should be relabelled estimate-tier at ~6×, not 72×.

**TP.** The repo has no TP row in the table but headline #2 says "TP is the same shape" as EP. It is not: TP moves 2 all-reduces per layer × 24 layers × 1M × 2048 × 2 B = 196.6 GB of tensor volume, which on a 5-way ring is 1.6× that on the wire = **314.6 GB per GPU per step** → 210 s → **~28×** compute, roughly 5× worse than EP. And the reason is regime-dependent, which is where the locus's own rationale is wrong. At *decode* (T=1) TP is latency-bound — 86 collectives × a host-staged round trip — which is what the club measured as flat ~800 tok/s prefill and 60.4 tok/s decode `[C]`. At *training* batch sizes (32k-token micro-batches) the tensors are 134 MB and TP is **bandwidth-bound**; the flat prefill curve from 1.5k to 77k tokens is equally consistent with bandwidth-bound behaviour (a constant comm/compute ratio), and if it were latency-bound throughput would have risen with batch size. So the repo's "86 all-reduces per forward" is an inference framing applied to a training question.

### Position A: "DDP is the only viable topology" — its best case

The strongest version is not "DDP is fast" but "DDP is the only topology that fits the frozen-table design without new systems work." On that reading: DDP is one line of code, has no bubble, no stage-balancing problem, no static-shape constraint, and no custom scheduler; the 1B-trainable / 6B-frozen design lands at 28 GB/card and fits; and at 1M-token batches the all-reduce is only 2.67 s against 7.4 s of compute, so the dense-arm overhead is 36% at peak and ~18% at realistic MFU — which is exactly the measured 18% at 494M tokens `[M]`. FSDP is worse on the link (1.5×), PP needs model surgery and a scheduler, TP and EP are 6–28× compute. Under the constraint "the trainable set must fit one card", DDP is the rational default, and the repo's recommendation is defensible *for that design*.

### Position B: "PP is viable and cheaper than FSDP on a bandwidth-starved fabric" — its best case

The strongest version is structural, not empirical: **PP is the only topology whose communication volume is independent of parameter count.** DDP and FSDP move `O(params)`; PP moves `O(tokens × hidden × stages)`. For the repo's 7B/1B MoE at hidden 2048, PP moves 32.8 GB/step while DDP moves 28 GB and FSDP 42 GB — comparable volume, but PP's is *overlappable* (134 MB per hand-off inside a 243 ms compute window) whereas DDP's is a single 18.7 s barrier at the end of the step and FSDP's is a per-layer all-gather on the forward critical path. GPipe states the design intent explicitly — "efficient scaling performance even on accelerators without high-speed interconnects" `[P]`; Megatron-LM calls PP's point-to-point "much cheaper… without bottlenecking the entire computation" `[P]`; RoundPipe demonstrates it with *zero* GPU P2P at 1.48–2.16× over FSDP `[C]`; Mobius demonstrates the same on 4×3090-Ti with 3.8–5.1× lower training time than GPipe/DeepSpeed `[C]`; ZeroPP shows PP+FSDP beating TP at every batch size with the gap widening as batch grows `[C]`. And the bubble is not a fixed tax: 1F1B's (p−1)/m is a ceiling that Zero Bubble PP drives to zero and RoundPipe drives below 4.5% `[P][C]`. The decisive point is memory: PP=5 puts 7B trainable at **22.4 GB of weights+optimizer per stage + ~3.4 GB of in-flight activations ≈ 25.8 GB/card**, which fits 64 GB with 38 GB of headroom — so PP trains the goal doc's 7B-trainable MoE *without* freezing anything, while DDP needs 112 GB/card and cannot.

### Position C: "DDP's overhead is invariant to model size" — its best case

For dense models this is not just true, it is an identity. Comm time = `2·N·b / B_wire`; compute time = `6·N·T / F_agg`; the ratio is `2·b·F_agg / (6·T·B_wire)`, independent of `N`. At 1M tokens and 810 TFLOPS that is 36%, and it stays 36% whether the model is 500M or 7B — until it stops fitting. The repo's arithmetic is correct, its 494M datapoint is consistent with it, and the "bigger global batches are the only lever" conclusion follows. The invariance is a real property of DDP, not a mistake.

### Position D: "That invariance fails for MoE" — its best case

The failure is exact and mechanical, not a matter of degree. In a standard MoE every expert weight is an `nn.Parameter` with `requires_grad=True`, so DDP's reducer buckets and all-reduces the **full** parameter set regardless of which experts saw tokens; an expert that received zero tokens contributes a zero gradient but still occupies its slice of the all-reduce buffer. Megatron-Core states both halves in one paragraph: "All 𝐸 experts' parameters, gradients, and optimizer states must reside in memory" and "Per-token FLOPs scale only with 𝑁_active (∝𝐾), not 𝑁_total (∝𝐸), leaving insufficient computation to overlap with the growing communication. The consequence: MoE training is fundamentally communication-bound unless parallelism is designed specifically for this asymmetry. **This is not a matter of degree; it is a qualitative difference from dense models**" `[P]`. DeepSpeed-MoE's 52B-total/1.3B-active model is the production instance of a 40× ratio `[P]`; DeepSeek-V3's 671B/37B is 18× `[P]`; the repo's own axis is 7×. The overhead formula is `36% × (N_total/N_active)` at 1M tokens — so the repo's 36% becomes **252%** for its own stated MoE axis. The repo's headline #3 is true only for the frozen-table design where trainable = active = 1B; it is false for the goal doc's "sparse MoE up to 7B total / ~1B active", whose 7B experts are trainable by construction (MoE routing is learned).

### Position E: "EP is ruled out by the fabric" — its best case, and the better reason

The repo's arithmetic case is directionally right but numerically weak: ~6× compute (per-GPU volume at the wire rate), not 72×. The *stronger* case is a software barrier, and ThunderEP states it as a closed list: "Communication libraries specialized for MoE EP, including DeepEP, pplx-kernels, NIXL-EP, NCCL EP, and UCCL-EP, similarly rely on NVLink, GPUDirect P2P, or RDMA for their optimized GPU-initiated paths" and "existing EP communication libraries cannot operate on these systems" [[efficient-expert-parallel-communication]] `[P]`. Without P2P "every inter-GPU transfer must instead be staged through a bounce buffer in CPU memory", and NCCL's ring all-gather then moves `2(N−1)S` bytes per GPU against `N·S` for a single-step host broadcast — a `2(N−1)/N` penalty, 1.6× at N=5 `[P]`. ThunderEP itself proves degraded-but-workable host-staged EP is *possible* (2.00× dispatch, 1.53× combine over NCCL `[P]`), but it is inference-only, with no gradient collectives. So EP is ruled out for a reason the repo does not give: **no EP library runs on this fabric at all**, and the one research prototype that does is not a training system. The repo's 72× should be relabelled ~6× estimate-tier.

## Committed position

**DDP is not the only viable topology, and for the repo's own stated MoE axis it is not even a viable one.** The repo's "DDP is the only viable topology" is true only for the ≤1.5B-trainable / frozen-table design, where trainable = active = 1B and the 36% overhead is arithmetically correct; it is false for the goal doc's "sparse MoE up to 7B total / ~1B active", because DDP at 7B trainable needs 112 GB/card on 64 GB cards — it does not fit at all — and if you force it through FSDP you pay 3Ψ = 42 GB/step against 1B-active compute, i.e. 378% overhead. PP is the topology that makes the stated axis feasible: it shards the 7B by layer (22.4 GB of weights+optimizer per stage, ~25.8 GB/card with in-flight activations), moves only activations (32.8 GB/step, 134 MB per hand-off inside a 243 ms compute window, 66% link utilisation and therefore hidden), and pays a bubble of 4–14% rather than 252–378%. The load-bearing reason is a single asymmetry: **PP's communication scales with tokens × hidden, DDP's and FSDP's scale with parameters** — so PP is the only topology whose cost does not grow when you add experts, which is exactly the axis this box is built to explore. The repo's PP row is wrong not because its volume is wrong (16.4 GB is the forward half of a 32.8 GB total) but because it models PP's traffic as additive when 1F1B exists precisely to overlap it; and its headline #3 is wrong because it conflates "params" with "active params" — the correct invariance is `overhead = 36% × (N_total/N_active)` at 1M tokens, which is 36% for dense and 252% for 7B/1B. EP and TP are both out, but EP for a different and stronger reason than the repo gives (no EP library runs without NVLink/P2P/RDMA), and TP for a different reason than the locus assumed (bandwidth-bound at training batch sizes, latency-bound only at decode).

- **Position:** PP is the correct training topology for the 7B-total/~1B-active MoE arm at 1M-token global batches on this box — DDP cannot fit it and FSDP costs 1.5× DDP's bytes for the same total params — while DDP remains the right default for the ≤1.5B-trainable frozen-table arm.
- **Confidence:** high on the qualitative claim (PP is viable and DDP is not unique; the MoE invariance fails by exactly the total/active ratio; EP is blocked by library requirements), **medium** on the quantitative margin (PP 4–14% vs DDP 252%) because every number is arithmetic from user-reported bandwidths and there is **zero** on-box training measurement in the corpus.
- **Boundary conditions:** This holds for hidden 2048, 5 stages, 32k-token micro-batches, and a per-direction link of ≥1.1 GB/s. Below ~660M active params PP's hand-off stops being hideable and PP becomes comm-bound; below ~1.1 GB/s per direction the same happens at 1B active. If MoE stage imbalance or `lm_head` weight-binding pushes the bubble past ~30% (RoundPipe's measured Llama-3.1-8B figure `[C]`), PP's overhead rises to ~43% — still ~3× better than DDP's 126% at realistic MFU, but no longer a landslide. The position does not extend to 128k context, where Seq1F1B's single-micro-batch memory overflow becomes the binding constraint `[P]`.
- **What would change this position:** (a) a measured 5-stage PP step on this box at 1M tokens showing >60% overhead — that would mean the hand-off is not hideable in practice, and DDP-with-frozen-tables becomes the right call; (b) a measured DDP all-reduce on a 7B bf16 gradient buffer sustaining >2 GB/s, which would cut the MoE overhead to <100% and make DDP competitive; (c) a demonstration that `torch.distributed.pipelining` cannot express this model's static shapes (MoE routing + Engram lookup + CSA2 top-k), which would make PP an engineering non-starter regardless of its arithmetic; (d) a measured 5-GPU all-reduce materially worse than 0.75 GiB/s (e.g. <0.3 GB/s), which would make *both* DDP and FSDP hopeless and strengthen PP further rather than weaken it.
- **Evidence weight:** 5 published systems papers (Megatron-LM, GPipe, Zero Bubble PP, Seq1F1B, PipeOffload) plus 4 community systems (RoundPipe, Mobius, ZeroPP, BFS-PP) support PP's viability on slow fabrics; 2 primaries (FSDP PVLDB-16, ZeRO SC20) plus the FSDP docs establish the 1.5× multiplier and its fp32-reduce 2× variant; 1 production MoE reference (Megatron-Core) plus DeepSpeed-MoE and DeepSeek-V3 establish the total/active asymmetry; 1 paper (ThunderEP) establishes the EP library barrier; 2 community measurements (club-170hx PP-vs-TP 6.6× prefill, 4.2× link-tolerance) establish the direction on this hardware class; **0** on-box training, DDP, or PP-training measurements exist.

## Open questions

- **No one has measured PP training on this box.** The 66% link-utilisation figure is arithmetic from a single-card host-copy bandwidth (1.68 GB/s) and assumes full-duplex TX/RX and no root-complex contention. ThunderEP measured 6×5090 at Gen5 x16 dropping from 56.5 to 35.5 GB/s per GPU under two-way concurrency `[C]`; the equivalent 5-card Gen2 x4 measurement does not exist. A `torch.distributed.pipelining` 1F1B run with 5 stages and 32 micro-batches of 32k tokens would settle it in one afternoon.
- **The 0.75 GiB/s all-reduce is unverified at 5 GPUs and Gen2 x4.** The corpus's only NCCL datapoint is 2-GPU at Gen2 x16 (3.69–3.85 GB/s bus bandwidth) `[C]`. Whether the 5-GPU ring degrades to 0.75 GiB/s or holds closer to the 1.68 GB/s wire rate changes every DDP/FSDP number by up to 2×.
- **PP's bubble under MoE routing imbalance is unquantified.** RoundPipe's 30% figure is for a dense Llama-3.1-8B with weight binding `[C]`; no one has measured a 5-stage MoE pipeline's bubble with top-2 routing at 32k sequences. This is the single largest uncertainty in PP's 4–14% claim.
- **Whether PP can express this model at all.** `torch.distributed.pipelining` requires static shapes `[P]`; Engram's hash-based lookup and CSA2's non-differentiable top-k may produce data-dependent shapes. Manual stage splitting is the likely route, and its engineering cost is not in any source.
- **The repo's internal inconsistency is unresolved.** `goal-and-requirements.md` describes a 7B-total/1B-active MoE (experts trainable); `research.md` §5's memory table describes 1B trainable + 6B frozen. These are different models, and the topology analysis silently switches between them. The drafter should force the repo to pick one before quoting any overhead number.
- **FSDP's real multiplier on this box.** 1.5× assumes bf16 gradient reduction (`reduce_dtype=None` → `param_dtype`) `[P]`; if the recipe forces fp32 reduce-scatter for stability it becomes 2.0×. No source says which this project would use.
