- [[deepseek-v3-technical-report]] — DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report — DeepSeek-V3 technical report (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv 2412.19437, Dec 2024) — the canonical primary source for what expert parallelism actually costs and what it takes to hide that cost. Model: 671B total / 37B active MoE with MLA and DeepSeekMoE, 8 routed experts plus 1 shared, trained on 14.8T tokens on 2048 H800 GPUs for 2.664M GPU hours (~180K H800 GPU-hours per trillion tokens; 2.788M GPU hours / $5.576M total). The load-bearing admission for this run: 'the communication overhead introduced by cross-node expert parallelism results in an inefficient computation-to-communication ratio of approximately 1:1' — i.e. on their fabric, EP all-to-all cost roughly as much time as the compute it fed, before overlap. Their fix is DualPipe, which splits each chunk into attention / all-to-all dispatch / MLP / all-to-all combine and rearranges forward and backward chunks so that both all-to-all and PP communication are fully hidden, reaching 'near-zero all-to-all communication overhead' as long as the compute-to-communication ratio is held constant. That overlap is built on a two-tier fabric: intra-node NVLink at 160 GB/s versus inter-node IB at 50 GB/s (3.2x), with each token dispatched to at most 4 nodes (averaging 3.2 experts per node, so up to 13 experts at the same communication cost) and only 20 SMs dedicated to saturating both links. Training topology: EP32 with no tensor parallelism at all — they explicitly say the memory work 'enabl[es] us to train DeepSeek-V3 without using costly Tensor Parallelism (TP)'. Inference uses TP4+DP8+EP32 for prefill and EP320 for decode. Relevance: this is the strongest published evidence that (a) EP all-to-all is a first-order cost even on NVLink+IB, (b) TP was avoided for training by the lab with the most reason to use it, and (c) the standard mitigation (overlap) presupposes a fast intra-node tier and a separate scale-out network — neither of which exists on a flat Gen2 x4 no-P2P box. `review`
- [[democratizing-moe-inference-on-commodity-gpus]] — Democratizing MoE inference on commodity GPUs — CoMoE (Fan et al., Tsinghua + Alibaba Cloud, arXiv 2610.09424): host-centric MoE inference on commodity GPUs. Its measurement section is the cleanest quantification of how much a no-P2P PCIe fabric costs MoE: on 8x RTX 5090 (PCIe 5.0 x16, 64 GB/s nominal per GPU vs 400 GB/s NVLink = 0.16x), inter-GPU communication accounts for 81.6% of total MoE execution time, and naive deployment reaches only 61% of the throughput of an FLOP-matched datacenter GPU. CoMoE elevates host DRAM from a passive bounce buffer to an active routing hub — host-backed token multicast for dispatch (each shared token written once, receivers pull in parallel) and token-level host-staged aggregation for combine (replacing the global All-to-All barrier with a completion-driven pipeline that absorbs stragglers). Results: 1.24-1.41x throughput per model (up to 1.46x), 85-90% of an 8x A800 + DeepEP configuration at 23.4% of the hardware cost, TTFT -18.8% and TPOT -26.9% on average, combine latency 2.46x geometric-mean faster than NCCL All-to-All. Ablations show the win comes from decomposing All-to-All into one-sided transfers (1.55x) plus NUMA-aware buffers, thread-tile pipelining and batched commit (to 2.46x). Scope: inference, not training; 8 GPUs on Gen5 x16 — a far faster link than Gen2 x4. `review`
- [[efficient-expert-parallel-communication]] — Efficient Expert-Parallel Communication — ThunderEP (Lee et al., Seoul National University, arXiv 2609.40093): an EP communication design for MoE inference on PCIe consumer GPUs with no P2P. Establishes the fabric mechanics the CMP 170HX box shares: without P2P every inter-GPU byte is host-staged and crosses PCIe twice (sender writes to pinned host buffer, receiver reads it back), so NCCL's ring All-Gather moves 2(N-1)S bytes per GPU versus N*S for a single-step host broadcast — a ratio of 2(N-1)/N, i.e. 1.6x at N=5. Measured PCIe contention on 6x RTX 5090 (Gen5 x16): one GPU alone reaches 56.5 GB/s, but two GPUs under the same host bridge communicating concurrently drop to 35.5 GB/s each, so per-GPU bandwidth falls as more GPUs join a collective. ThunderEP (single-step host broadcast, DMA-engine transfers, per-sender completion flags) beats NCCL by 2.00x on dispatch and 1.53x on combine, 1.66x end-to-end prefill and 1.26x decode; ablation TTFT 2115.9 ms -> 1436.4 ms. Scope limits: inference only (no gradient collectives), and every existing EP library (DeepEP, pplx-kernels, NCCL EP, UCCL-EP, NIXL) assumes NVLink, GPUDirect P2P or RDMA and therefore cannot run on this fabric at all. `review`

Builds mini-gda and mini-proxy minimal transports and measures them against NVSHMEM IBGDA, NCCL GIN, DeepEP, UCCL-EP, MSCCL++ and fabric-lib on H100/H200/B200/GB200 with ConnectX-7 InfiniBand. - [[memory-efficient-expert-routing-for-distributed-moe]] — Memory-Efficient Expert Routing for Distributed MoE — RelayMoE (Tarafder et al., William & Mary + BSC, arXiv 2610.07333): memory-efficient expert routing for distributed MoE training. Its diagnostic contribution is the most useful part for a 64 GB-card budget: peak memory in MoE training is set by the MoE block, not attention, because every intermediate buffer in the dispatch pipeline is scaled by top-k. Profiling a Qwen3-style model (top-8, 4 layers) at micro-batch 24 / 16384 tokens on 8x H100 with TP=SP=2, CP=4, EP=8 and full recompute, the MoE input activation is only 336 MiB per GPU but the top-k-expanded dispatch tensors are ~2.6 GiB each (8x expansion) and per-layer peak exceeds 42 GB during backward, with spikes to 51 GB against a 14 GB inter-spike baseline. - [[multi-head-latentmoe-and-head-parallel]] — Multi-Head LatentMoE and Head Parallel: — Multi-Head LatentMoE and Head Parallel (Cui et al., Arizona State University, arXiv 2602.04870): an architecture + parallelism co-design that attacks the property that makes EP expensive on a slow fabric. EP's communication volume and all-to-all latency are O(k) in activated experts, traffic is load-imbalanced, and routing-dependent dispatch needs a metadata exchange to stay deterministic. Multi-Head LatentMoE splits each token into Nh sub-tokens, each with its own router and expert set, and Head Parallel distributes sub-tokens to GPUs BEFORE any routing decision, so each GPU routes and computes entirely locally. Result: O(1) communication volume regardless of k, perfectly balanced traffic, deterministic communication — and still composable with EP. At k=4 the communication volume is 25% of EP's. Trained on a 10B-token FineWeb-EDU subset on H100 80GB (NVLink): 1.11x faster at 2B total params (31.68 vs 35.36 h) and 1.61x faster at 4B (34.41 vs 55.34 h) at comparable quality, with IO-aware routing (online top-k in SRAM) and a pure-PyTorch dropless expert computation to avoid custom kernels. Relevance: this is the strongest architectural counterexample to 'expert parallelism is ruled out by the fabric' — MoE training can be made fabric-insensitive by moving the communication before routing, at the cost of a fixed per-layer all-to-all that is still host-staged on a no-P2P box. `review`

- [[scalable-training-of-mixture-of-experts-models-with]] — Scalable Training of Mixture-of-Experts Models with — Scalable Training of MoE Models with Megatron Core (NVIDIA technical report, arXiv 2603.07685, Mar 2026; 39k words): the production reference for how EP/TP/PP/DP compose for MoE training, and the source of the arithmetic this run needs for the EP question. It frames MoE training as three coupled walls. The Communication Wall: EP's per-GPU send volume in one all-to-all is approximately T*K*h*(EP-1)/EP (T local tokens, K top-k, h hidden dim), and a dispatch+combine cycle doubles it; as EP grows the volume saturates but traffic migrates from NVLink to narrower inter-node links where bandwidth drops by an order of magnitude, and unoptimized all-to-all can consume up to 60% of total training time in DeepSeek-V3-like models. The Memory Wall: all E experts' parameters, gradients and optimizer states must be resident even though only K activate, and offloading to host memory costs PCIe bandwidth. The Compute Efficiency Wall: TP on MoE experts is actively harmful because expert hidden dimensions are already small, so TP shards them into even smaller GEMMs while adding communication; GEMMs are ~70% of execution time in dense Llama-3 405B but under 50% in DeepSeek-V3, with routing/permutation adding ~9%. DeepSeek-V3 illustrates the parameter-compute mismatch: 685B total vs 37B active per token (18x). Dispatcher backends are AllGather, all-to-all (NCCL), and Flex (DeepEP/HybridEP, which assume NVLink-rich topologies). Reference throughput on GB300/GB200: 1,233/1,048 TFLOPS/GPU for DeepSeek-V3-685B, 974/919 for Qwen3-235B. It documents why PCIe all-to-all is hard: no dedicated scale-up fabric means intra-node GPU traffic and NIC traffic share the same PCIe hierarchy, so concurrent transfers contend on bottleneck links; demand is skewed (top-k routing) and changes every layer (sub-millisecond per layer), defeating static schedules.
