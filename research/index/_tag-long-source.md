- [[analysing-the-impact-of-sequence-composition]] — Analysing The Impact of Sequence Composition — Zhao et al., 'Analysing The Impact of Sequence Composition on Language Model Pre-Training' (arXiv 2402.13991; ACL 2024 long paper) — the primary source behind the ACL landing-page note. Setup: LLaMA-architecture 1.3B-parameter models pretrained on 150B tokens sampled from SlimPajama (7 sub-corpora, token proportions preserved), at context windows of 2,048 (2K) and 8,192 (8K). Four sequence-construction / masking variants: MIXChunk (baseline random concatenation), UNIChunk (same-source documents only), BM25Chunk (retrieval-based: for each document, BM25-select related documents from the corpus to fill the chunk), and INTRADoc (intra-document causal masking).

Results: BM25Chunk has the lowest PPL of the three causal-masking variants — -0.62 at 2K and -0.78 at 8K vs MIXChunk; UNIChunk also beats MIXChunk (-0.34 / -0.21). INTRADoc has the lowest PPL of all. GitHub is the most sensitive domain: INTRADoc improves over UNIChunk by -1.3 PPL at 2K and -2.0 at 8K, suggesting code pretraining is disproportionately harmed by unrelated preceding context. Downstream: BM25Chunk improves in-context learning +11.6%, knowledge memorisation +9.8%, context utilisation +7.2%.

Cost: intra-document causal masking costs 4.0% training-efficiency degradation in their implementation, implemented with FlashAttention-2; the authors state they do not fully solve this efficiency issue. This is the number to price against any packing / masking change on the CMP 170HX box: a 4% throughput tax buys double-digit downstream gains, a favourable trade at small scale. `review`
- [[cramming-training-a-language-model-on-a]] — CRAMMING: TRAINING A LANGUAGE MODEL ON A — Cramming (Geiping & Goldstein, arXiv 2212.14034) is the canonical single-GPU compute-budget study: train a transformer LM from scratch with masked LM on one GPU for 24 hours and measure GLUE. Hardware tested: RTX 2080 Ti (11 GB), RTX A4000 (16 GB), RTX A6000 (48 GB), each paired with 4 CPU cores and 32 GB RAM; the A6000 run reaches BERT-base-class GLUE in one day.

Findings that bear on CMP 170HX feasibility: (1) scaling laws hold in the extreme low-compute regime — per-token training efficiency depends strongly on model size but not on transformer type, so architecture changes only help insofar as they raise throughput at constant parameter count; (2) the authors explicitly find no benefit from CPU/NVMe offloading in this setting; (3) FlashAttention (Dao et al. 2022) is only re-enabled for the final architecture variant after ablations, i.e. it is a throughput lever, not an accuracy lever; (4) the ablation accounting attributes ~2 GLUE points to architecture changes, ~1 point to data changes, and ~0.5 point to training-recipe changes; (5) sequence length 128 with packed sequences gives micro-batches of 64-96 on the 2080 Ti, accumulated into larger batches, and single-epoch training is feasible at this budget.

Relevance: it is the strongest published evidence that a single GPU with no interconnect at all can produce a usable model in a bounded wall-clock budget — the limiting case of the CMP 170HX box (5 cards, no NVLink, no P2P). It also warns that offloading, a plausible workaround for a 64 GB-per-card memory ceiling, bought nothing in their measurements. `review`
- [[delta-n-gram-forward-only-continual-writing]] — Delta N-gram: Forward-Only Continual Writing — Delta N-gram (Hao Wu, Illinois Institute of Technology, arXiv 2610.31125v1, 22 Oct 2026) — the strongest counter-datapoint to the run's frozen-table optimism, and the paper that makes the Memory Grafting design space precise. Setup: a hashed N-gram table T = T0 + Δ, where T0 is the pretrained table and Δ is a sparse patch nonzero only on written rows; every row with Δj = 0 reads exactly its pretrained value, and clearing Δ returns the original model. On Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (48 layers, 512 routed experts, 10 active/token) the native table is read through H = 16 hash heads (8 bigram + 8 trigram), row width d = 160, concatenated to R^2560 and injected before layer-2 attention. Backbone, read-out and T0 are ALL frozen; the only trained component is a 4,075,681-parameter writer. Writes at use time are forward-only: no backpropagation, no optimizer state, no labels; the state stores only written row ids, patches Δj and write counts cj — explicitly 'It carries no optimizer state.'

That is the direct support for the repo's memory math: a frozen table costs its bf16 rows and nothing else (no fp32 master, no m/v, no grad), and the paper demonstrates a working design whose trainable budget is 4.1M params against a 48-layer 512-expert backbone. But the paper's own training run contradicts the box's fabric assumptions: the writer was trained on four H200 GPUs with expert parallelism 4 and data parallelism 4, 12 trajectories per update, AdamW lr 3e-4, 100M target tokens over 4,172 updates. EP4 is exactly the topology the CMP 170HX's no-P2P Gen2 x4 fabric rules out, so reproducing this recipe on the box would require re-deriving the writer training under pure DDP.

The capability results are the counter-datapoint. Frozen memory is NOT free capacity: on a 0.6B prototype the forward writer keeps 16/16 facts after a second write where online gradient writes keep 2-4/16, but the binding is tied to the trained question form — a paraphrased question stays at the empty-memory 2/16 level, and the rows such queries read overlap the written rows in only ~6% of positions. Longer chains erode: four writes leave 60/64 facts answerable, sixteen writes leave 46/64 right after their own write but only 19 at the end. On 64 held-out late-2025 news events the plain writer lowers continuation NLL by 0.068 nats/token against empty memory and 0.024 against another document's memory (0.063 and 0.013 after 16 intervening articles); the delta writer is more document-specific and retains 70% vs 54% of that specificity under distraction, at a smaller total gain.

Long-chain stability is real: 35,000 consecutive writes on two hardware stacks with no numerical failure, and learned-task accuracy drifts only -0.26pp (plain) / -0.52pp (delta) over the rest of the chain versus -7.29pp for the Adam table updates of the prior Representation Drift work. But net acquisition on tasks the writer never saw is NEGATIVE (-12/448) and recall of written examples is only 23-25%. General-input big-K drift is 32.8%, 1.6x the Adam baseline's 20.75%, concentrated in eight format bigram rows that every example writes and whose patches reach 4.4-11.7x their T0 norm; zeroing those eight rows removes over 98% of the layer-2 drift on math inputs — and also removes their NLL gain. Optional decay pulls Δ toward zero (table toward T0), giving reversible forgetting.

Training data mix (relevant because it overlaps the run's own pipeline): Stack v2 Edu 20%, arXiv 25%, Project Gutenberg 20% (Common Pile), FineWeb-Edu 15%, CC-News 17.5%, same-event CC-News streams 2.5%; 38,566 training documents. Prototype used a zero-initialised external table of 16,384 rows each for bigrams and trigrams, row width 1024, 1,190,465 writer params. 16,791 words — LONG SOURCE, flag for source-analyst delegation. `review`
- [[published-as-a-conference-paper-at-colm-2026]] — HISA: Efficient Hierarchical Indexing for Fine-Grained Sparse Attention (COLM 2026) — HISA: Efficient Hierarchical Indexing for Fine-Grained Sparse Attention (Xu, Meng et al., Peking University + Xiaohongshu, arXiv 2603.28458v4, 10 Sep 2026, COLM 2026) — the hierarchical/pooled-block indexer prior work behind DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's CSA2, and the paper that makes the run's 32k CSA2 question answerable. Problem statement: token-level sparse attention (DSA, DeepSeek-V3.2, also GLM-5) scores EVERY historical key per query through a lightweight indexer before running Sparse MLA on the top-k; the downstream attention is cheap but the indexer still scans the full prefix, so per-layer indexing cost is O(L^2) — the same asymptotic scaling as dense attention — and it 'can transition from a negligible overhead into the dominant cost component' as context grows. Measured confirmation: at 64K context with top-k=2048, the Sparse MLA operator costs a roughly constant ~1.6 ms while the indexer reaches 5.6 ms, i.e. the indexer is ~3.5x the sparse-attention cost and is 'the main performance bottleneck in DSA'.

HISA's fix is a two-stage rewrite of the search path that preserves the search result: (1) block-level coarse filtering — partition the prefix into M = ceil(L/B) causally valid blocks, mean-pool each block's indexing keys into one representative key, score those pooled keys with the SAME indexer queries and gating weights as DSA (J_t,b = sum_j w_j * ReLU(q_j . k_pooled_b)), and take the top-m blocks (first and last blocks always forced in, following MoBA, to keep the attention sink and local context); (2) token-level refinement — run the original DSA scoring only over the union of tokens in the selected blocks, then take the final top-k. Per-query cost drops from O(L) to O(L/B + mB), with the feasibility constraint mB >= k. Crucially it is a plug-and-play replacement that preserves the token-level top-k index interface consumed by Sparse MLA and leaves the KV cache layout unchanged; pooled summaries are maintained incrementally alongside the KV cache.

THE BOUNDARY BEHAVIOUR IS THE LOAD-BEARING PART FOR THIS RUN. Three regimes: when t <= k, all prefix tokens are selected and HISA is EQUIVALENT TO DENSE ATTENTION; when k < t <= mB, the coarse filter selects all blocks (m >= M) and Stage 2 reduces to k tokens, so HISA is EQUIVALENT TO THE ORIGINAL DSA INDEXER with no hierarchical advantage; only when t > mB does non-trivial block pruning activate. So the hierarchical win is a step function of the candidate budget mB, not a smooth function of context. With the paper's configuration (B=128, m=64 -> mB=8192) the cap binds at 32k (32768 > 8192) and pruning is active; with a 16,384 cap (the figure the repo attributes to CSA2) 32k still exceeds it by 2x, so the cap DOES bind at 32k — the repo's phrasing that it 'barely binds' understates the pruning but its qualitative conclusion (a smaller win than the published 1M-context numbers) holds.

Measured results. Kernel-level, ALL ON AN NVIDIA A100 (SM80 — the same architecture class as the CMP 170HX's GA100), both indexers in TileLang, query len 1024, top-k 2048, B=128: at 64K context HISA gives ~2.16x speedup at a 4:1 first-stage compression ratio (16K candidate budget) and up to 3.75x under a fixed 8K budget; the paper's abstract cites 2-4x kernel-level speedup at 64K. End-to-end in SGLang on DeepSeek-V3.2 (8xH200, TP=8, 128K input / 512 output): DSA official 5868.43 tok/s, E2E 143764.47 ms, TTFT 106449.81 ms, TPOT 73.02 ms; HISA (B=128, m=64) 9712.21 tok/s, 86718.06 ms, 62722.91 ms, 46.96 ms -> 1.65x throughput, 1.66x E2E, 1.70x TTFT, 1.55x TPOT, achieved with TileLang/Triton operators that are less mature than DeepSeek's hand-tuned CUDA, so the speedups understate HISA at matched kernel maturity.

Quality: HISA is TRAINING-FREE — applied at inference time by replacing the indexer module, with no fine-tuning and no architectural modification. NIAH (8K-648K contexts, 0-100% needle depth): HISA closely matches DSA's near-perfect retrieval with only marginal degradation at extreme lengths/depths, while the block-sparse baseline degrades badly in the middle of the context. LongBench averages: DeepSeek-V3.2 DSA 51.05, HISA 50.78 (-0.27), Block-Sparse 49.54; GLM-5 DSA 46.01, HISA 46.32 (+0.31), Block-Sparse 42.67. So hierarchical indexing costs ~0.3 LongBench points and buys 1.65x serving throughput.

Two corrections this forces on the run's CSA2 reasoning. (a) The repo says CSA2's indexer 'adds extra code and an extra loss term' to training; HISA shows the HIERARCHICAL part is training-free and drop-in, so only the base DSA indexer needs the distillation loss — the hierarchical layer adds zero training cost. (b) The repo's premise that no DSA path runs on SM80 is too strong: the cuDNN DSA kernels are SM90/SM100-only, but HISA's indexer kernels are TileLang and were benchmarked on A100, so the dominant cost component (the indexer) has an SM80-runnable implementation; the caveat is that HISA retains the official flashmla sparse path downstream, which is Hopper-oriented. Open directions the authors name: overlapping/adaptive blocks or max pooling to reduce coarse-filter information loss, and 'training-aware HISA' (jointly training the block-scoring stage) which they explicitly have NOT done. 8,581 words — LONG SOURCE, flag for source-analyst delegation. `review`

- [[moba-mixture-of-block-attention-for]] — MoBA: Mixture of Block Attention for Long-Context LLMs — MoBA: Mixture of Block Attention for Long-Context LLMs (Moonshot AI + Tsinghua + Zhejiang Lab, arXiv 2502.13189, technical report) — the TRAINED block-level sparse-attention baseline that HISA compares against and whose forced first/last-block rule HISA adopts, and the most directly comparable 32K training datapoint in the corpus. Mechanism: partition the context of length N into n blocks of size B = N/n; apply MoE-style top-k gating where the affinity score is s_i = <q, mean pool(K[I_i])> — the inner product between the query and the MEAN-POOLED keys of block i — and g_i = 1 iff s_i is in the top-k; attention is computed only over blocks with g_i > 0, with a causal constraint forbidding future blocks. This is exactly the pooled-block scoring HISA reuses for its Stage 1. MoBA adds no parameters and removes none, so it is a drop-in substitute for full attention, and the paper shows sliding-window attention and attention sink are special cases of MoBA under particular gating networks.

THE 32K RESULT THAT MATTERS: the scaling-law experiment trains five models (568M/10.8B tokens, 822M/15.3B, 1.1B/20.6B, 1.5B/27.4B, 2.1B/36.9B) at sequence length 8K with block size 512 and top-k 3 (sparsity up to 81.25%). At 8K, MoBA and full attention track within 1e-3 LM loss (fitted curves 2.625*C^-0.063 vs 2.622*C^-0.063). But when the maximum sequence length is raised from 8K to 32K — making the pattern 95.31% sparse — MoBA shows a marginally higher trailing-token LM loss than full attention in ALL FIVE experiments (fitted 1.546*C^-0.108 vs 1.464*C^-0.097), with the gap progressively narrowing as compute grows. So block-level sparsity trained at 8K extrapolates WORSE to 32K than full attention at these scales, which is the strongest published evidence for the repo's suspicion that CSA-style sparsity disappoints at 32k.

THE FIX, and the recipe the box should copy: a MoBA/full HYBRID. Three 1.5B models trained on 30B tokens at 32K context with block size 2048 and top-k 3: (a) MoBA/full hybrid — MoBA for 90% of tokens, then full attention for the last 10%; (b) full attention throughout; (c) MoBA throughout. The MoBA-only recipe gives higher position-wise losses for trailing tokens, while the 90/10 hybrid 'reaches a loss nearly identical to that of full attention', with no significant loss spikes at the switch. A layer-wise hybrid is also proposed for SFT: switching the LAST several Transformer layers from MoBA to full attention significantly reduces SFT loss, motivated by the observation that MoBA is sometimes suboptimal during SFT because prompt tokens are masked out of the loss, creating a sparse-gradient problem for sparse attention. Block granularity also matters: at 1.5B/32K, holding sparsity at 75%, there is a 1e-2 LM-loss difference between the coarsest setting (2 of 8 blocks) and finer settings (4/16, 8/32, 16/64, 32/128), so fine-grained segmentation is a general win for MoE-family attention.

Downstream and efficiency: Llama-8B-1M-MoBA (continual pre-training from Llama 3.1 8B Base, 128K -> 256K -> 512K -> 1M with position interpolation) matches Llama-8B-1M-Full within noise across AGIEval (0.5144/0.5146), BBH (0.6573/0.6589), CEval (0.6273/0.6165), GSM8K (0.7278/0.7142), HellaSWAG (0.8262/0.8279), Loogle (0.4209/0.4016), Competition Math (0.4254/0.4324), MBPP (0.5380/0.5320), MBPP Sanitized (0.6926/0.6615), MMLU (0.4903/0.4904), MMLU Pro (0.4295/0.4328), HumanEval (0.6951/0.7012), SimpleQA (0.0465/0.0492), TriviaQA (0.5673/0.5667) and LongBench@32K (0.4828/0.4821). Efficiency: up to 6.5x speedup when prefilling 1M tokens, and 16x reduction in attention computation time at 10M tokens. MoBA is deployed to support Kimi's long-context requests.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE BOX, versus HISA: MoBA's gate is LEARNED — it is part of the model, trained end-to-end, with the same parameter count as full attention. So adopting MoBA on the CMP 170HX means training the block router, which is a real training-time cost and a real architecture change. HISA's hierarchical indexer, by contrast, is training-free and drop-in. The two are complementary: HISA gives the inference-time indexer speedup without retraining, while MoBA gives a trainable sparse-attention architecture whose 90/10 hybrid recipe is the only published recipe that matches full attention at 32K. The repo's CSA2 experiment should therefore specify which of the two it is testing. 8,376 words — LONG SOURCE, flag for source-analyst delegation. `review`
- [[physical-modsmd]] — Physical modifications — AC-coupling capacitor mod — Consensus-Protocol/cmp170hx — Consensus-Protocol/cmp170hx 'Physical modifications' page (docs/operations/physical-mods.md, 39,791 bytes) — the procedure page the PCIe subsystem page defers to, and the primary source for the run's link-width question. Headline: the CMP 170HX trains at PCIe x4 because 12 of its 16 lanes ship with their AC-coupling capacitors depopulated from the factory; hand-soldering the 24 missing 0402 220 nF X7R capacitors restores a full x16 link with NO software patch of any kind. The mod changes LINK WIDTH ONLY and never PCIe generation: a cap-modded card with no unlocker installed reports x16 at PCIe 1.0, while an unlocker with no cap mod reports Gen2 x4. The four-state verification table is the cleanest artifact in the whole corpus — stock: LnkSta 'Speed 2.5GT/s, Width x4 (downgraded)', nvidia-smi 1,1,4; cap mod only: 'Speed 2.5GT/s, Width x16', 1,1,16; unlocker only: 'Speed 5GT/s, Width x4 (downgraded)', 2,2,4; cap mod + unlocker: 'Speed 5GT/s, Width x16', 2,2,16. The '(downgraded)' suffix disappearing from the width field once all 24 parts are in place is the single clearest before/after signal; '(overdriven)' on the speed field is normal and not an error.

Procedure and risk. Parts: 24 x 0402, 220 nF, X7R, 6.3 V or higher; confirmed part Taiyo Yuden MAASJ105SB7224KFCA01 (AEC-Q200), value read off the NVIDIA A100 GA100-883 reference schematic P1001-B02 page 3. Tooling: fine-point iron at ~380 C, wick away all factory lead-free solder first, gel flux, tin one pad, tack the part, flow the other side; hot air with a correctly sized nozzle is faster for batches; Kapton-tape the surroundings; no preheat. Time: ~20 minutes per card by hand for an experienced modder, medium confidence. Difficulty: routine 0402 rework on labelled, uncramped pads on the back of the board beside the gold fingers — no BGA, no reball, no stencil, no firmware step; experienced modders call it 'probably the easiest card to do PCIE mod'. The dominant beginner failure mode is preheating the whole board (an IR stove plus hot air), whose documented consequence is a bent PCB, broken internal traces and cooked ICs. An imperfect result is non-destructive: it under-trains the link rather than damaging the card, and the card keeps working at whatever width it negotiated while the joints are fixed — no attempt limit, no state to reset. Population-to-width mapping: 12 of 24 correctly populated trains x8; 12-23 of 24, or any bridged/cold joints, trains x8; 24 of 24 trains x16. An x8 result means incomplete or bridged solder work, not a distinct hardware limit; the remedy is mechanical — reflow and inspect all 24 joints under magnification for tombstoned parts, solder bridges between the two pads of a pair, and joints that merely rest on the pad without wetting it.
