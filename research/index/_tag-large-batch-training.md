# Tag: large-batch-training

**3** notes.

- [[accurate-large-minibatch-sgd]] — Accurate, Large Minibatch SGD: Training ImageNet in 1 Hour — Goyal et al. (Facebook AI, arXiv 1706.02677, 2017) - the canonical large-minibatch recipe. Shows that on ImageNet large minibatches cause optimization difficulty rather than poor generalization, and that once addressed there is no loss of accuracy up to minibatch 8,192. The two ingredients are a hyperparameter-free linear learning-rate scaling rule (LR proportional to minibatch size) and a gradual warmup over the first epochs that overcomes early optimization problems. Result: ResNet-50 at minibatch 8,192 on 256 GPUs in one hour, matching small-minibatch accuracy, with about 90% scaling efficiency from 8 to 256 GPUs; the rule also transfers to object detection and instance segmentation via Mask R-CNN. This is the mechanism the repo relies on when it says large global batches are the only lever, and it also states the precondition the repo omits: warmup is mandatory, and the rule is derived for SGD with momentum on vision, not for Adam on LLM pretraining (Merrill et al. 2025 argue Adam needs square-root scaling instead). `review`
- [[an-empirical-model-of-large-batch-training]] — An Empirical Model of Large-Batch Training — McCandlish, Kaplan, Amodei and the OpenAI Dota Team (arXiv 1812.06162, 2018) - introduces the gradient noise scale (the signal-to-noise ratio of the gradient across training examples) as a predictor of the largest useful batch size. Theory: training parallelizes almost linearly up to a batch size equal to the noise scale, then switches smoothly but rapidly to a regime where further parallelism buys almost nothing; the noise scale rises as loss falls, is larger for harder tasks, and depends on model size mainly through improved model performance rather than size per se. Verified across 8 tasks spanning supervised learning (MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, ImageNet, Billion Word), RL (Atari, Dota) and generative autoencoders on SVHN, with a compute-efficiency versus time-efficiency tradeoff model and an analysis of dynamically varying batch size. This is the framework the repo's large-global-batches rule implicitly assumes, and the paper Merrill et al. (2025) test and reject as a proxy under Adam; its evidence base is small-scale and pre-LLM. `review`
- [[critical-batch-size-revisited-a-simple-empirical]] — Critical Batch Size Revisited: A Simple Empirical Approach to Large-Batch Language Model Training — Merrill, Arora, Groeneveld and Hajishirzi (Allen Institute for AI, NeurIPS 2025; assigned DOI 10.52202/085713-3898, read via arXiv 2505.23971v3) - measures the critical batch size (CBS) directly by branched training from checkpoints instead of via the gradient-noise-scale proxy, which they show rests on two assumptions LLM pretraining violates (SGD optimizer, well-conditioned Hessian) and find unreliable under Adam. On OLMo 1B and 7B with 4,096-token documents, CBS starts near 0 at initialization, rises rapidly and plateaus around 4,096 documents (~16.8M tokens); the trend is the same at 1B and 7B, so CBS measured on small runs transfers upward. They turn this into batch-size warmup: CBS reaches 2,048 documents (~8.4M tokens) by 168B tokens and 4,096 (~16.8M tokens) by 503B tokens, doubling the batch at each point. OLMo 1B trained this way beats the original run by 0.0166 loss after pretraining (0.0053 after mid-training) with 43% fewer gradient steps, while a fixed 4,096-document batch from step 0 uses 75% fewer steps but ends with worse loss than the small-batch control. Scaling rule used is square-root, not linear, because Adam. Implication for this run: a 1M-token global batch is roughly 17x below the measured CBS at plateau, so token efficiency is safe late in training, but CBS is near 0 at initialization - starting at 1M tokens without batch-size warmup or LR compensation is the real risk, and the repo's plan does not mention warmup. `review`
