# Tag: small-scale

- [[factorengram-factorized-n-gram-memory-with-basis-level]] — FactorEngram: Factorized N-gram Memory with Basis-Level — FactorEngram (Baidu, arXiv 2609.35578) - replaces Engram's monolithic per-n-gram embedding with sparsity-regularized coefficients over a dictionary of basis vectors shared across patterns, and reuses the same dictionary for basis-level contextual gating (the hidden state is scored against each basis vector to gate its coefficient before reconstruction). Also covers unigram as well as multi-token n-grams, and sweeps memory-branch insertion locations. Scale is small: 340M and 1B Transformer backbones trained from scratch on 30B and 120B tokens of FineWeb-Edu at 8K context with a 32K vocab; the 340M config carries 1B lookup-table parameters (bigram and trigram capacities 250K each) and the 1B config carries 2B lookup-table parameters (250K bigram / 750K trigram). Gains: at 340M WikiText perplexity 23.19 -> 21.29, LAMBADA 24.90 -> 21.69, +1.76 pp average downstream accuracy over the Transformer, and it beats a reproduction of Engram on all metrics, with the largest gains on 8K Needle-in-a-Haystack. Best insertion point is before attention in middle layers (10 and 12 in the reported config); sparsity coefficient lambda = 1e-3. Relevant as evidence that the Engram memory-table design space is still being actively refactored at sub-1B scale, and that table capacity can be traded against dictionary factorization rather than raw row count. `review`
- [[smollm2-when-smol-goes-big]] — SmolLM2: When Smol Goes Big — — SmolLM2 (Hugging Face, arXiv 2502.02737) is the reference recipe for training a ~1.7B model from scratch on a fixed compute budget, and the primary source for the FineMath / Stack-Edu / SmolTalk datasets. Base model: 1.7B params, Llama-2 architecture, 49,152-token tokenizer, ~11T tokens over four stages on 256 H100s with the nanotron framework; total cost stated as ~1e23 FLOPs, i.e. ~$250,000 of GPU compute — cited as the reason the authors rebalance mixtures online instead of running multiple from-scratch runs. Stage 1 (0-6T tokens) is 60% FineWeb-Edu / 40% DCLM plus 10% StarCoder-Data; later stages add OWM/InfiMM-WebMath/FineMath and Stack-Edu at 14-24% code and 4-10% math. Ablation models are 1.7B params, sequence length 2048, global batch ~2M tokens, LR 3e-4 cosine, 350B tokens per ablation.

FineMath: up to 54B tokens of math from Common Crawl, built by re-extracting 7.7B URLs (5.7B classifier-identified + 0.6B OWM + 1.3B InfiMM-WebMath) with the OWM pipeline to preserve LaTeX, then scoring with a Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct-annotated classifier on a 5-point scale. FineMath4+ = 10B tokens / 6.7M docs; FineMath3+ = 34B tokens / 21.4M docs. Reported: FineMath4+ gives a 2x GSM8K and 6x MATH improvement over InfiMM-WebMath; Infi-WebMath4+ plateaus after ~80B tokens (~10 epochs) from repetition, a plateau not seen in FineMath4+.

Stack-Edu: filtered variant of StarCoder2Data restricted to the 15 largest languages (~450B-token subset); 15 language-specific StarEncoder classifiers trained on Llama3-70B-Instruct synthetic annotations (0-5 educational scale, 500k samples each, F1 > 0.7 at threshold 3). Result: ~125B tokens across 15 languages; Python 50.6B -> 21.8B with HumanEval 20.7 -> 25.6, C++ 69.7B -> 16.0B with MultiPL-E 16.7 -> 24.8.

Context extension: 2k -> 8k by continuing from a stage-4 checkpoint with RoPE base 130k and a mixture of 40% long documents (>=8k tokens) from DCLM (10%), FineWeb-Edu (10%) and Dolma books (20%). Relevance to the CMP 170HX run: it establishes that a 1.7B-class model at 2k context with a ~2M-token global batch is a normal, published ablation configuration — the scale this box can plausibly reach — and gives concrete token counts for the three datasets in the repo's data pipeline. `review`
- [[the-fineweb-datasets-decanting-the-web-for-the]] — The FineWeb Datasets: Decanting the Web for the — The FineWeb Datasets paper (Penedo et al., NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks; arXiv 2406.17557) is the primary source behind the repo's web-data assumptions. FineWeb is ~15T GPT-2 tokens of Common Crawl (36T tokens before filtering), built with datatrove; FineWeb-Edu is the 1.3T-token educational subset. The method is a chain of data ablation experiments: identical 1.71B-parameter Llama-architecture models, sequence length 2048, global batch ~2M tokens, GPT-2 tokenizer, trained with nanotron on ~28B tokens for filtering ablations (roughly Chinchilla-optimal for that size) and 350B tokens for dedup / cumulative ablations. Two models per data version with different subsets and seeds, averaged. Over 70 models, ~80,000 H100 GPU hours total.

Pipeline findings with numbers: trafilatura extraction from WARC beats WET text; fastText English filter at score >=0.65; MinHash fuzzy dedup with 112 hash functions, 8-hash buckets, 14x8 bands, union-find over connected components; heuristic quality filters tuned by taking the lower tail of each metric's histogram (16 candidate metric-threshold pairs). FineWeb-Edu: Llama3-70B-Instruct scored 500k CC-MAIN-2024-10 pages on a 0-5 additive scale; a BERT-like regression classifier (Snowflake-arctic-embed-m base) fine-tuned on those annotations reached F1 82% at threshold 3; applying it to the 15T-token FineWeb took 6,000 H100 GPU hours; threshold 3 removed 92% of the data leaving 1.3T tokens, threshold 2 leaves 5.4T.

Why it matters here: it fixes the shape of a defensible data-ablation experiment — 1.7B params, 2k context, ~2M-token global batch, 28B tokens — which is almost exactly the scale a 5x CMP 170HX box can reach, and it quantifies the accelerator cost of the curation step separately from the training step. It also documents that the ablation protocol (two seeds, equal token counts, lighteval benchmarks chosen for low variance) is what makes small-scale data comparisons trustworthy. `review`
- [[training-compute-optimal-large-language-models]] — Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models — Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Hoffmann et al., DeepMind, arXiv 2203.15556) is the primary behind the '20 tokens per parameter' rule that FineWeb and SmolLM2 both use to size their ablations. The paper fits three independent approaches — minimum over training curves, IsoFLOP profiles, and parametric loss modelling — on models from 70M to over 16B parameters trained on 5B to over 400B tokens, and finds that model size and training tokens should be scaled in approximately equal proportions as compute grows: exponents a = 0.50 / b = 0.50 (approach 1), 0.49 / 0.51 (approach 2), 0.46 / 0.54 (approach 3), versus Kaplan et al.'s 0.73 / 0.27.

The practical frontier (Table 3, approach 1) gives exact FLOPs and token counts: 400M params -> 1.92e19 FLOPs / 8.0B tokens; 1B -> 1.21e20 FLOPs / 20.2B tokens; 10B -> 1.23e22 FLOPs / 205.1B tokens; 67B -> 5.76e23 FLOPs / 1.5T tokens; 175B -> 3.85e24 / 3.7T; 1T -> 1.27e26 / 21.2T. The FLOPs constraint used throughout is FLOPs(N,D) = 6ND.

Headline result: Chinchilla (70B params, 1.4T tokens) trained on the same compute budget as Gopher (280B) uniformly and significantly outperforms Gopher, GPT-3 (175B) and Jurassic-1 (178B), reaching 67.5% on MMLU, a >7% improvement over Gopher. The paper's central claim is that the models of its era were considerably over-sized relative to their compute budgets.

Why it matters here: 6ND plus the Table 3 frontier converts the box's ~810 TFLOPS bf16 into a concrete answer to 'how big a model and how many tokens can this run afford' — a 1B model at 20.2B tokens costs 1.21e20 FLOPs, a multi-day-to-multi-week run at realistic MFU, while a 10B model at 205B tokens (1.23e22 FLOPs) is out of reach. It also justifies the repo's choice of a ~1B-trainable model rather than a larger one at fixed compute. `review`
