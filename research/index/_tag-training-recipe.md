- [[deepseek-v32-pushing-the-frontier-of-open]] — DeepSeek-V3.2: Pushing the Frontier of Open — DeepSeek-V3.2 technical report (arXiv 2512.02556) — the origin of DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) and therefore the primary source for the indexer training recipe the repo infers. The lightning indexer computes an index score between each query token and the latent KV entries using a small number of indexer heads and a ReLU activation (chosen for throughput rather than softmax), and a fine-grained token-selection mechanism retrieves only the top-k KV entries, reducing core attention from O(L^2) to O(Lk) while the indexer itself retains a complexity that grows with sequence length.

The two-stage training recipe is stated with exact numbers. Dense warm-up: keep dense attention and freeze all model parameters except the lightning indexer; build a target distribution by summing the main attention scores across all heads and L1-normalizing along the sequence dimension; minimize a KL divergence between that target and the softmax of the indexer scores; LR 1e-3; 1000 steps of 16 sequences of 128K tokens = 2.1B tokens. Sparse training: introduce fine-grained token selection and optimize all parameters; keep the KL alignment but renormalized over only the selected token set S_t; detach the indexer input from the computational graph so the indexer's training signal comes only from its own KL loss while the main model is optimized only on the language-modeling loss; LR 7.3e-6; select 2048 KV tokens per query; 15000 steps of 480 sequences of 128K tokens = 943.7B tokens.

Two facts matter most for the repo's CSA2-at-32k claim: (1) the DSA lineage trains at 128K sequence length, not 32k — both the 2.1B-token indexer warm-up and the 943.7B-token sparse stage use 128K sequences; (2) the indexer and backbone are trained as two separate objectives via a detached graph, which is the concrete mechanism behind the repo's "extra loss term, not a drop-in" claim. The report also covers the 128K long-context extension and the post-training recipe (specialist distillation plus mixed RL). 9,409 words. `review`
- [[deepseek-v41-flash]] — DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: — DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash technical report (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv 2609.19969v1, 17 Sep 2026): a 552B-backbone multimodal MoE with a Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED) split of 20 encoder + 20 decoder layers, activating 8B params/token in prefill and 16B in decode, pretrained on 45T tokens at a fixed 100.6M-token batch. CSA2 is the paper's central KV-compression mechanism and differs materially from the repo's description of it: it is NOT a per-layer indexer running in parallel everywhere. Each CSA2 layer is statically assigned one of three modes — Full (own main KV, own indexer Q, fresh Top-K), Reindex (borrows a preceding layer's main KV + indexer K, rescores with its own indexer Q), Reuse (borrows both KV and the latest Top-K indices, no indexer at all). In the actual 40-layer configuration only 4 layers run a Full-mode indexer (3 encoder + 1 decoder), 4 run Reindex indexers, 27 are Reuse (zero indexer work), and the first 2 layers are pure SWA — 67.5% of layers execute no indexer at all. Hyperparameters: attention top-k = 512 KV entries per query; indexer 32 query heads, head dim 128; main attention 64 heads, head dim 512, query compression dim 1280; SWA window 128; compression ratio m=2 in encoder layers and m=1 in decoder layers; Hierarchical Sparse Indexer selects at most 2,048 blocks of 8 positions = 16,384 candidate positions. The hierarchical indexer is "used only in the decoder of CED to reduce this repeated scoring during decode" and is "introduced in post-training", with the candidate restriction applied identically during training and inference so deeper indexers are optimized under the same search domain they use at inference; the first Full-mode layer still scans the entire causally visible range. Result: 890 bytes/token global KV cache (~1/4 of V4-Flash) and nearly flat decode FLOPs (256-fold context growth 4K→1M raises decode FLOPs only 1/4).

Training-side facts that bear directly on the repo's CSA2-at-32k claim: (1) sparse attention is trained FROM SCRATCH at sequence length 64K with NO dense-attention warm-up stage, then extended to 1M at 34T tokens — so 32k is below the paper's trained regime, not a natural operating point; (2) FP4 global KV caches are used DURING TRAINING with only marginal performance degradation (QAT extended from the FP4 indexer Q/K to the main KV cache, MXFP4/NVFP4-style block scaling); (3) distributed CSA2 training needs extra machinery the repo does not budget for — "shadow indexers" (a lightweight executable replica per pipeline stage with a single logical owner, kept consistent by parameter sync and gradient aggregation), pipeline payload extensions carrying intermediate representations and sparse routing information across stage boundaries, and micro-batch-level shared-state management coordinating state lifetimes across forward, activation recomputation and backward; (4) Engram tables are partitioned by row across engram-parallel process groups, optimizer state is sharded across replicas, embeddings are stored/fetched in FP8, prefetch is initiated for the whole local batch before each pipeline stage, and embedding gradients are buffered during backward and returned to owning ranks after the backbone backward — Engram LR is scaled 5x. Optimizer mix: Muon for linear transforms, AdamW for RMSNorm/non-matrix params, Sinkhorn-balanced update for embeddings and prediction head. Reuse-mode layers execute with only 15 kernels during prefill and 11 during decode. 24,845 words — long source, flag for source-analyst. `review`
- [[deepseek-v4]] — DeepSeek-V4: — DeepSeek-V4 technical report (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv 2606.19348) — the CSA/HCA predecessor that CSA2 in V4.1-Flash simplifies. V4-Flash is 43 layers at d=4096 with pure SWA in the first two layers and CSA and HCA interleaved thereafter; CSA uses compression rate m=4, 64 indexer query heads, indexer head dimension 128, and attention top-k of 512 (V4-Flash) or 1024 (V4-Pro); HCA uses m'=128. Under this configuration V4-Flash is 284B total parameters.

The training recipe is the load-bearing part for the CSA2-at-32k question. Training starts at a 4K sequence length and is gradually extended to 16K, 64K and 1M. Critically, V4 warms up with DENSE attention for the first 1T tokens and only introduces sparse attention at a sequence length of 64K — the direct opposite of V4.1-Flash's "no dense attention warmup stages" claim, and in both generations sparse attention is introduced at 64K, never at 32k. When sparsity is introduced, V4 first runs a short stage to warm up the lightning indexer in CSA and then trains with sparse attention for most of the run. Batch size is raised to 75.5M tokens and held there; LR is warmed up over 2000 steps, held at 2.7e-4, then cosine-decayed to 2.7e-5. Sample-level attention masking is used during pretraining.

Precision and determinism machinery: KV entries use a mixed storage format (BF16 for RoPE dimensions, FP8 for the rest) which nearly halves KV cache size versus pure BF16; lightning-indexer attention is computed in FP4 precision; FP4 QAT is applied during post-training to MoE expert weights and the indexer QK path. Sparse-attention backward uses atomicAdd to accumulate KV gradients, which makes training non-deterministic, so V4 allocates per-SM accumulation buffers and performs a deterministic global summation across them. Optimizer is Muon for the majority of parameters with AdamW for the embedding module.

Also relevant: V4 explicitly notes that relative to DeepSeek-V3.2 a SMALLER attention top-k is chosen in the V4 series to improve efficiency on short- and medium-length texts — i.e. the top-k budget was tuned down for shorter contexts, which undercuts the repo's framing that the budget is "tuned for 1M context" and therefore mismatched to 32k. Sparse-attention kernels are co-designed with the KV cache layout (padding blocks to align with hardware), and the visible range of compressed KV entries is precomputed by rules. 26,488 words — long source, flag for source-analyst. `review`
