Each CSA2 layer is statically assigned one of three modes — Full (own main KV, own indexer Q, fresh Top-K), Reindex (borrows a preceding layer's main KV + indexer K, rescores with its own indexer Q), Reuse (borrows both KV and the latest Top-K indices, no indexer at all). Outer level: KV Bridging adopts a YOCO-style self-decoder/cross-decoder structure but bridges only full-attention layers — the KV caches for cross-decoder full-attention layers are generated from the hidden states of self-decoder full-attention layers, so prefill can exit after the self-decoder and skip all cross-decoder layers. Inner level: KV Reuse (HySparse's core design) with two refinements — block-level sparsity replaced by token-level sparsity for finer long-context retrieval, and the separate SWA branch removed in favour of forcing a sliding window of recent tokens into the sparse selection. After pretraining HySparse2 retains broadly comparable general capabilities and improves long-context retrieval; after light post-training it improves mean MRCR-style retrieval and agentic multi-turn performance.

Cost results: at 1M tokens HySparse2 reduces prefill FLOPs by 2.92x relative to HySparse and 5.02x relative to Hybrid SWA, with KV cache of only 2.69 GB versus 6.72 GB (HySparse) and 12.09 GB (Hybrid SWA) under FP8 KV storage. - [[indexcache-accelerating-sparse-attention-via-cross-layer]] — IndexCache: Accelerating Sparse Attention via Cross-Layer — IndexCache (Bai, Dong, Jiang, Lv, Du, Zeng, Tang, Li — Tsinghua + Z.ai, arXiv 2603.12201) exploits cross-layer redundancy in DeepSeek Sparse Attention: the lightning indexer retains O(L^2) complexity and must run independently at every layer even though the resulting top-k selections are highly similar across consecutive layers. IndexCache partitions layers into a small set of Full layers that run their own indexers and a majority of Shared layers that simply reuse the nearest Full layer's top-k indices. Two variants: training-free IndexCache (greedy search over which layers keep indexers, minimizing LM loss on a calibration set, no weight updates) and training-aware IndexCache (a multi-layer distillation loss training each retained indexer against the averaged attention distributions of all layers it serves; Proposition 1 shows gradient equivalence to distillation against the averaged distribution, so even simple interleaved patterns match full-indexer accuracy).

Results: on a 30B DSA model across nine long-context and reasoning benchmarks, removing 75% of indexer computations gives negligible quality degradation, with up to 1.82x prefill speedup and 1.48x decode speedup at 200K context (decode gains because DSA runs a per-token indexer pass every step). Preliminary results on production-scale GLM-5: 50% indexer removal with comparable long-context/reasoning performance and ~1.2x end-to-end speedup. The paper also documents the standard DSA training recipe: a short dense warm-up trains ONLY the indexer via KL-divergence distillation against the full-attention distribution, after which sparse training proceeds with the indexer receiving distillation gradients on a detached computational graph. 8,771 words. `review`
- [[lisa-linear-indexed-sparse-attention-for-efficient]] — LISA: Linear-Indexed Sparse Attention for Efficient — LISA (Zhao et al., Alibaba International Digital Commerce + Dalian University of Technology + Amazon, arXiv 2607.19358) is a plug-and-play attention replacement that requires NO pretraining from scratch: a Linear Attention branch (O(n) time, long-range memory) runs in parallel with a Lightning Indexer that selects the top-M tokens for a Sparse Self-Attention, the two branches fused by a gating mechanism, reducing inference complexity from O(n^2) to O(nM) for generating n tokens.

Two-stage training: Stage 1 cold-starts the linear attention plus a sliding-window attention mechanism, optimized by knowledge distillation to approximate the full self-attention distribution of a frozen teacher; Stage 2 introduces the Indexer to replace the static sliding window, trained with a per-head KL divergence loss aligning its selection behaviour with the teacher's attention patterns. All original model parameters are frozen — only the newly added linear-attention and Indexer modules train. Because top-M is argmax, gradients cannot flow back through the selection; early in training they select top-1.2M candidates and randomly mask out 20%, gradually reducing the dropout ratio, and apply z-loss to the Indexer.

Budget analysis: MASS (attention mass captured) reaches 80% at M=256, only 6% below the theoretical oracle upper bound; 70% at M=128 and 88% at M=512, so M=256 is the recommended balance. Every compressed variant is phase-sensitive with period equal to its stride; full-attention baselines are flat (Prefix-Padding gaps up to 78pp for compressed vs at most 6.1pp for baselines). Mechanistic work (KV-head knockouts, attention-readout mean replacement) shows phase specialization — different heads/layers serve different phases — and an idealized induction task proves gradient flow drives compression gates to sharp, persistent phase preferences. Prescription: evaluate compressed-attention models by shifting inputs a few tokens, not by average accuracy.
