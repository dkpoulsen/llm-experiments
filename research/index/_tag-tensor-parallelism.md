- [[deepseek-v3-technical-report]] — DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report — DeepSeek-V3 technical report (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv 2412.19437, Dec 2024) — the canonical primary source for what expert parallelism actually costs and what it takes to hide that cost. Model: 671B total / 37B active MoE with MLA and DeepSeekMoE, 8 routed experts plus 1 shared, trained on 14.8T tokens on 2048 H800 GPUs for 2.664M GPU hours (~180K H800 GPU-hours per trillion tokens; 2.788M GPU hours / $5.576M total). The load-bearing admission for this run: 'the communication overhead introduced by cross-node expert parallelism results in an inefficient computation-to-communication ratio of approximately 1:1' — i.e. on their fabric, EP all-to-all cost roughly as much time as the compute it fed, before overlap. Their fix is DualPipe, which splits each chunk into attention / all-to-all dispatch / MLP / all-to-all combine and rearranges forward and backward chunks so that both all-to-all and PP communication are fully hidden, reaching 'near-zero all-to-all communication overhead' as long as the compute-to-communication ratio is held constant. That overlap is built on a two-tier fabric: intra-node NVLink at 160 GB/s versus inter-node IB at 50 GB/s (3.2x), with each token dispatched to at most 4 nodes (averaging 3.2 experts per node, so up to 13 experts at the same communication cost) and only 20 SMs dedicated to saturating both links. Training topology: EP32 with no tensor parallelism at all — they explicitly say the memory work 'enabl[es] us to train DeepSeek-V3 without using costly Tensor Parallelism (TP)'. Inference uses TP4+DP8+EP32 for prefill and EP320 for decode. Relevance: this is the strongest published evidence that (a) EP all-to-all is a first-order cost even on NVLink+IB, (b) TP was avoided for training by the lab with the most reason to use it, and (c) the standard mitigation (overlap) presupposes a fast intra-node tier and a separate scale-out network — neither of which exists on a flat Gen2 x4 no-P2P box. `review`

Topology: a single TP8/EP1 instance — explicitly 'no routed-expert A2A' — with a 1,048,576-token logical KV pool. Relevance to this run: it is the closest published datapoint for running a 284B CSA-family MoE across eight 64 GB cards with tensor parallelism and zero expert all-to-all — i.e. TP8 + EP1 is the topology a serving stack chose when it wanted to avoid routed-expert A2A entirely. - [[scalable-training-of-mixture-of-experts-models-with]] — Scalable Training of Mixture-of-Experts Models with — Scalable Training of MoE Models with Megatron Core (NVIDIA technical report, arXiv 2603.07685, Mar 2026; 39k words): the production reference for how EP/TP/PP/DP compose for MoE training, and the source of the arithmetic this run needs for the EP question. It frames MoE training as three coupled walls. The Communication Wall: EP's per-GPU send volume in one all-to-all is approximately T*K*h*(EP-1)/EP (T local tokens, K top-k, h hidden dim), and a dispatch+combine cycle doubles it; as EP grows the volume saturates but traffic migrates from NVLink to narrower inter-node links where bandwidth drops by an order of magnitude, and unoptimized all-to-all can consume up to 60% of total training time in DeepSeek-V3-like models. The Memory Wall: all E experts' parameters, gradients and optimizer states must be resident even though only K activate, and offloading to host memory costs PCIe bandwidth. The Compute Efficiency Wall: TP on MoE experts is actively harmful because expert hidden dimensions are already small, so TP shards them into even smaller GEMMs while adding communication; GEMMs are ~70% of execution time in dense Llama-3 405B but under 50% in DeepSeek-V3, with routing/permutation adding ~9%. DeepSeek-V3 illustrates the parameter-compute mismatch: 685B total vs 37B active per token (18x). Dispatcher backends are AllGather, all-to-all (NCCL), and Flex (DeepEP/HybridEP, which assume NVLink-rich topologies). Reference throughput on GB300/GB200: 1,233/1,048 TFLOPS/GPU for DeepSeek-V3-685B, 974/919 for Qwen3-235B.
