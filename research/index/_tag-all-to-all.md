Model: 671B total / 37B active MoE with MLA and DeepSeekMoE, 8 routed experts plus 1 shared, trained on 14.8T tokens on 2048 H800 GPUs for 2.664M GPU hours (~180K H800 GPU-hours per trillion tokens; 2.788M GPU hours / $5.576M total). The load-bearing admission for this run: 'the communication overhead introduced by cross-node expert parallelism results in an inefficient computation-to-communication ratio of approximately 1:1' — i.e. on their fabric, EP all-to-all cost roughly as much time as the compute it fed, before overlap. Their fix is DualPipe, which splits each chunk into attention / all-to-all dispatch / MLP / all-to-all combine and rearranges forward and backward chunks so that both all-to-all and PP communication are fully hidden, reaching 'near-zero all-to-all communication overhead' as long as the compute-to-communication ratio is held constant. Inference uses TP4+DP8+EP32 for prefill and EP320 for decode. - [[zipccl-efficient-lossless-data-compression-of]] — ZipCCL: Efficient Lossless Data Compression of — ZipCCL (Lin, Pan, Fan, Shi, Chu; arXiv 2604.27844) is a lossless compression layer for NCCL collectives in LLM training. Core observation: LLM training tensors (activations, gradients, parameters) are near-Gaussian, so BF16 exponents are highly concentrated - the top-7 most frequent exponents cover >97% of the data. ZipCCL encodes each BF16 exponent in 3 bits (7 codes plus a zero-point escape flag) instead of 8, leaving sign/mantissa untouched, for a theoretical compression ratio up to 11 bits from 16 (~1.45x). It adds GPU-optimised compress/decompress kernels with communication-aware data layout and an adaptive switcher driven by a cost model over measured bandwidth, latency and compression ratio.

Evaluation: 64-GPU cluster of 8x RTX A6000 per node over a 200 Gb/s network, integrated into Megatron-LM (MoE all-to-all) and TorchTitan (FSDP all-gather/reduce-scatter), plus a 16xH800 400 Gb/s cluster. Communication speedups over NCCL: 1.25x on DeepSeek-V3 all-to-all, 1.24x on Qwen3-MoE, 1.35x on Llama3-8B all-gather; averaged across message sizes 1.36x for all-to-all and 1.31x for all-gather. End-to-end training speedups: 1.16x (DeepSeek-V3), 1.16x (Qwen3-MoE), 1.18x (Llama3-8B); on the 16xH800 cluster 1.13x for both Qwen3-MoE and Llama3-8B.

Two findings are load-bearing for this box. First, the benefit is bandwidth-saturation dependent: "When the data volume is small, bandwidth cannot be fully utilized, and our ZipCCL shows very close performance to NCCL. However, as the data volume increases ... and bandwidth is better saturated, the improvement ... becomes significant." On a ~1 GB/s fabric the saturation point arrives at far smaller tensors, so 1.3x is a floor rather than a ceiling. Second, Reduce-Scatter was NOT worth compressing on their platform ("the original Reduce-Scatter implementation was found to be faster"), so a DDP/FSDP gradient path does not automatically inherit the gain - all-gather did, reduce-scatter did not.

Also notable: ZipCCL beats DietGPU (Meta's GPU lossless compressor), where DietGPU-compressed all-gather was slower than plain NCCL all-gather while ZipCCL got 1.35x. The scheme is bit-exact, so unlike FP8/quantisation work it carries no convergence risk; the relevant risk on this box is compress/decompress kernel cost against 5 weak GPUs. `review`
