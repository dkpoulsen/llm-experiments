Model: 284B total / ~13B active, FP4 routed experts, mostly FP8 non-expert weights, 48 safetensors shards. Host: 8 MI250 GCDs (~64 GiB HBM each) on one Supermicro AS-4124GQ-TNMI, 1 TiB DDR4. Relevance to this run: it is the closest published datapoint for running a 284B CSA-family MoE across eight 64 GB cards with tensor parallelism and zero expert all-to-all — i.e. TP8 + EP1 is the topology a serving stack chose when it wanted to avoid routed-expert A2A entirely. - [[democratizing-moe-inference-on-commodity-gpus]] — Democratizing MoE inference on commodity GPUs — CoMoE (Fan et al., Tsinghua + Alibaba Cloud, arXiv 2610.09424): host-centric MoE inference on commodity GPUs. Its measurement section is the cleanest quantification of how much a no-P2P PCIe fabric costs MoE: on 8x RTX 5090 (PCIe 5.0 x16, 64 GB/s nominal per GPU vs 400 GB/s NVLink = 0.16x), inter-GPU communication accounts for 81.6% of total MoE execution time, and naive deployment reaches only 61% of the throughput of an FLOP-matched datacenter GPU. CoMoE elevates host DRAM from a passive bounce buffer to an active routing hub — host-backed token multicast for dispatch (each shared token written once, receivers pull in parallel) and token-level host-staged aggregation for combine (replacing the global All-to-All barrier with a completion-driven pipeline that absorbs stragglers). Results: 1.24-1.41x throughput per model (up to 1.46x), 85-90% of an 8x A800 + DeepEP configuration at 23.4% of the hardware cost, TTFT -18.8% and TPOT -26.9% on average, combine latency 2.46x geometric-mean faster than NCCL All-to-All. Ablations show the win comes from decomposing All-to-All into one-sided transfers (1.55x) plus NUMA-aware buffers, thread-tile pipelining and batched commit (to 2.46x). Scope: inference, not training; 8 GPUs on Gen5 x16 — a far faster link than Gen2 x4. `review`
- [[efficient-expert-parallel-communication]] — Efficient Expert-Parallel Communication — ThunderEP (Lee et al., Seoul National University, arXiv 2609.40093): an EP communication design for MoE inference on PCIe consumer GPUs with no P2P. Establishes the fabric mechanics the CMP 170HX box shares: without P2P every inter-GPU byte is host-staged and crosses PCIe twice (sender writes to pinned host buffer, receiver reads it back), so NCCL's ring All-Gather moves 2(N-1)S bytes per GPU versus N*S for a single-step host broadcast — a ratio of 2(N-1)/N, i.e. 1.6x at N=5. Measured PCIe contention on 6x RTX 5090 (Gen5 x16): one GPU alone reaches 56.5 GB/s, but two GPUs under the same host bridge communicating concurrently drop to 35.5 GB/s each, so per-GPU bandwidth falls as more GPUs join a collective. ThunderEP (single-step host broadcast, DMA-engine transfers, per-sender completion flags) beats NCCL by 2.00x on dispatch and 1.53x on combine, 1.66x end-to-end prefill and 1.26x decode; ablation TTFT 2115.9 ms -> 1436.4 ms. Scope limits: inference only (no gradient collectives), and every existing EP library (DeepEP, pplx-kernels, NCCL EP, UCCL-EP, NIXL) assumes NVLink, GPUDirect P2P or RDMA and therefore cannot run on this fabric at all. `review`
- [[variopath-workload-aware-all-to-all-communication-for-pcie-gpu-clusters]] — VarioPath: Workload-Aware All-to-All Communication for PCIe GPU Clusters — VarioPath (Fei et al., USTC + Tsinghua, arXiv 2609.34340): workload-aware AlltoAllv scheduling for PCIe GPU clusters — the primitive that EP dispatch/combine and sequence parallelism are built from. It documents why PCIe all-to-all is hard: no dedicated scale-up fabric means intra-node GPU traffic and NIC traffic share the same PCIe hierarchy, so concurrent transfers contend on bottleneck links; demand is skewed (top-k routing) and changes every layer (sub-millisecond per layer), defeating static schedules. VarioPath splits an offline topology-aware analyzer (records contention-free transfer patterns as channels, exploits topology symmetry) from an online demand-aware scheduler. On four platforms up to 256 GPUs it averages 5.88x over FAST and 1.72x over DeepEP (1.55-2.00x geomean over DeepEP on random-demand AlltoAllv, 3.73-10.61x over NCCL), reaching 30.91 GB/s algorithmic bandwidth at 256 GPUs vs 28.11 (DeepEP) and 18.85 (NCCL). End-to-end: Qwen3-30B-A3B prefill latency -19.1% to -27.2% at EP16/24/32, Wan2.1 generation -1.1% to -6.6%. Ablation: fixing either peer matching or path choice roughly doubles completion time (2.03x / 2.00x), fixing both 2.84x — peer-path co-design is not optional. Testbeds are Gen4/Gen5 x16 with 32-64 GB/s per GPU, so the absolute GB/s figures do not transfer to a Gen2 x4 box; the structural findings do. `review`
