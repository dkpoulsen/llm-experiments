- [[analysing-the-impact-of-sequence-composition]] — Analysing The Impact of Sequence Composition — Zhao et al., 'Analysing The Impact of Sequence Composition on Language Model Pre-Training' (arXiv 2402.13991; ACL 2024 long paper) — the primary source behind the ACL landing-page note. Setup: LLaMA-architecture 1.3B-parameter models pretrained on 150B tokens sampled from SlimPajama (7 sub-corpora, token proportions preserved), at context windows of 2,048 (2K) and 8,192 (8K). Four sequence-construction / masking variants: MIXChunk (baseline random concatenation), UNIChunk (same-source documents only), BM25Chunk (retrieval-based: for each document, BM25-select related documents from the corpus to fill the chunk), and INTRADoc (intra-document causal masking).

Results: BM25Chunk has the lowest PPL of the three causal-masking variants — -0.62 at 2K and -0.78 at 8K vs MIXChunk; UNIChunk also beats MIXChunk (-0.34 / -0.21). INTRADoc has the lowest PPL of all. GitHub is the most sensitive domain: INTRADoc improves over UNIChunk by -1.3 PPL at 2K and -2.0 at 8K, suggesting code pretraining is disproportionately harmed by unrelated preceding context. Downstream: BM25Chunk improves in-context learning +11.6%, knowledge memorisation +9.8%, context utilisation +7.2%.

Cost: intra-document causal masking costs 4.0% training-efficiency degradation in their implementation, implemented with FlashAttention-2; the authors state they do not fully solve this efficiency issue. This is the number to price against any packing / masking change on the CMP 170HX box: a 4% throughput tax buys double-digit downstream gains, a favourable trade at small scale. `review`

- [[arxiv230516264v5-cscl-28-jun-2025]] — Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models (arXiv 2305.16264v5) — Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models (Muennighoff et al., arXiv 2305.16264v5, revised 28 Jun 2025) is the primary behind the '4-5 epoch' repetition threshold that SmolLM2 cites when sizing its data mixture. Setup: more than 400 models from 10M to 9B parameters, trained for up to 1500 epochs, with budgets ranging up to 900B training tokens; the headline experiment uses a 4.2B-parameter model. All models and datasets are released as huggingface/datablations.

Core findings: (1) with constrained data at a fixed compute budget, training with up to 4 epochs of repeated data yields negligible loss change versus unique data, and differences in downstream task performance are insignificant up to around 4 epochs (25% of budget) before dropping; (2) beyond that, the value of additional compute decays to zero — the fitted data-constrained scaling law replaces the raw token count D with an effective-token count D' = U_D + U_D*R_D*(1 - e^(-R_D/R*_D)), where R*_D is the 'half-life' of repeated data; (3) in the data-constrained regime, compute should be allocated to both more parameters and more epochs, with epochs scaled slightly faster than Chinchilla would predict.

Mitigations: mixing Python code into natural-language training provides a 2x increase in effective tokens even when evaluated only on natural-language tasks, and filling up to 50% of the data with code (42B tokens) shows no deterioration. On filtering, perplexity filtering is effective but deduplication does not help on their (clean) benchmark; they recommend reserving filtering for noisy datasets.

Why it matters here: it bounds how far a 9B-token mix can be stretched on a fixed box. If the unique-token pool is ~9B, roughly 36B tokens of training are available at the 4-epoch threshold before returns collapse — the real constraint on how long a run this hardware can justify, not the FLOP budget alone. `review`
- [[smollm2-when-smol-goes-big]] — SmolLM2: When Smol Goes Big — — SmolLM2 (Hugging Face, arXiv 2502.02737) is the reference recipe for training a ~1.7B model from scratch on a fixed compute budget, and the primary source for the FineMath / Stack-Edu / SmolTalk datasets. Base model: 1.7B params, Llama-2 architecture, 49,152-token tokenizer, ~11T tokens over four stages on 256 H100s with the nanotron framework; total cost stated as ~1e23 FLOPs, i.e. ~$250,000 of GPU compute — cited as the reason the authors rebalance mixtures online instead of running multiple from-scratch runs. Stage 1 (0-6T tokens) is 60% FineWeb-Edu / 40% DCLM plus 10% StarCoder-Data; later stages add OWM/InfiMM-WebMath/FineMath and Stack-Edu at 14-24% code and 4-10% math. Ablation models are 1.7B params, sequence length 2048, global batch ~2M tokens, LR 3e-4 cosine, 350B tokens per ablation.

FineMath: up to 54B tokens of math from Common Crawl, built by re-extracting 7.7B URLs (5.7B classifier-identified + 0.6B OWM + 1.3B InfiMM-WebMath) with the OWM pipeline to preserve LaTeX, then scoring with a Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct-annotated classifier on a 5-point scale. FineMath4+ = 10B tokens / 6.7M docs; FineMath3+ = 34B tokens / 21.4M docs. Reported: FineMath4+ gives a 2x GSM8K and 6x MATH improvement over InfiMM-WebMath; Infi-WebMath4+ plateaus after ~80B tokens (~10 epochs) from repetition, a plateau not seen in FineMath4+.

Stack-Edu: filtered variant of StarCoder2Data restricted to the 15 largest languages (~450B-token subset); 15 language-specific StarEncoder classifiers trained on Llama3-70B-Instruct synthetic annotations (0-5 educational scale, 500k samples each, F1 > 0.7 at threshold 3). Result: ~125B tokens across 15 languages; Python 50.6B -> 21.8B with HumanEval 20.7 -> 25.6, C++ 69.7B -> 16.0B with MultiPL-E 16.7 -> 24.8.

Context extension: 2k -> 8k by continuing from a stage-4 checkpoint with RoPE base 130k and a mixture of 40% long documents (>=8k tokens) from DCLM (10%), FineWeb-Edu (10%) and Dolma books (20%). Relevance to the CMP 170HX run: it establishes that a 1.7B-class model at 2k context with a ~2M-token global batch is a normal, published ablation configuration — the scale this box can plausibly reach — and gives concrete token counts for the three datasets in the repo's data pipeline. `review`
