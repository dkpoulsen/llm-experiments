- [[deepseek-v3-technical-report]] — DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report — DeepSeek-V3 technical report (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv 2412.19437, Dec 2024) — the canonical primary source for what expert parallelism actually costs and what it takes to hide that cost. Model: 671B total / 37B active MoE with MLA and DeepSeekMoE, 8 routed experts plus 1 shared, trained on 14.8T tokens on 2048 H800 GPUs for 2.664M GPU hours (~180K H800 GPU-hours per trillion tokens; 2.788M GPU hours / $5.576M total). The load-bearing admission for this run: 'the communication overhead introduced by cross-node expert parallelism results in an inefficient computation-to-communication ratio of approximately 1:1' — i.e. on their fabric, EP all-to-all cost roughly as much time as the compute it fed, before overlap. Their fix is DualPipe, which splits each chunk into attention / all-to-all dispatch / MLP / all-to-all combine and rearranges forward and backward chunks so that both all-to-all and PP communication are fully hidden, reaching 'near-zero all-to-all communication overhead' as long as the compute-to-communication ratio is held constant. That overlap is built on a two-tier fabric: intra-node NVLink at 160 GB/s versus inter-node IB at 50 GB/s (3.2x), with each token dispatched to at most 4 nodes (averaging 3.2 experts per node, so up to 13 experts at the same communication cost) and only 20 SMs dedicated to saturating both links. Training topology: EP32 with no tensor parallelism at all — they explicitly say the memory work 'enabl[es] us to train DeepSeek-V3 without using costly Tensor Parallelism (TP)'. Inference uses TP4+DP8+EP32 for prefill and EP320 for decode. Relevance: this is the strongest published evidence that (a) EP all-to-all is a first-order cost even on NVLink+IB, (b) TP was avoided for training by the lab with the most reason to use it, and (c) the standard mitigation (overlap) presupposes a fast intra-node tier and a separate scale-out network — neither of which exists on a flat Gen2 x4 no-P2P box. `review`
- [[megablocks-efficient-sparse-training-with-mixture-of-experts]] — MEGABLOCKS: EFFICIENT SPARSE TRAINING WITH MIXTURE-OF-EXPERTS — MegaBlocks (Gale, Narayanan, Young, Zaharia; arXiv 2211.15841, Nov 2022): the canonical dropless MoE training system. Reformulates MoE computation as block-sparse matrix multiplication with variable-sized blocks, so no token is dropped and no expert batch is padded to a fixed capacity factor; reports up to 40% end-to-end speedup over Tutel and 2.4x over Megatron-LM dense at equal validation loss. Two facts matter for this run. First, MoE training does not require expert parallelism to be useful: the small-scale language-modeling study trains MoE-XS/Small/Medium on a SINGLE A100 80GB (batch 512 sequences, seq len 1024, 10B tokens), and only the larger comparison runs use 8x A100 with 8-way expert model parallelism plus data parallelism for non-MoE layers. Second, the padding/dropping tradeoff is quantified: capacity factor 1 loses 0.15 validation-loss reduction versus 0.26 when no tokens are dropped (1.73x larger gain), while avoiding drops more than doubles MoE-layer math; Tutel's padding cut the maximum micro-batch by 2x, 4x and 8x across the three model sizes. Scope: A100 SXM4 80GB, NVLink-class node, 2022 kernels — no PCIe-only or no-P2P measurements. `review`
- [[memory-efficient-expert-routing-for-distributed-moe]] — Memory-Efficient Expert Routing for Distributed MoE — RelayMoE (Tarafder et al., William & Mary + BSC, arXiv 2610.07333): memory-efficient expert routing for distributed MoE training. Its diagnostic contribution is the most useful part for a 64 GB-card budget: peak memory in MoE training is set by the MoE block, not attention, because every intermediate buffer in the dispatch pipeline is scaled by top-k. Profiling a Qwen3-style model (top-8, 4 layers) at micro-batch 24 / 16384 tokens on 8x H100 with TP=SP=2, CP=4, EP=8 and full recompute, the MoE input activation is only 336 MiB per GPU but the top-k-expanded dispatch tensors are ~2.6 GiB each (8x expansion) and per-layer peak exceeds 42 GB during backward, with spikes to 51 GB against a 14 GB inter-spike baseline. RelayMoE replaces the single-shot all-to-all dispatcher with a ring that circulates expert weights or tokens, computes locally at each hop, and reconstructs expert intermediates hop-by-hop during backward so they can be freed. Results on 30B-57B production MoE models: 2x average single-layer speedup over Megatron-LM, up to 2.02x full-model throughput at the same memory budget, 3.8x average (7.6x peak) reduction in MoE execution memory, and up to 2.85x longer trainable sequence. Scope caveat: the ring relies on GPU-to-GPU peer-to-peer hops over NVLink 4.0 within a node and NDR InfiniBand across nodes — the exact capability the CMP 170HX box lacks, so the mechanism does not port, but the memory arithmetic does. `review`
- [[multi-head-latentmoe-and-head-parallel]] — Multi-Head LatentMoE and Head Parallel: — Multi-Head LatentMoE and Head Parallel (Cui et al., Arizona State University, arXiv 2602.04870): an architecture + parallelism co-design that attacks the property that makes EP expensive on a slow fabric. EP's communication volume and all-to-all latency are O(k) in activated experts, traffic is load-imbalanced, and routing-dependent dispatch needs a metadata exchange to stay deterministic. Multi-Head LatentMoE splits each token into Nh sub-tokens, each with its own router and expert set, and Head Parallel distributes sub-tokens to GPUs BEFORE any routing decision, so each GPU routes and computes entirely locally. Result: O(1) communication volume regardless of k, perfectly balanced traffic, deterministic communication — and still composable with EP. At k=4 the communication volume is 25% of EP's. Trained on a 10B-token FineWeb-EDU subset on H100 80GB (NVLink): 1.11x faster at 2B total params (31.68 vs 35.36 h) and 1.61x faster at 4B (34.41 vs 55.34 h) at comparable quality, with IO-aware routing (online top-k in SRAM) and a pure-PyTorch dropless expert computation to avoid custom kernels. Relevance: this is the strongest architectural counterexample to 'expert parallelism is ruled out by the fabric' — MoE training can be made fabric-insensitive by moving the communication before routing, at the cost of a fixed per-layer all-to-all that is still host-staged on a no-P2P box. `review`
- [[technical-report]] — Pangu Ultra MoE: How to Train Your Big MoE on Ascend NPUs — Pangu Ultra MoE technical report (Huawei Pangu team, arXiv 2505.04519v1, May 2025): a 718B-parameter sparse MoE (256 experts, topk=8 plus shared experts, chosen via pilot runs on a 20B/1.6B-active model and a throughput simulator) trained on 6K Ascend 910B NPUs at MFU 30.0% and 1.46M tokens/s, versus a 4K-NPU baseline of 18.9% MFU / 0.61M TPS. The system half is the relevant part for a no-P2P PCIe box: it quantifies what expert parallelism costs and what it takes to hide that cost. Table 4 gives per-layer communication volumes — plain AllGather dispatch is Seq x TP x EP inter-node; plain All-to-All is Seq x TopK; their Hierarchical EP converts inter-node All-to-All into an inter-node AllGather of Seq x (EP-1) followed by an intra-node All-to-All of Seq x TopK, cutting total volume and letting the two stages overlap with forward and backward compute. Adaptive Pipe Overlap then overlaps EP A2A with finer-grained compute; Table 5 attributes +28.6% MFU to that overlap alone (baseline 1 -> +15.8% fine-grained recompute/swap -> +28.6% adaptive pipe overlap -> +49.2% host optimisation -> +58.7% fused operators, cumulative 30.0% MFU). Design constraints they hit: an excessively large EP degree introduces large communication overhead and degrades performance, so EP degree is chosen jointly against memory and comms; and large TP on granular experts (2048 intermediate size) shrinks matmuls enough to lose efficiency. Caveat: Ascend 910B has an HCCS intra-node fabric and a dedicated inter-node network — nothing here transfers numerically to Gen2 x4, but the structure of the EP cost (volume x per-link bandwidth, hidden only by overlap) does. `review`

- [[scalable-training-of-mixture-of-experts-models-with]] — Scalable Training of Mixture-of-Experts Models with — Scalable Training of MoE Models with Megatron Core (NVIDIA technical report, arXiv 2603.07685, Mar 2026; 39k words): the production reference for how EP/TP/PP/DP compose for MoE training, and the source of the arithmetic this run needs for the EP question. It frames MoE training as three coupled walls. The Communication Wall: EP's per-GPU send volume in one all-to-all is approximately T*K*h*(EP-1)/EP (T local tokens, K top-k, h hidden dim), and a dispatch+combine cycle doubles it; as EP grows the volume saturates but traffic migrates from NVLink to narrower inter-node links where bandwidth drops by an order of magnitude, and unoptimized all-to-all can consume up to 60% of total training time in DeepSeek-V3-like models. The Memory Wall: all E experts' parameters, gradients and optimizer states must be resident even though only K activate, and offloading to host memory costs PCIe bandwidth. The Compute Efficiency Wall: TP on MoE experts is actively harmful because expert hidden dimensions are already small, so TP shards them into even smaller GEMMs while adding communication; GEMMs are ~70% of execution time in dense Llama-3 405B but under 50% in DeepSeek-V3, with routing/permutation adding ~9%. DeepSeek-V3 illustrates the parameter-compute mismatch: 685B total vs 37B active per token (18x). Dispatcher backends are AllGather, all-to-all (NCCL), and Flex (DeepEP/HybridEP, which assume NVLink-rich topologies). Reference throughput on GB300/GB200: 1,233/1,048 TFLOPS/GPU for DeepSeek-V3-685B, 974/919 for Qwen3-235B. Very long source — flag for source-analyst if the EP arithmetic needs to be reproduced exactly. `review`
