- [[deepseek-v4]] — DeepSeek-V4: — DeepSeek-V4 technical report (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv 2606.19348) — the CSA/HCA predecessor that CSA2 in V4.1-Flash simplifies. V4-Flash is 43 layers at d=4096 with pure SWA in the first two layers and CSA and HCA interleaved thereafter; CSA uses compression rate m=4, 64 indexer query heads, indexer head dimension 128, and attention top-k of 512 (V4-Flash) or 1024 (V4-Pro); HCA uses m'=128. Under this configuration V4-Flash is 284B total parameters.

The training recipe is the load-bearing part for the CSA2-at-32k question. Training starts at a 4K sequence length and is gradually extended to 16K, 64K and 1M. Critically, V4 warms up with DENSE attention for the first 1T tokens and only introduces sparse attention at a sequence length of 64K — the direct opposite of V4.1-Flash's "no dense attention warmup stages" claim, and in both generations sparse attention is introduced at 64K, never at 32k. When sparsity is introduced, V4 first runs a short stage to warm up the lightning indexer in CSA and then trains with sparse attention for most of the run. Batch size is raised to 75.5M tokens and held there; LR is warmed up over 2000 steps, held at 2.7e-4, then cosine-decayed to 2.7e-5. Sample-level attention masking is used during pretraining.

Precision and determinism machinery: KV entries use a mixed storage format (BF16 for RoPE dimensions, FP8 for the rest) which nearly halves KV cache size versus pure BF16; lightning-indexer attention is computed in FP4 precision; FP4 QAT is applied during post-training to MoE expert weights and the indexer QK path. Sparse-attention backward uses atomicAdd to accumulate KV gradients, which makes training non-deterministic, so V4 allocates per-SM accumulation buffers and performs a deterministic global summation across them. Optimizer is Muon for the majority of parameters with AdamW for the embedding module.

Also relevant: V4 explicitly notes that relative to DeepSeek-V3.2 a SMALLER attention top-k is chosen in the V4 series to improve efficiency on short- and medium-length texts — i.e. the top-k budget was tuned down for shorter contexts, which undercuts the repo's framing that the budget is "tuned for 1M context" and therefore mismatched to 32k. Sparse-attention kernels are co-designed with the KV cache layout (padding blocks to align with hardware), and the visible range of compressed KV entries is precomputed by rules. 26,488 words — long source, flag for source-analyst. `review`
- [[periodic-weak-spots]] — Periodic Weak Spots: Phase Sensitivity from Chunked KV-Cache Compression — Periodic Weak Spots (Zhu, Yi, Cheng, Lv, Liu, Ying, Ma, Dong — ByteDance Seed + Princeton/Stanford/Berkeley, arXiv 2609.36322v1, 28 Sep 2026): chunked KV-cache compression (the CSA family used by DeepSeek-V4) introduces a positional coordinate — a token's phase, its position modulo the compression stride S — and retrieval accuracy varies sharply and periodically with it. Evidence: (1) DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base completing its own inference code flips its top-1 token with period 4, matching CSA's stride S=4; (2) controlled needle-in-a-haystack at 128K context shows up to a 40-percentage-point retrieval swing across phases in the DeepSeek-V4 family (V4-Flash-Base, V4-Flash-0731, post-trained V4.1-Flash all show it, with periods of 2 or 4); (3) they pretrain from scratch a family of transformers on the Qwen3-0.6B backbone — 100B tokens, sequence length 2,048, global batch 1,024 sequences, BF16, AdamW, 47,518 steps — with KV compression as the only architectural change (reference W8/S8, strides 4/6/8/12, uniform-average gates, no-RoPE, partial-RoPE, plus DeepSeek-V4's CSA kernel on the Qwen backbone). Every compressed variant is phase-sensitive with period equal to its stride; full-attention baselines are flat (Prefix-Padding gaps up to 78pp for compressed vs at most 6.1pp for baselines). The effect is invisible to averages: the 8-KV-head W8/S8 model nearly matches its full-attention baseline on mean accuracy and validation loss, yet its worst residue is 9.9% vs 58.4% for the baseline. Mechanistic work (KV-head knockouts, attention-readout mean replacement) shows phase specialization — different heads/layers serve different phases — and an idealized induction task proves gradient flow drives compression gates to sharp, persistent phase preferences. Prescription: evaluate compressed-attention models by shifting inputs a few tokens, not by average accuracy. Relevance to this run: it is direct evidence that CSA-style compression has a training-time failure mode that only shows up across phases, and it demonstrates the whole effect reproduces at 0.6B params / 100B tokens / 2k context — i.e. inside this box's budget. It says nothing about CSA2's indexer compute cost or about 32k training; the DeepSeek-V4 training length claim has to come from the V4.1 report. `review`
- [[streamindex-memory-bounded-compressed-sparse]] — StreamIndex: Memory-Bounded Compressed Sparse — StreamIndex (Jaber & Jaber, RightNow AI, arXiv 2605.02568) identifies the memory bottleneck that actually gates CSA-class sparse attention at long context: public CSA implementations materialize a [B, S, HI, T] FP32 indexer score tensor before the top-k reduction. With V4-Flash dimensions (HI=64 indexer heads, compression m=4 so T=S/4), that intermediate is 256 GB at S=65,536 and 4 TB at S=262,144; at S=32,768 the pre-head-sum intermediate alone is 64 GB and measured peak HBM is 129 GB on a 140 GB H200. Every public CSA pipeline the authors know of OOMs on a single H200 for S >= 64K before the top-k has been selected — the indexer step, not the attention kernel, gates maximum sequence length ("Assuming attention is the bottleneck at long context is incorrect for CSA: the pipeline runs out of memory in the indexer step before attention runs").

The fix is a Triton chunked partition-merge top-k driver (151 lines, chunked_indexer.py) exploiting per-key separability of the lightning-indexer score (no softmax coupling across keys), so per-tile top-k can be merged; a per-chunk causal mask keeps mask memory at O(cS*cT) instead of O(S*T). Measured on one H200 (140 GB HBM3e, BF16, CUDA 13, Triton 3.7): materialize OOMs at S=65,536; chunked runs to S=1,048,576 at 6.21 GB peak HBM (32x regime extension). At S=32,768 where both fit, chunked is 10.3x faster (31 ms vs 317 ms) at 0.40 GB vs 129 GB. Set-overlap recall vs the materialize reference is bit-exact at small S and min >= 0.9980 across three 5-point design-space sweeps; FP32 score accumulation is required (FP16 drops perfect-recall rows from 99.99% to 91.82%); chunk-size knee at cS=2048; single-chunk over T optimal when memory allows. Composed with TileLang's pipelined attention at S=262,144, the materialize indexer OOMs while the chunked indexer runs in 1.97 s at 18.56 GB peak (1.83x lower peak HBM at S=131,072). Scope limits: synthetic-but-realistic inputs (no V4 checkpoint fits on one H200 — 270 GB FP8 weights), indexer step only, explicit non-claim of a faster attention kernel or real-checkpoint end-to-end behaviour.
