- [[agoq-activation-and-gradient-quantization-for]] — AGoQ: Activation and Gradient Quantization for — AGoQ (Lin et al., HIT Shenzhen + Huawei; arXiv 2605.00539, May 2026) combines two techniques for memory-efficient distributed LLM training: a layer-aware activation quantisation that assigns bit-widths per layer type and pipeline stage to reach near-4-bit activation storage (RMSNorm 4U -> 0.5U, SiLU & Multiply 12U -> 2U, attention stays at 5U; overall activation memory drops from 28U in Megatron-LM to 7.75U, an approximately three-fold reduction), and QuanGrad, an 8-bit (FP8) gradient representation used both for local accumulation and for a "precision-preserved FP8 All-Reduce" implemented as quantise -> reduce-scatter -> re-quantise -> all-gather.

Reported results: up to 52% memory reduction and up to 1.34x training-speed improvement versus Megatron-LM (with and without ZeRO), DeepSpeed and COAT, on 8B-32B LLaMA models, with convergence loss on pretraining and comparable downstream accuracy. Gradient memory drops 75% versus COAT; activation memory drops a further 30% versus COAT.

The most transferable result for this box is their commodity-bandwidth experiment: they explicitly re-ran the collective breakdown at 10 Gbps "to simulate commodity constraints" alongside their 200 Gbps primary testbed. At a 32 MB message under 10 Gbps the decomposed 8-bit all-reduce takes 432.36 ms versus 1,603.78 ms for the baseline - a 3.7x speedup (vs 3.4x at 200 Gbps, 39.17 ms vs 131.23 ms). That is the closest published analogue to the CMP box's ~0.75 GiB/s (~6 Gbps) NCCL fabric: the lower the bandwidth, the LARGER the relative win from halving the payload, which is the opposite of the usual "compression overhead dominates on slow links" intuition.

Caveats: the primary testbed is 64x RTX A6000 over 200 Gb/s InfiniBand (PCIe Gen4-class per-GPU links), and the FP8 path is exercised on NVIDIA Pro 6000 GPUs "to support the FP8 format required by COAT" - again flagging that FP8 needs sm_89+/Hopper-class hardware. GA100 (sm_80) has no native FP8, so on the CMP 170HX the 8-bit path would need int8 or emulated FP8, and the paper does not measure that. `review`
- [[coat-compressing-optimizer-states-and-activation-for-memory-efficient-fp8-traini]] — COAT: Compressing Optimizer states and Activation for Memory-Efficient FP8 Training — COAT (Xi, Cai, Zhu, Lu, Keutzer, Chen, Han; UC Berkeley / NVIDIA / MIT / Tsinghua; arXiv 2410.19313, NVlabs) is the FP8 training framework AGoQ benchmarks against. Two innovations: Dynamic Range Expansion, which shifts optimizer-state distributions to better fill the FP8 representable range and so cuts quantisation error; and Mixed-Granularity Activation Quantization, which combines per-tensor and per-group strategies for activation memory.

Results: 1.54x end-to-end training-memory reduction versus BF16 with nearly lossless quality across LLM pretraining, fine-tuning and VLM training; 1.43x end-to-end training speedup versus BF16, on par with or better than Transformer Engine. It enables full-parameter training of large models on fewer GPUs and "facilitates doubling the batch size in distributed training settings". The worked example is Llama-2-13B on 8x80GB H100 under FSDP.

Relevance here: COAT is the memory-side counterpart to the communication-side FP8 work, and its 1.54x memory reduction is the strongest published lever for fitting a larger trainable parameter count per card. But like FP8-LM it is Hopper-native - the paper explicitly ties FP8 to "the advent of Nvidia's H100 GPU" - so on GA100/sm_80 the memory savings would require emulation. Its "double the batch size" result is directly relevant to the repo's large-global-batch DDP strategy, since batch size is the only lever the repo has for amortising communication. `review`
- [[fp8-lm-training-fp8-large-language-models]] — FP8-LM: Training FP8 Large Language Models — FP8-LM (Peng et al., Microsoft Azure/Research; arXiv 2310.18313) is the primary FP8 training framework paper and the baseline GeoFP8/AGoQ build on. It pushes FP8 into weights, gradients, optimizer states and collective communication in three incremental levels, and reports on GPT-175B over Azure NDv5 H100 nodes: 39% reduction in real memory use and 75% faster than the BF16 Megatron-LM baseline, 37% faster than NVIDIA Transformer Engine, with 42% less memory than TE.

The communication result is the load-bearing one for this run: 8-bit gradients for all-reduce give a theoretical 75% reduction in communication cost versus the mainstream 32-bit scheme (BF16 mixed precision computes gradients in 16-bit but still all-reduces in 32-bit), and the observed practical reduction during GPT training is 63% to 65%. Memory reduction versus BF16 is 28% to 39% (29% for GPT-7B, 39% for GPT-175B).

The technical obstacle they solve is overflow/underflow in low-bit all-reduce: pre-scaling divides before summation (underflow), post-scaling sums first then divides (keeps values near the FP8 max). They add distributed and automated scaling with a single shared scalar per gradient tensor, computed as a global minimum across GPUs, so that standard NCCL all-reduce can still be used - NCCL itself "lacks the capability of performing all-reduce operation considering the additional tensor-wise scaling factors".

HARDWARE CAVEAT that decides feasibility on the CMP 170HX: FP8 here is Hopper-native. The paper frames FP8 as arriving "with the release of Nvidia H100 GPU", and the only prior usable framework (NVIDIA Transformer Engine) applies FP8 solely to GEMM. GA100/sm_80 has no FP8 tensor-core path, so the 63-65% communication saving is not directly available on a CMP 170HX without emulation. Training config: 100B tokens, batch size 4M tokens, sequence length 2048, Adam with beta1=0.9/beta2=0.95, weight decay 0.1. `review`
- [[zero-ofﬂoad-democratizing-billion-scale-model-training]] — ZeRO-Ofﬂoad: Democratizing Billion-Scale Model Training — ZeRO-Offload (Microsoft / UC Merced): offloads optimizer states and gradients to CPU memory to train 13B parameters on a single V100-32GB, a 10x increase over the 1.4B ceiling of plain PyTorch, at 40 TFLOPS/GPU for a 10B model versus 30 TFLOPS for PyTorch on 1.4B. Scales near-linearly to 128 GPUs and reaches 70B parameters on one DGX-2 box when combined with model parallelism (4.5x over MP alone). The load-bearing constraint for this run: CPU memory bandwidth is at least an order of magnitude faster than PCIe bandwidth between CPU and GPU, and GPU memory is another order of magnitude faster again, so the design must minimize CPU-GPU traffic (theoretical minimum 4M for a model-state offload). On a PCIe Gen2 x4 box the PCIe tier is roughly an order of magnitude slower than a modern x16 Gen4 link, so host-offload is even less attractive than in this paper's V100 testbed. `review`
- [[zero-memory-optimizations-toward-training-trillion-parameter-models-sc20]] — ZeRO: Memory Optimizations Toward Training Trillion Parameter Models (SC20) — ZeRO (SC20) canonical memory-optimization paper, arXiv copy of doi 10.1109/sc41405.2020.00024. Derives the DP communication-volume arithmetic this run needs: an all-reduce is reduce-scatter plus all-gather, each moving Psi elements, so standard DP costs 2Psi per step; ZeRO-1/2 (Pos+g) stays at exactly 2Psi; ZeRO-3 (Pos+g+p) costs 3Psi, i.e. 1.5x baseline DP. Memory: a 7.5B model needs 120 GB under standard DP, 31.4 GB (Pos, Nd=64), 16.6 GB (Pos+g), 1.88 GB (Pos+g+p); 'Without ZeRO, the largest model DP alone can run has less than 1.5 Billion parameters' on 32 GB V100s; 1T parameters fit on 1024 GPUs. Empirically on 400 V100s (25 DGX-2 nodes, 800 Gbps inter-node): 170B model, 15 PFLOPs sustained (>30% of peak), 38 TFLOPS/GPU on 60B, 8x model size and 10x performance over Megatron-LM. Explicitly rejects CPU offload because PCIe bandwidth is 'severely constrained' - up to 50% of training time can go to GPU-CPU-GPU transfers. Notes that crossing a node boundary drops per-link bandwidth from 300 GB/s (NVSwitch) to 12.5 GB/s (InfiniBand EDR). `review`
