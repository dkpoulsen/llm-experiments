- [[indexcache-accelerating-sparse-attention-via-cross-layer]] — IndexCache: Accelerating Sparse Attention via Cross-Layer — IndexCache (Bai, Dong, Jiang, Lv, Du, Zeng, Tang, Li — Tsinghua + Z.ai, arXiv 2603.12201) exploits cross-layer redundancy in DeepSeek Sparse Attention: the lightning indexer retains O(L^2) complexity and must run independently at every layer even though the resulting top-k selections are highly similar across consecutive layers. IndexCache partitions layers into a small set of Full layers that run their own indexers and a majority of Shared layers that simply reuse the nearest Full layer's top-k indices. Two variants: training-free IndexCache (greedy search over which layers keep indexers, minimizing LM loss on a calibration set, no weight updates) and training-aware IndexCache (a multi-layer distillation loss training each retained indexer against the averaged attention distributions of all layers it serves; Proposition 1 shows gradient equivalence to distillation against the averaged distribution, so even simple interleaved patterns match full-indexer accuracy).

Results: on a 30B DSA model across nine long-context and reasoning benchmarks, removing 75% of indexer computations gives negligible quality degradation, with up to 1.82x prefill speedup and 1.48x decode speedup at 200K context (decode gains because DSA runs a per-token indexer pass every step). Preliminary results on production-scale GLM-5: 50% indexer removal with comparable long-context/reasoning performance and ~1.2x end-to-end speedup. The paper also documents the standard DSA training recipe: a short dense warm-up trains ONLY the indexer via KL-divergence distillation against the full-attention distribution, after which sparse training proceeds with the indexer receiving distillation gradients on a detached computational graph. 8,771 words. `review`
- [[lisa-linear-indexed-sparse-attention-for-efficient]] — LISA: Linear-Indexed Sparse Attention for Efficient — LISA (Zhao et al., Alibaba International Digital Commerce + Dalian University of Technology + Amazon, arXiv 2607.19358) is a plug-and-play attention replacement that requires NO pretraining from scratch: a Linear Attention branch (O(n) time, long-range memory) runs in parallel with a Lightning Indexer that selects the top-M tokens for a Sparse Self-Attention, the two branches fused by a gating mechanism, reducing inference complexity from O(n^2) to O(nM) for generating n tokens.

Two-stage training: Stage 1 cold-starts the linear attention plus a sliding-window attention mechanism, optimized by knowledge distillation to approximate the full self-attention distribution of a frozen teacher; Stage 2 introduces the Indexer to replace the static sliding window, trained with a per-head KL divergence loss aligning its selection behaviour with the teacher's attention patterns. All original model parameters are frozen — only the newly added linear-attention and Indexer modules train. Because top-M is argmax, gradients cannot flow back through the selection; early in training they select top-1.2M candidates and randomly mask out 20%, gradually reducing the dropout ratio, and apply z-loss to the Indexer.

Budget analysis: MASS (attention mass captured) reaches 80% at M=256, only 6% below the theoretical oracle upper bound; 70% at M=128 and 88% at M=512, so M=256 is the recommended balance. Results on DeepSeek-distilled Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B: 50% inference speedup at 16K context with +5.6% average reasoning accuracy; on Qwen2.5-7B, 70.6% average accuracy vs 263.3 -> 226.6 relative latency measured on a single A100 (batch 1, BF16, eager mode). Training cost: each of the two stages takes approximately 240 GPU-hours on an NVIDIA H100 (~480 GPU-hours total) for the 7B retrofit, with Stage 1 at LR 2e-5, batch 128, 2 epochs, 50-step warmup. 10,747 words. `review`
- [[when-sparse-reward-meets-dense-distilla]] — When Sparse Reward Meets Dense Distillation: Training Dynamics of On-Policy Distillation — When Sparse Reward Meets Dense Distillation (Peking University, arXiv 2609.34849, Sep 2026, ~21k words) - NTK analysis of hybrid RLVR plus on-policy teacher-KL training. Defines a token-level cross-signal NTK K_DR(n) measuring the alignment between reward and distillation gradients at each position, and identifies two failure modes: magnitude drowning (the reward gradient exceeds the distillation gradient by orders of magnitude, so even weak directional conflict makes the distillation loss rise) and localized directional conflict (K_DR(n) < 0, where the sequence-level advantage and the teacher's position-specific distribution push opposite ways). The gradient-norm ratio kappa = ||grad L_R|| / ||grad L_D|| is stable within a run but varies by roughly an order of magnitude across tasks, with an empirical threshold above which naive scalar mixing collapses persistently (Qwen3-1.7B on GSM8K collapses by step 500). Proposes M3 (magnitude normalization plus an NTK mask, a soft relaxation, or a fast-slow extragradient step) and reports stable training across four backbones and four benchmarks where scalar mixing collapses. Relevance to this run is peripheral: it is a post-training RL result, not a pretraining-feasibility one. Its only bearing is that any distillation-based objective (for example a CSA2 indexer distillation loss) needs explicit gradient-magnitude balancing, and that on-policy distillation keeps a teacher and a policy resident at once, which is a memory problem on 64 GB cards. `review`
