# Cross-locus comparisons

Six depth investigators committed to positions. Read side by side, they do not simply add up — they move the same numbers in opposite directions, and two of them converge on a conclusion the repo reaches by accident. These five dynamics are the argumentative spine of the final report. Every one must become a visible beat in the draft, not a one-line gesture.

---

## Tension 1: The denominator war — two corrections push the same ratio opposite ways

- **Locus 1** ([[interim-report-allreduce-ceiling-and-definition]]) commits: 0.75 GiB/s is the nccl-tests **busbw** column, not algbw. At N=5 busbw = 1.6 × algbw by construction, so the communication numerator is 1.6× larger than the repo assumed, and DDP overhead at 1M tokens is **54–58%** (constant 5.4e5–5.8e5), not 36% (3.6e5).
- **Locus 4** ([[interim-report-sustained-throughput-at-32k]]) commits: the defensible training denominator is **~90–100 TFLOPS/card** (450–500 aggregate, 45–50% MFU), not 162/810; and 6ND is invalid at 32k because PaLM's own formula is `6N + 12LHQT`, making attention **88% of the step** and total FLOPs **8.5× the 6ND estimate**.
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** these are not two independent errata — they are two corrections to the two halves of one ratio, and they push it in opposite directions. Locus 1 raises the numerator (communication is 1.6× bigger). Locus 4 raises the denominator (compute is 8.5× bigger *and* the TFLOPS figure is ~1.7× lower, which cuts the other way but is swamped). Net effect: DDP overhead at 1M-token batches falls from the repo's 36% to **~4%**. The repo's *conclusion* — that DDP overhead is small and large batches are the right lever — survives. Both of its *premises* fail. And the same correction that rescues DDP destroys the throughput prediction: 80–120k tok/s at 32k is **2–3× above the hardware's theoretical maximum of 39.6k tok/s at 100% MFU**; realistic is 9–22k tok/s, so 9B tokens takes **5–12 days, not 25 hours**.
- **How the draft should engage this:** the DDP Overhead Model section must present the two corrections as a **single joint re-derivation**, not as two separate errata in sequence. State plainly that the repo's conclusion is right and its arithmetic is wrong in both directions, then show the corrected number. The Verdict must carry the consequence: the overhead model was never the binding constraint, and the wall-clock is 5–12× worse than planned.
- **Calibration:** Locus 1 is **medium (~72%)** — the busbw reading is inferred from the mutual exclusivity of "no P2P" and 0.75 GiB/s as algbw, not from the user's actual command output. Locus 4 is **high** on the FLOP accounting (PaLM's formula is explicit) but flags its own biggest hole: whether 164.4–192.7 BF16 is burst or sustained at 250 W. **Both name the same missing measurement** — an on-box training-shaped benchmark — which makes it the report's single most important open question.

## Tension 2: "DDP only" is right about DDP and wrong about everything else — and the two memory loci converge from opposite sides

- **Locus 2** ([[interim-report-topology-cost-at-1m-tokens]]) commits: DDP is not the only viable topology — for the repo's own 7B-total/1B-active MoE it is **not even a viable one** (112 GB/card). FSDP costs 378% overhead (1.5× DDP's bytes, not 2×). **PP=5 fits at ~25.8 GB/card** and pays only a 4–14% bubble because its 134 MB/micro-batch activation hand-off hides inside a 243 ms compute window. MoE overhead is **252%**, not 36% — exactly the total/active ratio. EP is ~6×, not 72× (the repo divided aggregate volume by per-GPU bandwidth). TP is ~28× and bandwidth-bound at training batch sizes.
- **Locus 3** ([[interim-report-memory-budget-and-frozen-tables]]) commits: freezing the Engram tables is an **architecture choice, not a memory necessity**. 7B fully trainable fits 64 GB four ways — **ZeRO-2 at 5 ranks (33.6 GB state, DDP-identical 2Ψ communication, Adam preserved)**, Adafactor (28 GB), 8-bit Adam (42 GB); plain DDP+Adam does not (112 GB).
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** this is a **convergence, not a conflict**, and it is the most useful kind — two investigators working from different evidence reached the same structural claim from opposite sides. Locus 2 says DDP cannot hold a 7B model. Locus 3 says ZeRO-2 can, at DDP-identical communication. Both are correct because they are the same statement viewed from two ends: **DDP's communication profile is fine; DDP's memory profile is not.** ZeRO-2 is the minimal fix that preserves the communication profile exactly. So the repo's "DDP only" is really "**data-parallel only**", and the correct recommendation is ZeRO-2 (or PP=5), not plain DDP. The repo reached a defensible conclusion by conflating three different things.
- **How the draft should engage this:** the Training Topologies section must reframe "DDP only" as "data-parallel only, with ZeRO-2 as the memory fix that costs nothing in communication". The Verdict must name **ZeRO-2 and PP=5 as the two viable routes** and stop treating frozen tables as the only way to fit 7B. This is where the repo's headline finding #4 is most exposed.
- **Calibration:** Locus 2 is high confidence on the arithmetic (it reproduced the repo's own 36% under one explicit convention to validate it) and explicitly corrects its *own* locus's key_arithmetic, which used a 1M-token micro-batch as the compute window instead of a 32k one. Locus 3 is **high (>80%) on arithmetic and source readings, medium (50–80%) on the prescriptive claim**, because reduce-scatter throughput on this no-P2P fabric is unmeasured. The draft should weight Locus 3's arithmetic heavily and its prescription cautiously.

## Tension 3: The frozen-table design is right and the experiment still cannot detect it

- **Locus 3** commits: freezing is right on this box, but for reasons the repo does not state. At ≤1B the paper's *trained* Engram **loses** to MoE (45.03 vs 45.62) while frozen grafting wins (46.98); training a 6B table costs 1.4–2.2× step time on this fabric. The frozen table is **12.29B params / 24.58 GB**, not 6B/12 GB. The 32k LM-head logits are **9.94 GB bf16 naive**, omitted from the repo's 3.2 GB activation estimate (Liger FLCE cuts it to ~0.6 GB, so the repo's figure is valid only with fused CE — an unstated assumption). The locus's own offload finding was a **5× aggregate-vs-per-link error**: 1.35 GB/s is aggregate, per-GPU it is ~224 MB/s = **13% of the link, not 80%**.
- **Locus 6** ([[interim-report-token-budget-and-effect-size]]) commits: a 9B-token null is interpretable **only as a bound, never as a verdict**. Every published delta the box wants to reproduce is **0.014–0.082 nats (0.8–3.2% relative)** against a **3.5%-relative single-run noise floor** — so a null is the *expected* result even if the effects are real. The real failure mode is **ranking reversal**, because the dense Transformer has the steepest scaling exponent and MoBA's own 32K penalty shrinks 2.3× (0.105→0.045 nats) with compute.
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** Locus 3 establishes that frozen grafting is the **only** Engram variant that wins at ~1B — the design choice is defensible. But the win is +0.2 to +1.4 nats after 50B tokens, which Locus 6 says sits **below the single-run noise floor** at 9B. So the architecture is right and the experiment still cannot validate it. This is a genuine complication rather than a conflict: the repo's design survives scrutiny while its measurement plan does not. 100B/arm is 77 wall-clock days at the measured ~15k tok/s (386 days for five arms), so the fix is not more tokens — it is a **4-size ladder × 2 architectures trained to 1/3 of budget**, fitting exponents at 8K with likelihood/worst-residue metrics (~12–35 days, same envelope).
- **How the draft should engage this:** the Engram & Frozen Memory section must state both halves in one breath — the design is defensible, the experiment is underpowered — and the Verdict must recommend the ladder design rather than a bigger single run. Do not let the section read as "the repo is wrong"; it is "the repo is right and cannot prove it".
- **Calibration:** Locus 3 is high on arithmetic, medium on prescription. Locus 6 flags its own ln(tokens) projection from 100B deltas down to 9B as **low-confidence T4 extrapolation**; a 3-point budget sweep (3B/9B/27B, ~26 days) would settle it. Locus 6 also carries a **cross-locus dependency**: if sparse attention cuts attention FLOPs enough to raise throughput ~3×, the 100B-per-arm option reopens and its design recommendation weakens — which is exactly Tension 4.

## Tension 4: Sparse attention is the only lever that moves wall-clock — and CSA2 is the wrong vehicle for it

- **Locus 5** ([[interim-report-csa2-at-32k-sm80]]) commits: CSA2 at 32k on sm_80 is a **kernel-porting project, not an experiment**. The memory objection is soft (the indexer score tensor is 64–128 GiB but 32×-reducible; StreamIndex measures 0.40 GB at 32k). The architecture objection is hard: cuDNN DSA is SM90/SM100; FlashMLA now targets `sm_100a`/`sm_103a` with **Hopper explicitly dropped**; TileLang's wgmma stack is benchmarked on H800/H200; StreamIndex is `sm_90a`. The only sm_80 path is vLLM's **inference-only** `TRITON_MLA_SPARSE`, with no backward. **Run NSA instead**: measured on 8×A100, 6.3× fwd / 3.4× bwd at 32k, plus a quality win (0.456 vs 0.443).
- **Locus 4** commits: attention is **88% of step FLOPs** at 32k for the 500M baseline.
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** Locus 4 is what makes Locus 5's payoff ceiling large, and Locus 5 corrects Locus 4's implied ceiling upward. If attention is 88% of the step, NSA's measured 4.02× attention-time reduction is worth **−39% to −54% of step time**. CSA2's LongCat 0.83× speedup ratio is the opposite sign: **+10.7% to +14.7% step-time penalty**. That is a 50–69 pp swing between two arms the repo treats as one experiment. And note the ranking this produces: sparse attention (−39 to −54%) is a far bigger lever than the capacitor mod, because after Tension 1 the DDP overhead the capacitor mod attacks is already only ~4%. Locus 5 also corrects the repo's own reasoning in the opposite direction from the loci analyst: the 16,384 candidate cap is **not** what binds at 32k (attention density is top-k 512/32,768 = 64×, independent of the cap); the cap binds on only the 4 decoder Reindex layers, and the real layer mix is 2 SWA + 4 Full + 4 Reindex + 30 Reuse = 40, so **80% of layers are indexer-free**, not 67.5%.
- **How the draft should engage this:** the CSA2 section must effectively become "sparse attention at 32k", with the recommendation being **NSA (or HySparse2)**, and CSA2 named as a porting project with no sm_80 training path. The Verdict must rank the levers explicitly: sparse attention > capacitor mod > optimizer swap. This is the section where the repo's "most likely to disappoint" verdict gets replaced with "not runnable, and the wrong family anyway".
- **Calibration:** Locus 5 is **high** on the substitution recommendation and **medium** on "unportable" — it notes that no [T1] evidence exists for any sparse-attention kernel on a CMP 170HX, and that the single highest-value cheap measurement is running StreamIndex's `pytest tests/` on one card. Its NSA ratio is assumed to transfer from 108-SM/400 W A100 to 70-SM/250 W GA100 (ratio should hold; absolute throughput ~0.65×).

## Tension 5: The binding constraint is wall-clock, not the fabric — the repo's framing is inverted

- **Locus 1** commits: the 24-capacitor x16 mod is the only order-of-magnitude fabric lever — 3.9–4.0× busbw (0.93–1.07 → 3.69–4.24 GB/s), taking DDP overhead at 1M tokens from 54–58% to ~11% at theoretical peak.
- **Locus 4** commits: realistic throughput is 9–22k tok/s; 9B tokens is 5–12 days.
- **Locus 6** commits: 100B/arm is 77 days; five arms is 386 days; the ladder design is 12–35 days.
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** the repo's entire framing is fabric-limited — "PCIe is the constraint, so use DDP and large batches". After correction, the fabric constraint on DDP is **nearly gone** (~4% overhead per Tension 1), and the binding constraint is **sustained compute throughput × token budget**. The capacitor mod's value collapses accordingly: it buys 54%→11% on a number that is now ~4%, so its real payoff is small for DDP and only matters for the topologies that need many small collectives. The repo's central premise — that the fabric is the thing to design around — is **inverted**: the thing to design around is 90–100 TFLOPS/card and the token budget. The experiment program is directionally right (data-parallel, large global batches, frozen tables) but its quantitative spine rests on two unverified premises (Gen2 **x4** and **162 TFLOPS bf16**) plus one confounded FSDP comparison, and its actual bottleneck is wall-clock.
- **How the draft should engage this:** the Verdict must invert the repo's framing explicitly and say so in one sentence a reader can act on. This is the report's thesis, and Tensions 1–4 are the evidence for it. The Verdict must also carry the two cheap pre-flight checks that would settle the unverified premises — `lspci -vv` for negotiated width and a bf16 matmul benchmark for sustained TFLOPS — plus the ECC/memtest_vulkan check the loci clamp dropped (see `loci.json` `skip_loci`), because silent corruption would invalidate the comparability premise the whole program rests on.
- **Calibration:** Locus 1's capacitor-mod figure is arithmetic from community measurements on a *different* host (three cards at Gen2 x16 behind PLX); **no 5-card x16 measurement exists**, and host-staged saturation near 4.3 GB/s is inferred across hosts rather than measured on one rig. Locus 4's throughput band is the widest-confidence item in the report. Locus 6's day counts inherit Locus 4's throughput uncertainty directly — a 2× error in tok/s is a 2× error in every wall-clock figure.

## Post-critic revisions (step 8 gap-fill, 225-note vault)

The corpus critic named six sources that would overturn the committed positions. Three were found and they materially revise the spine above. The draft must use these revised numbers, not the ones in Tensions 1–5.

### Revision A — Tension 1's throughput correction is ~2× too severe (cc-1 OVERTURNED)

SLMTrainBench ([[readmemd-faircslmtrainbench-at-main]]) measures batch-saturated single-GPU training throughput for 150M–8B models at 512–32,768 tokens on 10 GPUs, and computes MFU with **the same `6N + 12LHS` formula** the committed position uses. On A100 80GB PCIe at 505.5M params: **4k = 26,297 tok/s (39.88% MFU), 32k = 11,990 tok/s (63.88% MFU)** — a measured per-token cost ratio of **2.19×, not 4.39×**. A 1.60× MFU uplift absorbs 40% of the quadratic penalty. Both sides are batch-saturated and two seeds agree to 0.3%. The pattern is systematic: 150M 2.21×, 250M 2.27×, 350M 2.23×, 500M 2.19× on A100; 2.31–2.36× on H100 HBM3.

Cross-checks: [[skyladder-better-and-faster-pretraining-via]] (1B: 8K→32K FLOPs 2.20×, measured time tracks FLOPs within 5%); [[deepspeed-ulysses-system-optimizations-for-enabling]] (7B TFLOPs/GPU 161.4→157.4→147.4 across 65k→262k, flat); [[seq1f1b-efficient-sequence-level-pipeline-parallelism-for]] (7B/TP8: 32k→128k throughput falls 53.5k→30.4k while TFLOPs/device **rises** 110.6→136.7 — MFU improves at long context).

**Consequences the draft must carry:**
- Throughput band moves from 9–22k to roughly **14–39k tok/s** (centre ~20–27k); 9B tokens becomes **~3–7 days, not 5–12**.
- "Attention is 88% of step FLOPs at 32k" **survives in the FLOPs convention** but does **not** mean 88% of wall-clock — the 32k step runs at 63.88% MFU, not the 30–50% assumed.
- The committed config is arithmetically impossible: 494M params × 28 layers × d=2048 = **1.41B params**, not 494M. A physically consistent 494M/28L model has d≈1216, giving FLOPs ratio 3.51× and measured wall-clock ratio 2.19×. Even granting the committed 4.39× FLOPs ratio, the measured MFU uplift calibrates it to **~2.97×**.
- The "wall-clock, not the fabric, is binding" conclusion survives qualitatively; its quantitative spine does not.

### Revision B — the 9B experiment is not hopelessly underpowered (cc-5 OVERTURNED)

[[quantifying-pretraining-variance-data-initialization-and-floating-point-arithmet]] ran 23 runs of a 184M transformer on C4, 3.7B tokens, bf16, bitwise determinism verified: "At the final step, the 23 runs span the range [2.941, 2.953]—a maximum deviation of **0.4%** from the overall mean of 2.946. Within-group standard deviations are ≤0.004 for every category." That is **≤0.004 nats = ≤0.14% relative — ~25× below the 3.5% floor** the committed position used. [[effective-learning-rate-governs-loss-dynamics-in-language-model-pretraining]] corroborates: Llama-124M seed-only SD is 1.11e-2 (init) and 1.62e-2 (data-order) nats, with a practical detection threshold ≈**0.01 nats**.

**Consequence:** the memory axis's 0.014–0.082 nat deltas sit **at or above** the measured floor. A 9B-token A/B at 500M is **not** underpowered by 2–14×; it can resolve the upper half of the expected effect range, and a null would be an informative bound (~0.02 nats), not a foregone conclusion. **The ladder recommendation is a nice-to-have, not a necessity** — the draft must downgrade Tension 3's prescription accordingly. Caveat: both measured floors are at 124M–184M, not 500M/9B; seed variance typically shrinks with scale, so the extrapolation direction favours the overturn but is not directly measured at 500M.

### Revision C — P2P *is* a bandwidth lever at N=2, but a pessimization at N=3 and at x4 (cc-6 PARTIAL OVERTURN)

[[2026-10-01-cmp170hx-4card-bar1-p2p-plx-cudaipynb]] measures NCCL all-reduce over **BAR1 P2P on 4× CMP 170HX** (64 GiB BAR1 from the unlock, Gen2 x16, two PLX switches, four BAR1-P2P patches): 256 MiB **N=2: SHM 3.69 → P2P 4.83 GB/s (+31%)**; **N=3: SHM 4.24 → P2P 3.86 GB/s (−9%)** — P2P is *worse* at three ranks. Real workload: GLM-5.3-Flash TP4 decode **19.4 ms/step P2P off → 26.1 ms P2P on (−25 to −44%)**. [[readme_enmd]] at **Gen2 x4** with P2P working: "at Gen2-x4 bandwidth no row shows a strict win"; PP4 −19.1% aggregate. Counter-evidence: [[github-quixiaiopen-gpu-kernel-modules-p2p-support-for-nvidia-consumer-cards-gith]] (8× RTX 3090, PCIe 4.0 x16) shows NCCL busbw **24.7 GB/s vs 2.7 GB/s SHM (9×)** — P2P is a bandwidth lever when BAR1 spans the framebuffer and links are wide.

**Consequences:**
- The literal claim "P2P is a latency lever, not a bandwidth lever" is **false as stated at N=2** and must be restated as "P2P is a bandwidth lever only at N=2 on wide links; on a 5-rank Gen2 x4 fabric it is at best neutral, at worst negative."
- The corpus's "64 MB BAR1 ⇒ BAR1 P2P impossible ⇒ NCCL hangs" reading is **superseded**: the memory unlock supplies a 64 GiB BAR1, the `kbusIsPcieBar1P2PMappingSupported_GH100` blocker disappears, and NCCL collectives then run without hanging.
- The algbw-vs-busbw question remains narrative-only (~4.4% either way under the corrected compute window), exactly as the critic predicted.

### Revision D — the fabric position is confirmed and sharpened (cc-2 STRENGTHENS)

No 5-rank all-reduce receipt exists in any accessible source (club-170hx has 1/2/3/4-card results only). The falsifier (algbw ≤ 0.1 GB/s) is **arithmetically unreachable**: [[i-tested-the-cmp170hx]] measures this exact hardware at this exact width — **"800MB/s at Gen 1 and 1.6GB/s at Gen 2"** — and NCCL drives **53% of raw link** on these cards at Gen2 x16 (4.24 ÷ 8 GB/s). At x4 that is ~0.85 GB/s busbw → **~0.53 GB/s algbw at N=5**, giving 2.0 GB / 0.53 GB/s = 3.8 s over a 56.8 s window = **6.6%** (13.2% with fp32 buckets). Reaching 0.1 GB/s would require NCCL to run at <6% of a link it demonstrably drives at 53% on the same silicon.

Also: [[why-is-the-bandwidth-utilization-of-7-gpus-much-lower-than-that-of-6-8-gpus-for]] — NCCL maintainer sjeaugey confirms **5- and 7-GPU runs are "much lower than others"**, mechanism = offset alignment (4/8 bytes instead of 16), **payload-shape-dependent and paddable away in DDP buckets**. So the N=5 penalty is real but is a CPU-flow/alignment artifact, not a link-bandwidth collapse.

### Revision E — ZeRO-2's real overhead may be CPU-side, not bandwidth (cc-3 NO OVERTURN, one mechanism finding)

ZeRO-2 beats DDP at every GPU count on the closest published fabric analogue ([[pcie-110-gpu-스케일링]], 10×RTX 3090, `topo -p2p r` = CNS, ~3 GB/s shared: +27%/+50%/+10%). But [[perf-29-throughput-by-switching-from-deepspeed-to-ddp-issue-177-tensorautoopen-2]] on 8×A100 **NVSwitch** finds ZeRO-2 at 28.7 vs DDP 83.1 samples/s, and the decisive mechanism is **not bandwidth** — every overlap knob gave zero change (3,071→3,093 ms); the ~2.4 s delta is **CPU-side per-parameter Python hooks**, which is fabric-independent and would bite on this box too, worst on many-small-tensor models (i.e. MoE experts).

Also [[a-13-hour-training-experiment-on-a-pc-workstation-...]]: 4×RTX PRO 6000, PCIe 4, no NVLink, 500M MoE at ~1M-token effective batch — gradient comm is **50% of per-microbatch step time**, and `no_sync()` every 10th step cut 18 h → 13.2 h. This independently supports "DDP overhead at 1M tokens is small after accumulation" on a no-NVLink PCIe box.

### Revision F — sparse attention's payoff is ~half, and its sign is not guaranteed (cc-4 PARTIAL OVERTURN)

No sm_80 DSA/CSA2 training kernel exists — confirmed across FlashMLA ([[github-pzhao-engflashmla-github]]: Ampere port exposes only paged-KV *decode*, no backward, "performance of Ampere is currently poor due to register overflow"), the upstream tracker ([[missing-sparse-backward-kernel-issue-107-deepseek-aiflashmla-github]]: "the sparse attention implementation appears to be forward-only"), flash-attention ([[cutebwdsm100-add-sparse-mla-deepseek-v4-backward-kernels-2621-dao-ailabflash-att]]: sparse-MLA fwd+bwd is **SM100-only**), TileLang ([[exampledeepseek-v32-adaptive-threads-for-sparse-mla-backward-by-butterfingrz-pul]]: profiled on **H200**, no sm_80), and SGLang ([[feature-add-an-opt-in-sm80-fallback-for-deepseek-sparse-attention-dsa-on-nvidia]]: "no end-to-end CUDA path for DSA on A100/SM80"; both proposed fixes are inference-only).

**But the payoff is roughly half the committed size.** [[published-as-a-conference-paper-at-iclr-2026]] (FSA, ICLR 2026) measures end-to-end training latency on Llama3-8B / Qwen3-14B / Qwen2.5-32B at 32K and 64K: "FSA achieves up to 1.25× speedup and on average 1.09× speedup compared to NSA, and delivers up to 2.47× speedup and an average of 1.86× speedup compared to full attention." Dividing the two averages ⇒ **NSA ≈ 1.71× full attention end-to-end**, inside cc-4's 2× threshold. And directly: "vanilla NSA lags behind full attention in many tested cases… when the sequence length is 32K, one GQA group contains one query head, NSA consistently falls short of full attention." [[github-fla-orgnative-sparse-attention-...]] corroborates at kernel level: Triton NSA gets **2.66× fwd / 1.76× bwd at 16,384**, **1.35× / 1.06× at 8,192**, and is **slower than flash at 4,096** (0.72× fwd, 0.61× bwd).

**Consequences:**
- Sparse attention remains a real lever but roughly **half** the committed size (−39 to −54% step time becomes ~−20 to −30%).
- Its **sign is not guaranteed**: it depends on the repo's GQA group size at 32k. If the 494–500M baseline has g=1 at 32K, NSA is a **net loss**; at g≥4 it wins. **That GQA configuration is the decisive unmeasured variable and is not in the corpus.**
- The substitution arm IS runnable on sm_80 — [[github-relaxed-system-labflash-sparse-attention-...]] (FSA) is "well tested with NVIDIA Ampere or Hopper GPUs (e.g., A100 SXM)", supports training and prefill, and backprops through a 65,536+32,768-token batch; [[github-fla-orgnative-sparse-attention-...]] ships full NSA training via `flame`. So the *feasibility* of the substitution is fine; its *payoff* is what fails.

## What the draft must not do

- Do not present the six loci as six independent findings. Tensions 1, 2 and 4 are each a *pair* of corrections that only make sense jointly.
- Do not let the report read as "the repo is wrong". Tensions 2 and 3 both conclude the repo's *conclusions* survive while its premises fail. That distinction is the report's most defensible contribution.
- Do not state any figure without its evidence tier. The corpus contains **no on-box training, all-reduce, NCCL or DDP measurement at all** — `performancemd` and `club-170hxdocsbenchmarksmd` both say so explicitly. Every "measured on this box" number in the repo is user-reported and unverifiable, and must be labelled as such.
