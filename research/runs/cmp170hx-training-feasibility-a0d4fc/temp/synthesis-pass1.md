The recommendation in the repo survives. The reasoning offered for it does not. Data-parallel training, million-token global batches, frozen Engram tables, no expert or tensor parallelism, and a sparse-attention substitution arm are the right program for a five-card CMP 170HX box — and every one of those choices is defended in the documentation by a number that is either unmeasured, mislabelled, or computed against the wrong denominator. The two largest errors point in opposite directions: the link is faster than the model assumes once the right column is read, and the step is far longer than the model assumes once attention is counted. They nearly cancel, which is why the conclusion looks sound and why the program cannot tell when it has stopped being sound.

What binds this box is not the fabric. At million-token global batches, DDP's gradient all-reduce costs roughly 6.6% of step time (T4) — single-digit percent, against the documentation's 36%. What binds is wall-clock: 90–100 TFLOPS per card sustained (T1/T2), not the 162 TFLOPS GEMM peak the plan divides by, which turns a planned 25-hour run into 3–7 days and makes sparse attention, not the capacitor mod, the only lever worth pulling.

Evidence carries a tier throughout. **T1** is measured on this box and is user-reported only: the corpus contains no on-box training, all-reduce, NCCL or DDP measurement at all, and both [[performancemd]] and [[club-170hxdocsbenchmarksmd-at-main-pixelmlclub-170hx-github]] say so explicitly about their own pages. **T2** is a community report or a measurement on adjacent hardware with a receipt. **T3** is paper-measured. **T4** is arithmetic from the tiers above.

## Hardware Reality

A PCIe link's usable bandwidth is the product of two numbers set in different places. Generation fixes the signalling rate per lane — Gen1 is 2.5 GT/s, Gen2 is 5 GT/s — and width fixes how many lanes carry data. A card can be fast-and-narrow or slow-and-wide, and the two properties are fixed by different mechanisms: generation by firmware and fuses, width by whether the board has the capacitors that let the extra lanes carry a signal.

### Two axes, four states

The documentation states "PCIe Gen2 x4" as one measured fact and derives every bandwidth number from it. It is not one fact. Width is a board-level omission: twelve of sixteen lanes ship with their AC-coupling capacitors depopulated, the lane fuses are all clear (`OPT_PCIE_LANE_DISABLE` = `0x00000000`, "No lanes fused off"), and the registers show a physical-layer discontinuity — `LnkCap: Width x16` against `LnkSta: Width x4 (downgraded)` [[physical-modsmd]]. Soldering 24 × 0402 220 nF X7R capacitors restores x16 with no software patch, and the same page is explicit that the mod "changes link width only. It never changes PCIe generation" [[physical-modsmd]]. Generation is firmware: stock trains at Gen1, the cmpunlocker link patch reaches Gen2, and Gen3 is blocked by `OPT_DISABLE_GEN3_SPEED`, which reads 1 on 4/4 cards checked [[pcie-subsystemmd]], [[club-170hxdocshardwaremd-at-main-pixelmlclub-170hx-github]].

| State | Reached by | One-way rate | Tier |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Gen1 x4 | stock | ~0.8–1.0 GB/s | T2 |
| Gen2 x4 | unlocker only | 1.6–1.68 GB/s | T2 |
| Gen1 x16 | capacitor mod only | ~3.2 GB/s | T4 |
| Gen2 x16 | unlocker + capacitor mod | 6.63–6.68 GB/s | T2 |

The anchors are measured. A hands-on review of four of these cards states "the transfer rate is 800MB/s at Gen 1 and 1.6GB/s at Gen 2" [[i-tested-the-cmp170hx]] (T2), and a sanitized NCCL receipt on three cards at Gen2 x16 behind PLX switches measures 6.63–6.68 GB/s host-staged copy [[readmemd]] (T2). The modal state is Gen2 x4 — a four-card rig reports "All four slots negotiate Gen2 x4" across cold power cycles [[readme_enmd]] (T2) — because the capacitor mod is 24 solder joints nobody performs by accident. The gap between the modal state and the best state is 4× on the wire.

### What the link delivers to a collective

The rate that matters to a training step is the all-reduce rate, not the link rate, and the two differ by a large, measurable factor. At Gen2 x16, NCCL reaches 3.69–4.24 GB/s busbw against a 6.63–6.68 GB/s one-way rate — 53–64% of the link [[readmemd]] (T2/T4). Scaling by the measured width ratio 1.68/6.63 = 0.253 puts the x4 busbw band at 0.94–1.07 GB/s and the algbw band at 0.58–0.67 GB/s (T4), because a ring all-reduce's busbw is 2(n−1)/n times its algbw, which at five ranks is exactly 1.6 [[all_reducecu]], [[whats-the-difference-in-allreduce-for-factor-between-the-nccl-and-nccl-test-issu]].

Against that band, the plan's 0.75 GiB/s (0.805 GB/s) is either a busbw reading or an algbw reading, and only one of those is physically possible. Read as busbw it sits at 75–86% of the calibrated band — a slightly conservative measurement. Read as algbw it implies a busbw of 1.29 GB/s, which is 77% of the one-way rate on a fabric where every byte crosses PCIe twice; the best efficiency measured on this hardware class is 64% [[readmemd]] (T2). The busbw reading is the one the evidence supports, and it is the column a user quoting "all-reduce bandwidth" almost always quotes. That labelling question is worth 1.48× on the numerator of every overhead figure in the plan.

### The power brake, and the measurement nobody has made

PWRBRK# is the hardware hazard that most threatens every number here. Asserted on PCIe sideband pin B30, it silently caps a card near 88 W of a 250 W budget, drops clocks to ~1140 MHz, and takes measured fp16 throughput from 155.7 to 39.3 TFLOPS — a 4× collapse that presents as a bad GPU rather than a bad pin [[lessonsmd]] (T2). It is detected with `nvidia-smi -q | grep -A1 "HW Power Brake Slowdown"` and fixed by masking B30 or using a riser that does not route it. Any benchmark run without that check is a benchmark of unknown provenance.

The measurement gap is narrower and more consequential: the corpus contains no five-rank all-reduce at any width. Every receipt is one, two, three or four ranks [[readmemd]], [[2026-10-01-cmp170hx-4card-bar1-p2p-plx-cudaipynb]] (T2), and the three-card receipt concedes "One probe run per pair; no cross-run error bars" [[readmemd]]. Five ranks are also the awkward count: an NCCL maintainer confirms five- and seven-GPU runs are "much lower than others" because of offset alignment — 4- or 8-byte offsets instead of 16 [[why-is-the-bandwidth-utilization-of-7-gpus-much-lower-than-that-of-6-8-gpus-for]] (T2), a penalty paddable away in DDP gradient buckets. A five-rank `all_reduce_perf` at 1 MB / 100 MB / 1 GB with both columns printed is the highest-value missing measurement in the program.

## Training Topologies

Five ways to split a training job across five cards, and they differ in what their communication scales with. Data parallelism (DDP) replicates the model and all-reduces gradients once per optimizer step, so its traffic scales with parameter count and not with sequence length. Fully sharded data parallelism (FSDP, also called ZeRO-3) shards parameters, gradients and optimizer state, all-gathering each layer before use and reduce-scattering it after — the same parameter scaling, paid per layer. Pipeline parallelism (PP) splits the model by layer into stages and passes activations between them, so its traffic scales with tokens × hidden × stages. Tensor parallelism (TP) splits each layer's matrices across ranks and all-reduces inside every layer, so its traffic scales with tokens × hidden × layers and sits on the critical path. Expert parallelism (EP) distributes mixture-of-experts (MoE) experts across ranks and all-to-alls each token's activations to the ranks holding its experts. The question is not which is cheapest in bytes; it is which can run at all, and which puts its traffic where it cannot be hidden.

### DDP: one collective per step

DDP's only per-step traffic is the gradient all-reduce, and PyTorch's reducer registers one autograd hook per parameter, so a parameter with `requires_grad=False` has no gradient accumulator, no hook fires, and no bucket includes it [[pytorch-ddp-comm-hooks-default_hookspy-bf16fp16-compress-hooks]] (T3). That is the mechanism behind the frozen-table design and it is sound. The one rule the plan gets exactly right is `no_sync()`: DDP all-reduces once per optimizer step, and without it the reducer fires on every micro-step, multiplying communication by the accumulation depth.

The closest published analogue confirms the shape. Four RTX PRO 6000 cards on PCIe 4 with no NVLink, training a 500M-total/~330M-active MoE at an effective global batch of ~1M tokens — the same regime — measured "gradient communication accounted for 50% of the single-step time"; enabling `no_sync()` so the all-reduce fires only on the tenth micro-step cut wall-clock from under 18 hours to 13.2 hours, leaving ~1.7% in sync [[a-13-hour-training-experiment-on-a-pc-workstation-why-optimization-begins-with-m]] (T2).

### FSDP: 1.5×, not 2×, and the comparison is confounded

The plan's "FSDP costs ~2× DDP" is wrong on the multiplier and wrong on the comparison. ZeRO's arithmetic is explicit — "The total volume is therefore 3Ψ which is 1.5x compared to the baseline" [[zero-memory-optimizations-toward-training-trillion-parameter-models-sc20]] (T3). The 2× only reproduces if you force fp32 gradient reduction via `MixedPrecision(reduce_dtype=torch.float32)`; FSDP's default `reduce_dtype=None` "takes on the param_dtype value, still running gradient reduction in low precision" [[fullyshardeddataparallel-pytorch-213-documentation]] (T3). With bf16 reduce, FSDP moves 6 B/param against DDP's 4 = 1.5×; forcing fp32 gives 8 B/param = 2.0×. The plan's 2× is the pessimistic variant, not the default.

The comparison is also confounded: the plan's table pits FSDP at 7B trainable (28 GB, 37 s, 72%) against DDP at 1B trainable (2 GB, 2.7 s, 36%). At equal 7B trainable, DDP is 28 GB/18.7 s and FSDP is 42 GB/28.0 s — 1.5×, not 2× (T4), and the correct FSDP overhead at 7B dense is 54%, not 72%. FSDP's per-layer all-gather also sits on the forward critical path, the worst possible shape for a latency-bound fabric.

### PP and ZeRO-2: the two routes the plan never considers

PP's traffic is O(tokens × hidden × stages). For five stages at 32k tokens and hidden 2048, one hand-off is 32,768 × 2048 × 2 B = 134 MB (T4), and 1F1B overlaps each hand-off with the previous micro-batch's compute rather than serializing it. At 1.68 GB/s the hand-off takes ~80 ms inside a per-micro-batch compute window of 240–440 ms, so it hides with room to spare. What remains is the bubble: (5−1)/32 = 12.5% for 1F1B at 32 micro-batches, ~4.2% for zero-bubble schedules, and under 4.5% measured for RoundPipe with asynchronous optimizer updates [[efficient-training-on-multiple-consumer-gpus-with]] (T2/T3). PP=5 puts 7B/5 = 1.4B parameters per stage × 16 B/param = 22.4 GB of weights and optimizer state, plus ~3.4 GB of in-flight activations, at ~25.8 GB/card (T4) — 64 GB with 38 GB of headroom, and it trains the experts without freezing anything. PP is also the one topology with a published no-P2P demonstration: RoundPipe runs "without utilizing any GPU peer-to-peer communication (NVLink), relying entirely on PCIe host-to-device transfers" and still reaches "over 76% of the throughput of existing SOTA frameworks running on the A800" [[efficient-training-on-multiple-consumer-gpus-with]] (T2). The caveat is static shapes: `torch.distributed.pipelining` "needs to know the input and output shapes for the stage model… The shapes must be static", a genuine constraint for a model with MoE routing, Engram lookup and CSA2 top-k.

ZeRO-2 at five ranks is the other route. It shards optimizer state and gradients but not parameters, so it costs 4.8 B/param × 7B = 33.6 GB of model state (T4) and its communication is "exactly 2Ψ" — the same byte volume as DDP's all-reduce [[zero-memory-optimizations-toward-training-trillion-parameter-models-sc20]] (T3) — while preserving Adam, so comparability with the control survives. The evidence on whether it is faster than DDP is genuinely split. On the closest published fabric analogue — ten RTX 3090s sharing ~3 GB/s of PCIe with P2P disabled — ZeRO-2 beats DDP at every GPU count: +27% at four, +50% at eight, +10% at ten [[pcie-110-gpu-스케일링]] (T2). On 8×A100 with NVSwitch it loses 2.90×, for the CPU-hook reason above. So the recommendation is ZeRO-2 for the 7B arm with a measured step-time A/B against PP=5 before committing.

### The corrected comparison

| Topology | Collectives per step | Traffic scales with | Per-GPU volume at 1M tokens | Overhead vs 56.3 s | Fits 7B on 64 GB | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| DDP | 1 all-reduce | O(params) | 2.0 GB bf16 grads | ~6.6% | No (112 GB/card) | Viable ≤1B trainable |
| ZeRO-2 | reduce-scatter + all-gather | 2Ψ (DDP-identical) | 2.0 GB | ~6.6% + CPU hooks | Yes (33.6 GB) | Best memory/comm trade |

The table's shape is the argument. DDP and ZeRO-2 are cheap; PP is cheap and solves memory; TP and EP are not expensive so much as unavailable or badly placed. "DDP only" should be restated as "data-parallel only, with ZeRO-2 as the memory fix that costs nothing in communication and PP=5 as the fallback that costs nothing in memory".

### P2P is not the constraint it looks like

On a four-card PLX rig at Gen2 x16, BAR1 P2P moves a 256 MiB all-reduce at N=2 from 3.69 to 4.83 GB/s (+31%) but at N=3 from 4.24 to 3.86 GB/s (−9%); on a real workload, GLM-5.3-Flash TP4 decode goes from 19.4 to 26.1 ms/step with P2P on, a 25–44% regression [[2026-10-01-cmp170hx-4card-bar1-p2p-plx-cudaipynb]] (T2). At Gen2 x4 with P2P working, "at Gen2-x4 bandwidth no row shows a strict win" and PP4 loses 19.1% aggregate [[readme_enmd]] (T2). The reason is that the host-staged path already saturates the link at x16 — 6.21–6.30 GB/s at 256 MiB against a 6.63–6.68 GB/s one-way rate — so P2P's only real gain there is latency, 16 µs against 22 µs at 4 KiB [[readmemd]] (T2). Counter-evidence exists where links are wide and BAR1 spans the framebuffer: 8× RTX 3090 at PCIe 4.0 x16 shows NCCL busbw of 24.7 GB/s with P2P against 2.7 GB/s host-staged [[github-quixiaiopen-gpu-kernel-modules-p2p-support-for-nvidia-consumer-cards-gith]] (T2). So P2P is a bandwidth lever only at N=2 on wide links; on a five-rank Gen2 x4 fabric it is at best neutral and at worst negative.



## DDP Overhead Model

An overhead ratio is communication time divided by compute time. The communication half is a payload divided by the achieved collective bandwidth; the compute half is FLOPs divided by sustained throughput. Two conventions decide both halves. The first is the bandwidth column: nccl-tests reports algbw, the payload rate, and busbw, which multiplies algbw by 2(n−1)/n to describe the per-rank link rate, so at five ranks the two differ by 1.6×. The second is the FLOPs convention: the shorthand "6ND" counts only the matmuls outside attention, six FLOPs per parameter per token, while the canonical formula adds the attention term and grows with context length. Model FLOPs utilization (MFU) is achieved tokens per second divided by the peak the formula implies, so a peak used as a sustained rate inflates MFU by the gap.

### The joint re-derivation

The plan's model is a single ratio with a single constant: overhead = 3.6 × 10⁵ / global_batch_tokens, giving 36% at 1M tokens and 18% at 2M. It comes from dividing a 2.0 GB bf16 gradient payload by 0.75 GiB/s to get 2.7 s, and dividing 6 FLOPs/param/token × 10⁹ params × 10⁶ tokens by 810 TFLOPS to get 7.4 s. Both halves are wrong, and they are wrong in opposite directions — which is why the conclusion survives.

The numerator grows 1.48×. 0.75 GiB/s is 0.805 GB/s. At N=5, busbw = 1.6 × algbw by construction, so if 0.805 GB/s is a busbw reading then algbw is 0.503 GB/s and the payload takes 3.98 s rather than 2.7 s. Physics corroborates the reading: as algbw, 0.805 GB/s would require NCCL to hit 77% of the one-way link rate on a fabric where every byte crosses PCIe twice, and the best efficiency measured on this hardware class is 64%; the measured anchor — 1.6 GB/s link, ~53% NCCL efficiency, ~0.85 GB/s busbw, ~0.53 GB/s algbw — lands within 5% of the busbw reading (T2/T4).

The denominator grows 7.6×. Two errors compound. First, 6 FLOPs/param/token is the non-attention half of the canonical formula. PaLM states it explicitly: "an N-parameter decoder-only model requires 6N matmul FLOPs per token seen… The matmuls in dense self-attention add 6LH(2QT) FLOPs per token", so the theoretical peak is R = P(6N + 12LHQT) [[palm-scaling-language-modeling-with-pathways]] (T3). For a 1B-active, 24-layer, hidden-2048 model at 32k, the attention term is 12 × 24 × 2048 × 32,768 = 1.933 × 10¹⁰ FLOPs/token against a linear term of 6.0 × 10⁹ — attention is 76% of the step and total FLOPs are 4.2× the 6ND estimate (T4). Megatron-Core corroborates from measurement: "at 64K tokens, SDPA consumes 69% of FLOPs, compared to only 10–15% in short-sequence scenarios" [[scalable-training-of-mixture-of-experts-models-with]] (T3). Second, 810 TFLOPS is a peak, not a sustained rate; the defensible figure is 450–500. Together: 2.533 × 10¹⁶ FLOPs per 1M-token step ÷ 450 TFLOPS = 56.3 s of compute, not 7.4 s.

The net effect is 36% → 6.6%. The numerator grows 1.48×, the denominator grows 7.6×, and the ratio falls 5.4×. With fp32 gradient buckets (4.0 GB payload) the overhead is 13.2%.

|---|---:|---:|---:|

| Sustained denominator only: 6ND, 450 TFLOPS | 13.3 s | 2.5 s | 19% |
| 32k, 450 TFLOPS, 0.805 GB/s as algbw | 56.3 s | 2.5 s | 4.4% |

The 36% is reproducible from the plan's own inputs, which is why it looked solid: it is the overhead of a short-context run at burst peak. The 32k arms sit at 1.5–11.4%, centre 4–7%. The algbw-versus-busbw question, which looked like the decisive dispute, moves the answer by about 2.7 percentage points once the compute window is corrected — a narrative question, not a quantitative one. The derived 6.6% is the conservative figure — measured link rate, full attention FLOPs, 45% MFU — and it sits within a factor of four of the adjacent-hardware measurement at the same batch regime, 1.7% amortized after `no_sync()` [[a-13-hour-training-experiment-on-a-pc-workstation-why-optimization-begins-with-m]] (T2). The plan's 36% sits a factor of five above the derived number and twenty above the measured one.

### Where the invariance claim fails

The plan's headline — "DDP's overhead depends only on batch size, not model size" — is an identity for dense models. Communication time is 2·N·b/B_wire; compute time is 6·N·T/F_agg; the ratio is 2·b·F_agg/(6·T·B_wire), independent of N. At 1M tokens and the corrected denominators that is 6.6%, and it stays 6.6% whether the model is 500M or 7B, until it stops fitting.

It fails for MoE, and the failure is mechanical rather than a matter of degree. Every expert weight is a parameter with `requires_grad=True`, so DDP's reducer buckets and all-reduces the full parameter set regardless of which experts saw tokens; an expert that received zero tokens contributes a zero gradient but still occupies its slice of the all-reduce buffer. Megatron-Core states both halves: "All E experts' parameters, gradients, and optimizer states must reside in memory" while "Per-token FLOPs scale only with N_active (∝K), not N_total (∝E)… This is not a matter of degree; it is a qualitative difference from dense models" [[scalable-training-of-mixture-of-experts-models-with]] (T3). The corrected formula is overhead ≈ 6.6% × (N_total/N_active), which for the plan's own 7B/1B axis is 47% (T4). The invariance claim is true only for the frozen-table design where trainable equals active, and false for the MoE arm whose experts are trainable by construction. Stated properly: freezing the tables is what makes DDP's overhead invariant, not a property of DDP.

### The same correction destroys the throughput prediction

The plan predicts 80–120k tok/s for a ~500M dense baseline at 32k. At 100% MFU of the measured 202.1 TFLOPS ceiling, five cards deliver 1010.5 TFLOPS; a 500M model at 32k needs 2.554 × 10¹⁰ FLOPs per token, so the absolute maximum is 39.6k tok/s (T4). The prediction requires 2.0–3.0 PFLOPS aggregate, or 400–600 TFLOPS per card — 2–3× the hardware ceiling, before any MFU discount. At 30.2% MFU the box does 305 TFLOPS and 11.9k tok/s; at 46.2% it does 467 TFLOPS and 18.3k tok/s; adding PaLM's +25% rematerialization cost and the 4–7% DDP overhead gives 14–39k tok/s, centre 20–27k (T4). Nine billion tokens therefore takes 3–7 days, centre 4–5, not 25 hours.

The plan's throughput number is not fabricated; it is a short-context number wearing a 32k label. Its own 494M-model run at ~140k tok/s implies ~83 TFLOPS/card, a plausible 43–51% MFU against the 162–192.7 band, and scaled to 32k it becomes ~16.5k tok/s — inside the band.

The calibration that matters is that the FLOPs ratio is not the wall-clock ratio. SLMTrainBench measures a 505.5M model at 4k = 26,297 tok/s (39.88% MFU) and at 32k = 11,990 tok/s (63.88% MFU) on an A100 80GB PCIe, computing MFU with the same 6N + 12LHS formula: a measured per-token cost ratio of 2.19×, with a 1.60× MFU uplift absorbing 40% of the quadratic penalty [[readmemd-faircslmtrainbench-at-main]] (T3). The pattern is systematic — 2.21× at 150M, 2.27× at 250M, 2.23× at 350M, 2.19× at 500M — and SkyLadder's 1B model cross-checks it with a 2.20× FLOPs ratio from 8K to 32K [[skyladder-better-and-faster-pretraining-via]] (T3). The FLOPs ratio at this configuration is 4.2×; the wall-clock ratio is 2.19×. Presenting the first as the second overstates the context penalty by nearly 2×.

One further correction: the plan's baseline triple is arithmetically impossible. 494M parameters at 28 layers and hidden 2048 is ~1.41B parameters, not 494M (T4). A physically consistent 494M/28-layer model has hidden ≈1216, which gives a 32k/4k FLOPs ratio of 3.51× and the measured wall-clock ratio of 2.19×. Either the model is 1.4B and throughput sits at the low end of the band, or it is 494M with hidden 1216 and the FLOPs ratio is 3.51×, not 8.5×.

### Batch size: right destination, wrong starting point

A 1M-token global batch is only token-efficient once the critical batch size exceeds 1M tokens, and critical batch size is not a constant: it starts near zero at initialization, grows rapidly within the first 50k tokens, and plateaus around 4,096 documents of 4,096 tokens — about 16.8M tokens — on OLMo 1B and 7B [[critical-batch-size-revisited-a-simple-empirical]] (T3). At the plan's 9B-token budget it is far below 1M, so a 1M-token batch from step one sits above the critical point and burns token efficiency for wall-clock the box cannot deliver anyway. Warmup — start small, double as the critical batch size grows — reaches slightly better final loss than the small-batch control while using 43% fewer gradient steps, whereas a fixed 4,096-document batch from step 0 uses 75% fewer steps but ends with worse loss [[critical-batch-size-revisited-a-simple-empirical]] (T3). The 1M-token target is right as a steady-state destination and wrong as a starting point.

### The lever inversion

The plan's framing is that the fabric is the thing to design around, so the design goal is "make the trainable set fit on one card". After correction the fabric constraint on DDP is nearly gone — 6.6% at 1M tokens, 1.7% measured on adjacent hardware — and the binding constraint is sustained compute throughput times token budget. That inverts the lever ranking. The 24-capacitor x16 mod is the only order-of-magnitude fabric lever, taking busbw from ~0.94–1.07 to 3.69–4.24 GB/s, a 3.9–4.0× step [[physical-modsmd]], [[readmemd]] (T2). Under the plan's framing that takes DDP overhead from 54% to 11%; under the corrected framing it takes 6.6% to 1.7% — a 5-point saving on a 56-second step, or 6–7% of wall-clock over a 9,000-step run (T4). Sparse attention at −20% to −30% of step time is three to four times larger.

## MoE & EP

A mixture-of-experts layer replaces one dense feed-forward block with many expert blocks and a router that sends each token to a few of them, so total parameters can be large while per-token FLOPs stay small — only the selected experts compute. Expert parallelism distributes those experts across ranks, which means each token's hidden state must be shipped to whichever ranks hold its experts (dispatch) and the results shipped back (combine): two all-to-alls per MoE layer. That is the only collective in the training stack whose payload scales with tokens rather than parameters, and it is the one the plan rules out.

### The 70× is an aggregate-versus-per-GPU error

The plan's "EP does two all-to-alls per MoE layer; at 1M-token batches that is ~70× the compute time" divides the aggregate dispatch-plus-combine volume across all ranks (~400 GB) by the per-GPU collective rate. Each GPU sends only its share. Megatron-Core's per-GPU send volume in one all-to-all is T·K·h·(EP−1)/EP, where T is the tokens on that GPU, K is top-k, h is hidden width and EP is the expert-parallel size [[scalable-training-of-mixture-of-experts-models-with]] (T3). At a 1M-token global batch over five data-parallel ranks, T_local = 200,000; with top-k 2, hidden 2048 and EP=5, dispatch is 200,000 × 2 × 2048 × 0.8 = 6.55 × 10⁸ elements = 1.31 GB in bf16; dispatch plus combine is 2.62 GB per layer; across 24 MoE layers that is 62.9 GB per GPU per step (T4). At 0.805 GB/s that is 78 s. Against the plan's 6ND compute window of 7.4 s it is 10.6×; against the corrected 56.3 s window it is 1.39×.

TP at the same configuration moves four all-reduces per layer over forward and backward, each carrying T_local × h × 2 B = 819 MB per rank, which on a five-way ring is 1.6× that on the wire = 1.31 GB, so 4 × 1.31 × 24 = 126 GB per GPU per step, 156 s, or 2.8× against the corrected window (T4). At 32k, EP is ≈1.4× and TP ≈2.8× — not 70× and not 28×.

### The bandwidth case evaporates; availability and placement remain

Correcting the number strengthens the conclusion rather than weakening it, because bandwidth arithmetic was never the right argument. What rules EP out is software. Every MoE-specialised EP library in existence — DeepEP, pplx-kernels, NIXL-EP, NCCL EP, UCCL-EP — assumes GPUs can directly access one another through NVLink, GPUDirect P2P or RDMA, and "existing EP communication libraries cannot operate on these systems" [[efficient-expert-parallel-communication]] (T3). The one prototype that does work on PCIe without P2P, ThunderEP, reaches 2.00× dispatch and 1.53× combine over NCCL on 6×4090 and 6×5090 systems — but it is integrated into vLLM and evaluated on inference, with no gradient collectives [[efficient-expert-parallel-communication]] (T3). CoMoE reaches 85–90% of an NVLink A800 + DeepEP configuration at 23.4% of the hardware cost, and VarioPath reports 5.88× over FAST and 1.72× over DeepEP — but both are inference systems, and VarioPath's fastest platform is PCIe 5.0 x16 at roughly 55 GB/s, about 32× this box's link [[democratizing-moe-inference-on-commodity-gpus]], [[variopath-workload-aware-all-to-all-communication-for-pcie-gpu-clusters]] (T3). So EP is out because no training-grade EP implementation exists for a no-P2P, no-NVLink, no-RDMA fabric — a software fact, not a physical one.

TP is out for a placement reason rather than a bandwidth one. Its all-reduces sit inside the layer, serialized with the layer's compute, so equal volume does not mean equal cost: EP's all-to-all can overlap with expert computation, which is why communication-computation overlap is the standard EP optimization. DeepSeek-V3's DualPipe hides both all-to-all and pipeline communication behind compute, reaching "near-zero all-to-all communication overhead" as long as the compute-to-communication ratio holds [[deepseek-v3-technical-report]] (T3). TP has no equivalent hiding place. That is the real reason TP is the worst topology here, and it is a scheduling property, not an arithmetic one.

### MoE is the wrong axis for this box

There is a second reason to avoid MoE that the plan never states. MoE's purpose is to decouple parameter count from per-token FLOPs, which lets you buy capacity with memory. On a five-card box with 320 GB of HBM, memory is the scarce resource and FLOPs are comparatively abundant at 375–500 TFLOPS aggregate, so MoE spends the scarce resource to save the abundant one. Worse, it multiplies DDP's payload by the total-to-active ratio derived above: a 7B-total/1B-active model all-reduces 7B parameters while computing only 1B, so its DDP overhead is 47% rather than 6.6% (T4).The production literature supplies the scale of the problem from the other direction. On NVLink-rich multi-node DeepSeek-V3, "unoptimized all-to-all can consume up to 60% of total training time" [[scalable-training-of-mixture-of-experts-models-with]] (T3). That 60% is measured on a fabric where the drop from intra-node NVLink to inter-node InfiniBand is one order of magnitude; on this box the drop from NVLink's 600 GB/s to Gen2 x4's 1.68 GB/s is roughly 350×. Sixty percent of training time on a fabric 35× better than this one is not a cost estimate; it is a wall. The strongest lab in this space reached the same conclusion from the opposite direction: DeepSeek-V3 was trained without tensor parallelism at all, so the lab with the strongest incentive to use TP chose EP32 plus data parallelism instead [[deepseek-v3-technical-report]] (T3).

The plan's own MoE arm is the biggest correction in this section. A 7B-total/1B-active MoE has 7B trainable parameters, because the experts are learned; under plain DDP with Adam that is 7B × 16 B/param = 112 GB per card (T4), which does not fit 64 GB. DDP is not merely suboptimal for the MoE arm — it is not viable at all. The plan's headline says exactly this about a fully-trainable 7B model and then applies the fix (freezing) to the Engram tables, which are a different model. The MoE arm has no frozen tables to freeze. It needs ZeRO-2 at five ranks or PP=5, both of which fit and both of which are described above.

## Engram & Frozen Memory

Engram is conditional memory: hashed n-gram lookup tables that return learned hidden vectors and splice them into the residual stream, so the model can store knowledge in a table rather than in weights. The table costs VRAM but almost no per-token FLOPs, because a lookup is not a matmul. Memory Grafting takes a donor model's hidden states for the most frequent n-grams, freezes them as a table, and grafts them into a smaller recipient model that looks rows up by longest-match suffix. The question is whether freezing those tables is what makes a 7B-total / ~1B-trainable model fit DDP on 64 GB cards — and whether the design can be measured at all.

### Freezing is a collective-elimination design, not a memory trick

The plan justifies freezing on memory: 7B fully trainable needs 112 GB/card, so freeze the tables. That arithmetic is correct for the one configuration it tests and it is not the strongest available argument. Engram's own training recipe shards the tables across GPUs and uses All-to-All to gather active rows in the forward pass and dispatch gradients in the backward pass [[conditional-memory-via-scalable-lookup]] (T3). That is precisely the collective this fabric cannot run — the same all-to-all that rules out expert parallelism. Freezing the tables does not merely save memory; it removes the collective entirely: no all-to-all, no gradient dispatch, no optimizer state, no per-step table update. The frozen-table design is the only Engram variant that keeps the architecture off the wire, and that is a fabric argument rather than a memory argument.

### The table is 24 GB, not 12 GB

The paper is unambiguous: "For each n-gram order, we select the top 1M most frequent n-grams and compute their frozen memory representations offline, yielding 3M entries per grafted layer. Each entry stores a hidden-state vector of the grafting model in bfloat16; for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, this corresponds to roughly 12 GB of frozen memory per layer" [[memory-grafting-scaling-language-model]] (T3). The Figure 4 caption confirms the total: "0.92B trainable parameters with approximately 12B frozen grafting memory offloaded". Three million entries × 2,048 dimensions × 2 B = 12.29 GB per layer; two grafted layers (donor layers 8 and 24) = 24.58 GB = 12.29B parameters (T4).

The plan's numbers do not survive contact with that sentence. Its §3 says "33M entries per grafted layer" while keeping "roughly 12 GB per layer" — 33M × 2,048 × 2 B is 135.3 GB, an 11× internal inconsistency (T4) — and its §5 budgets "6B frozen" = 12 GB total, exactly one layer of the paper's table. Neither error is fatal to the conclusion, but both would be fatal to a reproduction attempt.

The logits term is the other omission. The plan's activation estimate — "32,768 tokens × 2048 hidden × 2 B × 24 layers ≈ 3.2 GB" — counts only checkpointed layer inputs and silently assumes a fused cross-entropy. At 32k × 151,669 vocabulary × 2 B, the LM head produces 9.94 GB in bf16 if materialized (T4); Liger Kernel's FusedLinearCrossEntropy "never materializes the full [B*T, V] logits tensor" [[liger-kernel-efficient-triton-kernels-for-llm-training]] (T3), which cuts the term to ~0.62 GB at this shape (T4). The plan's 3.2 GB is valid only with fused CE — standard in 2024–25 stacks, but an unstated assumption worth 10 GB of budget.

### Does it fit DDP on 64 GB?

| Component | Plan's budget | Corrected | Tier |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Frozen table (12.29B params, bf16) | 12 GB (6B) | 24.58 GB | T3/T4 |
| Trainable recipient (0.92B × 16 B) | 16 GB (1B) | 14.72 GB | T4 |
| Checkpointed activations at 32k, 24L, d=2048 | 3.2 GB | 3.22 GB | T4 |
| LM-head logits (fused CE) | omitted | 0.62 GB (9.94 GB naive) | T3/T4 |
| **Total** | **~31 GB** | **~43.1 GB** | T4 |
| Headroom on 64 GB | ~33 GB | **~21 GB** | T4 |

Yes, the frozen-table design fits DDP on 64 GB — at ~43.1 GB with fused cross-entropy and 21 GB of headroom, or ~53.1 GB and 11 GB of headroom without it. The conclusion survives; the arithmetic is wrong by ~12 GB in one direction (the table is twice as big) and ~10 GB in the other (the logits term is omitted). DDP's all-reduce payload is only the 0.92B trainable gradient, 1.84 GB, which at 0.53 GB/s algbw is 3.5 s against the 56.3 s compute window — 6.2% (T4).

### Freezing is an architecture choice, not a memory necessity

The plan's headline — "7B fully trainable with Adam needs ~112 GB per card — it does not fit DDP on 64 GB" — is correct for plain DDP plus Adam (7B × 16 B/param = 112 GB) and tests only that one configuration. Three alternatives fit:

| Route | Bytes per trainable param | Model state | Total with activations | Fits 64 GB? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| DDP + Adam (fp32 master + m + v) | 16 | 112 GB | 115.8 GB | No |
| DDP + 8-bit Adam (block-wise quantized) | 6 | 42 GB | 45.8 GB | Yes |
| DDP + Adafactor (bf16 w+g, factored state) | 4 | 28 GB | 31.8 GB | Yes |
| ZeRO-2 at 5 ranks + Adam | 4.8 | 33.6 GB | 37.4 GB | Yes |

Both optimizer routes are validated. Adafactor keeps "only the per-row and per-column sums" of Adam's second moment, giving state proportional to n + m rather than nm [[adafactor-adaptive-learning-rates-with-sublinear-memory-cost]] (T3), and Adafactor-with-momentum is "comparable to Adam" in optimal loss and learning-rate stability across 150M–1.2B [[published-as-a-conference-paper-at-iclr-2025]] (T3). 8-bit Adam's block-wise dynamic quantization matches 32-bit performance at 1.5B and 355M with no hyperparameter changes [[published-as-a-conference-paper-at-iclr-2022]] (T3), whereas naive linear quantization of the second moment "failed to converge smoothly" [[exploring-quantization-for-efficient-pre-training-of-transformer]] (T3). So freezing is an architecture choice, not a memory necessity, and the plan's headline is false as a general claim.

### Why freeze anyway: the sign flips, and training the table costs step time

Freezing is nonetheless the right choice here, for two reasons the plan does not state.

The first is that at this scale the trained-table baseline is negative. The paper's 0.92B/50B-token table reads MoE 45.62, vanilla Engram 45.03, MG-GLM 45.83, MG-DeepSeek 46.42, MG-Qwen3.5 46.98 [[memory-grafting-scaling-language-model]] (T3). Vanilla Engram loses to MoE by 0.59 points, which the paper attributes to hash collisions at the limited table size. At 2.8B/100B tokens the sign flips: MoE 51.95, vanilla Engram 52.43, MG 53.86. On a ≤1B-active box, freezing plus grafting is the only configuration in the paper with a positive sign — freezing is not just cheaper, it is the only variant that wins.

The second is fabric cost. Training the table rather than freezing it means its gradients must move. Under DDP the gradient tensor is dense, 12 GB at bf16, so the all-reduce moves 1.6 × 12 = 19.2 GB per rank; at 0.805 GB/s that is ~24 s against 56.3 s of compute, a ~42% step-time penalty (T4). Under the Engram recipe of sharded tables plus all-to-all the traffic is ~20 GB per step, ~25 s, a ~44% penalty (T4). Freezing removes both. And the penalty is a function of the link, which is the unmeasured premise: at Gen2 x16 with 3.7 GB/s busbw the gradient all-reduce takes 5.2 s, or 9%, while at Gen1 x4 with ~0.5 GB/s it takes 38 s, or 68%. Freezing is preferable on a fast link and necessary on a slow one.

Two caveats belong on the record. The plan's 86% frozen allocation sits outside both published allocation laws: Engram's U-shaped optimum puts "roughly 20%–25% of the sparse parameter budget" into memory rather than experts [[conditional-memory-via-scalable-lookup]] (T3), and the grafting paper finds that "training efficiency began to decline once the memory module accounts for more than 70% of total parameters" [[memory-grafting-scaling-language-model]] (T3). The 70% threshold applies to trainable memory, so freezing sidesteps it rather than violating it, but an 86% frozen share is three to four times outside the published optimum and should be described as a deliberate departure.

### The measurement problem is a unit problem

The deltas above are score points on a ten-task average, not nats of training loss, and no published Engram or CSA2 delta is reported in a unit in which a noise floor has been established. That is a stronger claim than "underpowered", because it is falsifiable by a single experiment that reports the delta in nats with two seeds.

The floor itself has been measured. A 23-run variance decomposition at 184M parameters and 3.7B tokens in bf16, with bitwise determinism verified across data centres, reports "Within-group standard deviations are ≤0.004 for every category; FP arithmetic is even tighter at 0.001" [[quantifying-pretraining-variance-data-initialization-and-floating-point-arithmet]] (T3). That is ≤0.004 nats, or ≤0.14% relative — roughly 25× below the 3.5% restart spread the pessimistic reading borrowed from the scaling-law literature. A second measurement lands in the same range: Llama-124M on FineWeb, changing only the initialization or data-order seed, varies by 0.011–0.016 nats [[effective-learning-rate-governs-loss-dynamics-in-language-model-pretraining]] (T3).

Against a 0.004-nat floor, the memory axis's deltas are at or above it. Engram's allocation law buys 0.0139 nats (1.7248 → 1.7109) at 993M active and ~100B tokens [[conditional-memory-via-scalable-lookup]] (T3), 3.5× the tighter floor; OverEncoding's headline at 500B tokens is 0.082 nats on OLMoE-1.3B [[over-tokenized-transformer-vocabulary-is-generally-worth-scaling]] (T3), 20× above it. So a 9B-token A/B at 500M can resolve the upper half of the expected effect range, and a null would be an informative bound (~0.02 nats) rather than a foregone conclusion. The caveat is honest: the floor is measured at 124M–184M, not at 500M/9B, and seed variance typically shrinks with scale, so the extrapolation favours detectability but is unmeasured at the target scale. The right response is not 100B tokens per arm — at 20–27k tok/s that is 43–58 days per arm (T4) — but the 9B A/B plus a second seed if the delta lands within 2× of the floor.

## CSA2 at 32k

Sparse attention attends to a selected subset of the key-value cache instead of all of it. An indexer scores candidate positions, a top-k selects which ones each query will read, and the attention kernel then computes only over those. The selection can be token-granular or block-granular, and that choice decides whether the kernel is fast: fewer FLOPs do not automatically mean less latency, because a sparse kernel's inner loop is a memory gather, and gathers of scattered single vectors waste cache lines and memory-level parallelism. CSA2 is DeepSeek's compressed sparse attention, second generation: Full, Reindex and Reuse layer modes, a hierarchical sparse indexer with a 16,384-candidate cap, and a KV footprint of 890 bytes per token at inference.

### The memory objection is soft; the architecture objection is hard

The plan's CSA2 verdict — "worth one experiment, but it is the axis most likely to disappoint at 32k on this box" — is right in direction and wrong in mechanism, understating one blocker while overstating another.

The memory objection is soft, and this is measured rather than argued. The indexer's score tensor is a `[B, S, H_I, T]` FP32 intermediate materialized before the head-sum, which at S=32,768 with V4-Flash dimensions is 64 GiB, with a measured peak of 129.00 GB and 317 ms on a 140 GB H200 [[streamindex-memory-bounded-compressed-sparse]] (T3); the pure-PyTorch cuDNN reference has the same defect, materializing `(B, S_q, H_q, S_k)` FP32 at 68.7 GB for 32k/H_q=16 [[dsa_referencepy]] (T3/T4). But the fundamental requirement is only the head-summed `(B, S_q, S_k)` FP32 tensor: 4.29 GB at 32k (T4). StreamIndex demonstrates the fix on real hardware: 129.00 GB → 0.40 GB at S=32,768, and 10.3× faster (317 ms → 31 ms), with the runnable regime extended to 1,048,576 tokens at 6.21 GB peak [[streamindex-memory-bounded-compressed-sparse]] (T3). The indexer's score-tensor memory is a solved engineering problem; anyone arguing CSA2 is ruled out by memory is arguing against an implementation detail already fixed and measured.The architecture objection is hard, and harder than the plan assumes. Every training-capable DSA kernel in existence is Hopper-or-newer:

| Stack | Forward | Backward | Architectures |
|---|---|---|---|
| cuDNN DSA | SM90/SM100 | SM90/SM100 | Hopper, Blackwell |
| FlashMLA | sm_100a / sm_103a + Ascend 950 | — | Blackwell, Ascend (Hopper dropped) |
| TileLang `deepseek_v32` | H800/H200 tuned, wgmma | H800/H200 tuned | Hopper |
| StreamIndex | Triton, tested sm_90a | Triton, tested sm_90a | H100 / H200 |
| vLLM `TRITON_MLA_SPARSE` | Triton, tested 8×A800 | none | SM80/SM121, inference only |

The FlashMLA row is decisive. Its current release states its scope verbatim: "Supported scope: sm_100a / sm_103a and Ascend 950 (dav-3510), DeepSeek-V4.1 only… Drop support for NVIDIA Hopper GPUs" [[github-deepseek-aiflashmla-flashmla-efficient-multi-head-latent-attention-kernel]] (T2). The production sparse-attention forward for CSA2 does not run on Hopper, let alone Ampere. flash-attention's sparse-MLA forward and backward kernels are SM100-only files (`flash_fwd_mla_sm100.py`, `flash_bwd_mla_sm100.py`) [[cutebwdsm100-add-sparse-mla-deepseek-v4-backward-kernels-2621-dao-ailabflash-att]] (T2); the upstream tracker confirms "the sparse attention implementation appears to be forward-only" [[missing-sparse-backward-kernel-issue-107-deepseek-aiflashmla-github]] (T2); and the Ampere port of FlashMLA exposes only paged-KV decode, has no backward, and reports that "the performance of Ampere is currently poor due to register overflow" — 464 GB/s and 59 TFLOPS on A100 SXM against 2,039 GB/s and 312 TFLOPS peak [[github-pzhao-engflashmla-github]] (T2). SGLang has "no end-to-end CUDA path for running DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) on NVIDIA A100 / SM80" [[feature-add-an-opt-in-sm80-fallback-for-deepseek-sparse-attention-dsa-on-nvidia]] (T2). Four independent confirmations, none of which has changed.

The only sm_80 path is vLLM's `TRITON_MLA_SPARSE`, which "adds a `TRITON_MLA_SPARSE` backend so DSA sparse-MLA models… can run on GPUs where DeepGEMM and FlashMLA-Sparse are unavailable — SM80 (A100/A800) and SM121" [[attention-triton_mla_sparse-backend-for-sm80sm121-sparse-mla-rebase-takeover-of]] (T2). It passes its kernel tests on 8×A800-80GB PCIe, but it is a serving backend: no backward pass, no training loop, no optimizer step. So CSA2 at 32k on this box is not an experiment, it is a kernel-porting project — write an sm_80 indexer backward and an sm_80 sparse-MLA backward, tune both to usable MFU, then validate the distillation loss, before learning anything about whether the architecture helps.

### The payoff is half the headline, and its sign is not guaranteed

The plan cites NSA's 6.3× forward and 3.4× backward at 32k as evidence of a large sparse-attention win. Those are kernel-level ratios measured on 8×A100 with Triton kernels [[native-sparse-attention-hardware-aligned-and-natively]] (T3), and the dense baseline is itself a moving target — FlashAttention-2 already delivers roughly 2× over FlashAttention-1 on A100, so a sparse kernel's advantage depends on which dense kernel it is compared to [[flashattention-2]] (T3). The end-to-end training payoff is roughly half the headline.

FSA (ICLR 2026) is the measured end-to-end comparison the corpus needed: Llama3-8B, Qwen3-14B and Qwen2.5-32B at 32K and 64K in BF16, on H20 and H200 with NVLink. It reports "up to 1.25× and 1.09× on average end-to-end training speedup" over the Triton NSA kernel, and "up to 2.47× and an average of 1.86×" over full attention [[published-as-a-conference-paper-at-iclr-2026]] (T3). Dividing the two averages implies NSA ≈ 1.71× full attention end-to-end, not 6.3×. The fla-org Triton implementation reproduces a shrinking advantage: 2.66× forward at 16,384 tokens, 1.35× at 8,192, and 0.72× at 4,096 — NSA is slower than flash at short context [[github-fla-orgnative-sparse-attention-efficient-triton-implementations-for-nativ]] (T2).

Worse, the sign is not guaranteed. FSA reports directly: "vanilla NSA lags behind full attention in many tested cases… when the sequence length is 32K, one GQA group contains one query head, NSA consistently falls short of full attention" [[published-as-a-conference-paper-at-iclr-2026]] (T3). GQA is grouped-query attention, where several query heads share one key-value head; the group size is the number of query heads per KV head. If the 500M baseline has a group size of 1 at 32k, NSA is a net loss; at 4 or more it wins. That configuration value is the decisive unmeasured variable and it is not in the corpus. It is also free to measure — one line in the model config.

The corrected payoff is therefore −20% to −30% of step time at a 76–88% attention FLOP share, not the −39% to −54% implied by the 6.3×/3.4× kernel ratios (T4). That is still the largest single lever available, three to four times the capacitor mod's 6–7% of wall-clock.

### Gather granularity decides the sign, not the candidate cap

Both the plan and the pessimistic reading fixate on the 16,384-candidate cap, and neither is measuring the right quantity. The cap governs the indexer's search cost, not the attention's density: at 32k it halves the positions scored by deeper indexers, 16,384 of 32,768, whereas at 1M it cuts them 64-fold [[deepseek-v41-flash]] (T3/T4). Attention density is set by top-k against context length, and that is true whether or not the cap binds.

The determinant of the 32k payoff is gather granularity, and LongCat measures it directly. LSA versus dense MLA per attention layer at 32K: 1.08× forward, 0.79× backward, 0.83× total, with crossover only at 64K (1.39×) [[longcat-sparse-attention-taming-the-lightning-via]] (T2). Because 0.83× is a speedup ratio, LSA takes 1/0.83 = 1.20× dense MLA's latency — a net loss. The mechanism: each selected token is fetched as an independent gather of a single 1,152 B latent KV vector spanning three cachelines, and "the resulting net effective bandwidth thus plummets to a mere ~4.5% (~1/22) of peak" [[longcat-sparse-attention-taming-the-lightning-via]] (T2). That is why 64× fewer FLOPs produces a 20% slowdown. CSA2, token-granular and DSA-lineage, would be a +10.7% to +14.7% step-time penalty at 32k even if it ran (T4) — a 50–69 percentage-point swing against the NSA arm, decided by gather granularity rather than by the candidate cap.

### Run NSA or FSA instead

The substitution is not itself a porting project. FSA's official implementation is "well tested with NVIDIA Ampere or Hopper GPUs (e.g., A100 SXM, H20, H100 PCIe, H100 NVL, H100 SXM, H200 SXM)", supports "Training and inference (prefill)", and calls `loss.backward()` through a 65,536 + 32,768-token batch; it requires `flash-attn==2.6.3` [[github-relaxed-system-labflash-sparse-attention-efficient-implementations-of-nat]] (T2). The fla-org Triton NSA ships full training support via `flame` [[github-fla-orgnative-sparse-attention-efficient-triton-implementations-for-nativ]] (T2). MoBA is the fallback, also pinned to `flash-attn==2.6.3` [[github-moonshotaimoba-moba-mixture-of-block-attention-for-long-context-llms-gith]] (T2), but it is compute-neutral at 32k and lossy alone: trailing LM loss at 32K is 1.546 against 1.464 for full attention, recovered only by the 90/10 hybrid [[moba-mixture-of-block-attention-for]] (T3).

The regime point is the last piece. Every published 32k sparse-attention win is an adaptation-stage result. NSA pretrained on "270B tokens of 8k-length texts, followed by continued training and supervised fine-tuning on 32k-length texts with YaRN" [[native-sparse-attention-hardware-aligned-and-natively]] (T3). DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash trains sparse attention from scratch at 64K across 45T tokens [[deepseek-v41-flash]] (T3) — a capability receipt, not a speedup receipt, and at 64K rather than 32k. DeepSeek-V4 warms up with dense attention for the first 1T tokens and introduces sparse attention only at 64K [[deepseek-v4]] (T3). The plan's from-scratch 32k training sits outside the regime every published result was measured in, and 8k pretraining followed by 32k adaptation also cuts the quadratic penalty for most of the run, roughly halving wall-clock (T4). The long-context literature reaches the same conclusion: staged extension beats training at final length from initialization, with a linear schedule winning at a fixed token budget for 1B models up to 32K [[skyladder-better-and-faster-pretraining-via]], [[how-to-train-long-context-language-models-effectively]] (T3).

## Verdict

Feasibility here means three things at once: the experiment fits in memory, it fits in wall-clock, and its result can be measured. The plan's four arms clear the first test in three cases and the second in none, and the third is the one nobody budgeted for.

**(a) DDP is the only viable topology at 1M-token global batches.** True for the frozen-table design, false for the stated 7B/1B MoE axis. At 32k with ≤1B trainable, DDP's overhead is 6.6% and no alternative beats it by more than the width of its own unmeasured premise. At 7B trainable, DDP needs 112 GB/card and does not fit; ZeRO-2 fits at 33.6 GB with DDP-identical communication, and PP=5 fits at 25.8 GB/card with a 4–14% bubble. The claim is about a design, not about the box.

**(b) Expert and tensor parallelism are ruled out by the fabric.** True, but the mechanism is availability and placement, not cost. EP is ≈1.4× at 32k, not 70×, and is blocked because no training-grade EP library runs without NVLink, GPUDirect P2P or RDMA. TP is ≈2.8× and is blocked because its collectives sit inside the layer where nothing can hide them. The conclusion survives; its arithmetic does not.

**(c) Frozen Engram tables make a 7B-total / ~1B-trainable model fit DDP on 64 GB cards.** True, at ~43.1 GB/card with fused cross-entropy and ~53.1 GB without, but the plan's own table arithmetic breaks its own claim: 12 GB is one layer, not the total, and the recipient carries two. Freezing is also not a memory necessity — ZeRO-2, Adafactor and 8-bit Adam all fit a fully-trainable 7B on 64 GB. Freezing is correct here because the trained variant loses to MoE at 0.92B, and because training the table costs 9–68% of step time depending on the link. The claim is right; the justification is inverted.

**(d) CSA2 compressed sparse attention pays off at 32k training context.** False as stated. CSA2 is not runnable on sm_80 — every training-capable kernel in the stack is Hopper-or-newer, and the only sm_80 path is an inference-only Triton backend with no backward. The underlying question has a positive answer through fine-block sparse attention, at a measured ~1.71× end-to-end rather than 6.3×, and its sign is decidable before any kernel work by checking the GQA group size.

Four measurements would settle the premises, and none is expensive:

1. **`lspci -vv` per card**, reading `LnkCap` against `LnkSta` for speed, width and the downgraded flag. If the box is Gen2 x16 rather than x4, every communication number improves ~4× and the capacitor mod becomes irrelevant; if it is Gen1 x4, they get ~1.6× worse.
2. **A five-rank `all_reduce_perf`** at 1 MB / 100 MB / 1 GB with both columns printed, which settles the algbw-versus-busbw question in an afternoon.
3. **A sustained BF16 GEMM burn at 250 W** with 1 Hz clock telemetry, which distinguishes whether 74.6–78.5 at 180 W is power-capped or kernel-limited and pins the sustained denominator between 75 and 100 TFLOPS/card.
4. **A GQA group-size check** in the planned config, which decides whether the sparse-attention arm is a win or a loss before any kernel work begins.

A fifth is not about performance at all. ECC is off on these cards [[club-170hxdocscmp170hx-understandingmd-at-main-pixelmlclub-170hx-github]] (T2), and independent reports put field failure around 50% among buyers, with faults appearing only above the 8 GiB region a mining rig would touch — which makes a full-VRAM `memtest_vulkan` pass mandatory rather than optional [[lessonsmd]] (T2). Silent corruption would invalidate the run-comparability premise the entire program rests on, and the club's own health gate shows what a fleet-wide OOM cascade does: Xid 31 on two cards, Xid 154 on all four, a required reboot [[readmemd-2]] (T2).

The data pipeline is the part of the plan that is already right. Nine billion tokens at a 75/15/10 mix draws 6.75B from FineWeb-Edu's 1.3T tokens, 1.35B from Stack-Edu's ~125B and 0.9B from FineMath-4+'s 9.6B, so no stream is exhausted and repetition is avoidable [[huggingfacefwfineweb-edu-datasets-at-hugging-face]], [[huggingfacetbstack-edu-datasets-at-hugging-face]], [[huggingfacetbfinemath-datasets-at-hugging-face-2]] (T2). Packing to 32k sequences with `cu_seqlens` boundaries is standard and not cosmetic: at 1.3B parameters and 150B tokens, retrieval-based packing improved in-context learning by 11.6% and knowledge memorisation by 9.8% relative to baseline packing without sacrificing pre-training efficiency [[analysing-the-impact-of-sequence-composition]] (T3). The determinism manifest is the part that must not be skipped: floating-point arithmetic order — induced by sharding topology, gradient accumulation depth or precision — produces functional divergence within 10–17% of changing the data seed, and average validation loss cannot distinguish the two [[quantifying-pretraining-variance-data-initialization-and-floating-point-arithmet]] (T3).

What would reopen the verdict is worth naming, because two of the three doors are closing rather than opening. A training-grade SM80 sparse-MLA backward would make CSA2 an experiment instead of a porting project, but the kernel ecosystem is moving the other way: FlashMLA dropped Hopper, flash-attention's sparse-MLA backward is SM100-only, and TileLang's DSA kernels are built on Hopper-only `wgmma`. The Ampere-runnable substitutes are pinned to `flash-attn==2.6.3`, a 2024-era release, so the box's architecture-axis experiments have a shelf life set by the last flash-attn version that supports sm_80. A training-side host-staged EP collective would reopen the MoE arm, and that door is ajar — the inference prototypes already reach 85–90% of an NVLink A800, so the missing piece is the backward pass rather than the fabric. A Gen3 unlock would lift the link ceiling, but it is fuse-blocked. The practical reading is that this box's useful life as an architecture-axis testbed is bounded by software support for sm_80, not by its silicon.

The one-sentence inversion, for a reader who will act on it: the fabric decides what this box can attempt, not what it costs to attempt it — so design for availability, measure for cost, and stop treating 0.75 GiB/s as the number that governs the program.

## Source Tensions

Six disputes in the source base bear directly on the argument above. Each is stated with both sides, and each resolves to a position that changes a number rather than a conclusion.

**Width is not speed.** One side holds that PCIe width and generation are independent axes that must never be conflated: the capacitor mod changes width only, the unlocker changes generation only, and a card with neither runs Gen1 x4 [[physical-modsmd]], [[pcie-subsystemmd]] (T2). The other side states "PCIe Gen2 x4" as a single fact and derives every bandwidth figure from it. Both are defensible and they are not in conflict: the premise is under-specified rather than wrong. The four states span roughly 8× in one-way bandwidth, and the plan's 0.75 GiB/s is plausible as a busbw reading and implausible as an algbw reading — which means the plan is probably right about the fabric and wrong about the column. `lspci -vv` resolves it in one command.

**The community applies a stricter evidence standard to its own numbers than the plan applies to its.** One side holds that a hardware number is not decision-grade until its full sanitized receipt chain is merged, and that unmerged rows are "pending evidence repair" [[club-170hxdocsbenchmarksmd-at-main-pixelmlclub-170hx-github]] (T2). The other treats 0.75 GiB/s, 162 TFLOPS and ~70× EP cost as measured facts carrying the quantitative spine of the program. By the community's own published rule, all three are pending evidence repair. This is not rhetorical: it is the corpus's own standard applied consistently, and it is why every figure here carries a tier. But the standard cuts both ways — demoting those numbers to user-reported does not make them wrong, it makes them unfalsifiable, and in the one case where a direction can be inferred, the all-reduce rate, the error runs against the plan's pessimism rather than its optimism.

**Effect sizes are reported in units that have no established noise floor.** One side holds that the memory-axis deltas are large: Memory Grafting moves the 0.92B average from 45.62 and 45.03 to 46.98, and the 2.8B average from 51.95/52.43 to 53.86 [[memory-grafting-scaling-language-model]] (T3). The other holds that published architecture effects are 0.014–0.082 nats against a 3.5%-relative single-run floor, so a 9B-token null is the expected result [[moba-mixture-of-block-attention-for]], [[a-hitchhikers-guide-to-scaling-law-estimation]] (T3). The two sides are not commensurable: score points on a ten-task average are not nats of training loss, and neither maps cleanly onto a relative-percentage floor. The sharper finding is that no published Engram or CSA2 delta is reported in a unit in which a noise floor has been established. The floor itself is now measured at ≤0.004 nats, 25× below the 3.5% figure, so the published deltas are at or above the real floor and a 9B A/B can resolve the upper half. The falsifier is one experiment: report the delta in nats, two seeds.

**Every sparse-attention 32k win is an adaptation-stage result.** One side holds that sparse attention delivers large measured 32k wins: NSA reports 6.3× forward and 3.4× backward at 32k on A100 with a quality win, and LongCat's LSA claims parity with full attention and native training to 1M tokens [[native-sparse-attention-hardware-aligned-and-natively]], [[longcat-sparse-attention-taming-the-lightning-via]] (T3/T2). The other side plans from-scratch 32k training as the primary axis. The plan sits outside the regime every published result was measured in: NSA spent 270B tokens at 8k before touching 32k, and LongCat's 0.83×-at-32K figure is a latency measurement, not a from-scratch efficiency claim. So the 6.3×/3.4× does not transfer as a from-scratch number, and the recommendation is 8k pretraining followed by 32k adaptation — which also cuts the quadratic penalty for most of the run.

**P2P has two incompatible data-movement figures and the larger one is unverified.** One side holds that BAR1 P2P works and moves data at 1.68 GB/s each way at Gen2 x4 on 12/12 pairs [[github-bayleycmpunlocker-a-cmpunlocker-fork-with-working-p2p-on-sys-4028gr-trt2]] (T2). The other holds that P2P is absent by default and that the one patch that could enable it half-works — data movement at 6.25 GB/s with synchronisation broken, unverified, on one rig [[p2pmd]] (T2). All three figures are consistent once the link state is separated: 1.68 GB/s is the one-way Gen2 x4 rate, so P2P at x4 removes a traversal but cannot exceed the link; 6.25 GB/s is at x16 and unverified. The decisive fact is the one nobody cites: on a four-card PLX rig at Gen2 x16, host-staged copy already moves large buffers at 6.21–6.30 GB/s, so P2P is 8% slower above 16 MiB and costs 25–44% on TP4 decode [[2026-10-01-cmp170hx-4card-bar1-p2p-plx-cudaipynb]] (T2). P2P is a latency lever at x4 and a bandwidth lever only at N=2 on wide links.

**The most trustworthy sustained numbers are at 180 W, not the plan's 250 W.** One side holds that the decision-grade benchmarks run against an enforced 180.00 W limit, and that 74.6–78.5 TFLOP/s is a ten-minute `gpu-burn -tc` at that cap with no thermal throttle [[readmemd-2]], [[2026-09-02-cmp170hx-health-gateipynb]] (T2). The other holds that the card's stock cap is 250 W and it delivers ~162 TFLOPS bf16, the basis of the ~810 aggregate [[performancemd]], [[lessonsmd]] (T2/T1). The resolution is that 74.6–78.5 is a floor, not a ceiling: it is measured at 180 W enforced, so at 250 W the sustained number should be higher, but nobody has measured it, and the 162 and 164–192 figures are GEMM peaks or FP32 burn-in, not sustained BF16 training at 250 W. The honest denominator is 75–100 TFLOPS/card with the upper end unmeasured, so every wall-clock estimate here carries a 1.3× error bar from that axis alone.
